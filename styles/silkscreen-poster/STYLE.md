# Silkscreen Travel Poster

> Opaque, hard-edged flat inks pulled one colour at a time through a screen: 4–6 inks per poster, stepped colour bands instead of gradients, thin paper-white gaps where the registration slips. Information is revealed the way the print is made: one layer, one pulse.
> References (grammar only):
> - **1930s–40s WPA national-park screen prints**: 4–6 flat inks, lit and shade faces, stepped-band skies, a dark information band, concentric sun rings.
> - **Fifty-Nine Parks (contemporary)**: restraint, paper as a colour, a series on one template with a new palette per poster.
> - **The physical process**: hinged screen, squeegee pushing an ink bead across mesh, split-fountain pulls, drag streaks, pinholes, off-register slivers.
>
> Never copy a real poster's composition, park name, lettering or logo, and never name a real agency.

A **scene style**: it does a practical job (a guide, an event poster, a destination series) for any place, event or product with a few facts. See §12 for the jobs.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

A poster being **printed on camera**. Every colour is an opaque ink laid by its own pull; later ink covers earlier ink completely. No halftone, no fluorescent ink, no line work: a shape's edge is where one flat ink stops. **Gradients are forbidden**: skies and haze are **stepped bands** (a flat colour, thinning stripes, the next colour).

What makes it read as silkscreen and not as flat vector art:
- **Registration**: every layer offset by about 0.1–0.3% of the frame; a knocked-out shape leaves a crescent of bare paper.
- **Overprint edge**: a faint multiply copy a hair off under each ink, leaving a darker sliver where ink laps over ink.
- **Ink thickness**: a light rim top-left, a dark rim bottom-right, fills only; fresh ink has a wet sheen that fades within a second.
- **Paper**: cream stock with fibres; mottle and pinholes in paper colour, visible only on ink.

Not risograph (translucent fluorescent overprint with halftone grain), not ukiyo-e (key-block line work with bokashi), not woodcut (carved line), not low-poly (3D facets). This style is opaque, line-free, flat and banded.

## 2. Materials & rendering

- **One path per ink layer**, filled flat; knock-outs are holes in the path. Any shape can be printed.
- **Pull reveal**: a ragged squeegee front as a clip (a drag tail of about 3% of the sheet's width); blade, bead and a sliver of handle ride on it.
- **Ink bead**: a round tube with lit half, dark underside, rolling glints and viscous strands; a split fountain puts several colours in one bead.
- **Screen**: a hinged frame with mesh, emulsion and tape; its shadow is a ring around the frame, never a full rectangle.
- **Shapes**: ridges with a jagged lit facet, pines as one outline of drooping tiers, water as a flat body with glint bars. Hard edges everywhere.
- **Poster templates**: a portrait sheet (2:3: paper border, art area, a dark **info band** at the foot holding a name, a number, a rule and a row of stats; columns sized by content), or a **full-bleed print** (the whole frame, art to every edge, the name printed into the sky, a dark info panel such as a ticket stub printed last). In a full-bleed film the camera stays inside the print. The portrait sheet fills a 9:16 frame and lies on the workbench in 16:9.
- **Texture budget**: fine paper fibre, mottle at about 55%, pinholes at about 75%, light grain. More reads as risograph.

## 3. Colour logic

- **One palette per poster, at most 6 inks**, assigned by role: light sky, deep sky / bands, far, mid, near (darkest; also the info band), and **one bright accent** (sun, route, highlights, key number). An optional lit-face ink.
- **Values step from far (light) to near (dark)**; depth is value, never blur.
- **The accent is rare**: the sun or light source, the one route or product, the most important number.
- **Paper is a colour**: an unprinted area is the brightest thing on the sheet (water, snow, a beam of light).
- **A series = one template, a new palette per item** (time of day, season, colourway, neighbourhood). Re-inking the same geometry in another palette is a statement.
- **Text inks are chosen by contrast** against the ink underneath, never by a fixed key; a night palette must not swallow a header.
- Examples (sky1 → near, accent): dawn `#F6CD98 #EE9A73 #9A7FA8 #3E7079 #1E3B3F #FBE6B4`; harbour noon `#E8F0F2 #7FB3C8 #D9C7A3 #2F6E8E #173247 #E8553D`; winter `#EEF1F4 #B8C9D9 #8B9BB0 #4A5D78 #1F2A3C #F2B134`.

## 4. Type & on-screen text

- **Condensed display** for names and values (the kind of Big Shoulders Display or League Gothic), fitted to its column; a geometric sans for labels (the kind of Outfit 600), letter-spaced in Latin script. A brand's own display face comes first when it is heavy enough to print as a flat ink.
- Headers are **printed into the sky**; the info band is printed last.
- **The poster text is the on-screen text.** No narration is needed; if there is a voice, its lines are a printed strip (a band pull), never a floating caption.
- **Reading time counts from a text's last item**: a row of stats starts its hold when the final stat lands, rounded up to the pulse.
- On a wall shot, labels and values must still read at phone size; otherwise the wall is texture and the reading happens in a close-up. Close-ups on a band keep the whole band in frame with side margins of at least 5%.

## 5. Motion quality

- **Pulls are linear and steady** (a squeegee does not ease). Accent inks start half a pulse after the main ink of their layer.
- **Info items** print in reading order on subdivisions of the pulse. **There is no fade anywhere.**
- **One pulse times the print**: pick a unit in frames; every pull, lift and snap starts or lands on it. Speed up by subdividing the pulse, not by changing it.
- **Screen lift**: the frame hinges up (vertical squash + growing shadow) in about 0.4 s.
- **Palette re-ink**: a wide squeegee travels diagonally over the sheet in about 0.35 s; geometry identical on both sides of the blade, only the inks change. Show only the blade, bead and a sliver of handle.
- **Loose layers**: in a multi-plane move, planes parallax by depth with paper gaps and thickness shadows, then **snap into register** on one hit.

## 6. Camera grammar

A vocabulary, not a route. Opening and ending come from the topic.

| Move | Expresses | Can serve |
|---|---|---|
| Truck with the squeegee, slight roll, frame edge in view | the act of printing; colour arriving | a hook; a split-fountain sky |
| Locked top-down full sheet | layers stacking into a picture | watching a poster build; a comparison |
| Fast pull-back as the screen lifts | the reveal of a finished layer | a scene complete; a title |
| Push + tilt to the info band | reading | facts; a date; a price |
| Truck through a foreground silhouette (tree, lamp post, sail) | passing into the next poster | poster-to-poster transitions |
| Crane through loose planes (multi-plane parallax) | depth, a journey inside the print | a route; a climb; a street |
| Slow lateral dolly along a row of prints | a set, a range | a series wall; colourways |
| Push into the mesh until the weave shows | the craft, the grain of the medium | an intimate moment; a detail |
| Log-zoom pull-back to the wall or window | the poster in the world | a final lockup (hold 3 s or more) |

Close-ups on a portrait sheet keep its edge in frame (a full-bleed print has no edge to show); a squeegee across a close-up is tilted with the handle hidden, so it doesn't split the frame. Layers change by squeegee, posters through foreground silhouettes, a diagonal sweep that prints the next poster behind the blade, or a table move. No dissolves, no bare cuts.

## 7. Sound palette

- **Foley by material**: squeegee on mesh (band-passed noise with a low mesh grain; a faster pull is brighter), wet ink squelch, screen clack and hinge creak, paper stamps, ticks for dashed routes, paper hiss, a wood-and-metal clamp, drying-rack clips.
- **One pull, one sound**: each ink is a squeegee stroke that ends in a soft thud as the blade lifts. Info items get a light stamp each; the registration snap is the loudest hit in the film.
- **What stays silent**: the camera, drying ink, a held poster. Sound belongs to the hand and the tools.
- **Ambience bed**: workshop room tone under the table shots, quiet and never tonal.
- **Ambience from inside the print**: a finished scene may "wake up" (surf, a street, a crowd), beginning before its last pull is done and running on a moment after the cut away.
- **Silence**: everything drops out for one pulse before the key facts; what remains is one ambience, and the first sound after is a decisive gesture (the clamp).
- **Voice**: optional. A friendly local guide, plain and brief, reading only what the poster says.

## 8. Native moves

Each serves information order. A menu: use the ones your film needs.

- **One ink, one pulse.** Facts print last, when they are read. *Fits content like:* museum hours; a ferry timetable; a café's menu.
- **Split fountain.** Several inks in one pull fill the frame with colour. *Fits content like:* a fairground's night sky; a sunset over a harbour; a rainbow of colourways.
- **Palette re-ink.** Same poster, another palette = time passing, a season, a variant. *Fits content like:* summer vs winter hours; three jacket colourways; day and night at a market.
- **Registration slip → snap.** Loose layers clamp into register with one hit, before the key facts. *Fits content like:* the date of a race; a launch price; a ticket sale opening.
- **Paper as a colour.** The unprinted sheet is the brightest element. *Fits content like:* a ski slope; a lighthouse beam; fresh snow on a roof.
- **The route as a printed layer.** A dashed accent line stamped segment by segment. *Fits content like:* a bus line; a marathon course; a delivery path.
- **The series wall.** Every print ends up hung together as the final lockup. *Fits content like:* a city's four districts; a touring exhibition's dates; a product family.

## 9. Pitfalls of the medium

- Stroked edges draw internal lines where shapes join → fills only (the rim is a shifted fill inside a clip).
- A full-rectangle screen shadow muddies the print → ring shadow.
- Cool shadow facets next to water read as water → shadows in the near ink.
- Jagged ink beads read as paper edges → a round bead.
- Parallax alone reads as "the mountains are moving" → paper gaps and thickness while loose, snapped to zero with a sound.
- Silhouette wipes hold a dark blank → centre on the switch so full cover lasts about one frame.
- Very wide banners are slivers in 16:9 and worse in 9:16 → about 4:1 at most, or stack the banner in two lines.
- Random coloured dots on the table read as confetti → a real workbench (planks, grain, muted stains, tape).
- Too many items at honest reading times overrun the length → print items side by side in the same pulls, or let secondary ones be read on the final wall.
- A soft edge anywhere (a blur, a gradient, a shadow on ink) breaks the print → hard edges and stepped bands only.

## 10. Building it here

- **Each ink is one SVG `<path>`** with a flat fill, stacked in print order with normal blending (the ink is opaque); knock-outs are subpaths with `fill-rule="evenodd"`. Stepped bands are a helper that returns rectangles, never `linear-gradient`.
- **The pull** is a `clip-path: polygon(…)` on the layer whose ragged front (points from `noise(i, seed)`) moves with `prog(frame, start, dur, (t) => t)`: linear, no easing. Blade and bead are small SVG pieces riding the same front; the wet sheen is a pale copy of the layer that drops to nothing within a second.
- **Registration and thickness**: a fixed `translate` per layer; the overprint copy is the same path a hair off with `mix-blend-mode: multiply`; the rims are two shifted copies clipped to the path. Loose layers add a larger offset by depth and a hard-edged offset copy as shadow, all set to zero on the snap frame.
- **Paper**: `feTurbulence` with a fixed `seed` for fibre; mottle and pinholes are a paper-coloured noise shown only where there is ink (mask it with the union of the layers). Nothing boils.
- **Camera**: one wrapper `transform` for trucks, rolls and pushes; the multi-plane crane is 2D parallax by depth. There is no 3D library; the workbench tilt is a small `rotateX` under `perspective`.
- **Overrides**: no springs (`springAt` and `SPRING` are out), no fades, no `MaskRise` (text is printed by the same pull clip), `<SceneTrack sweeps={false} />`, and `Finish` off or barely on (the texture budget is already spent). Scenes take no `SceneFrame` entry or exit: its blurred push is the engine's default look, not this one. The timeline's `overlap` is how long the old scene stays mounted under the new one (8 frames by default): build it with `{ overlap: 0 }` for a hard cut, or give it the length of this style's own transition.
- **The pulse lives in `timeline.ts`**; pulls, lifts and the snap derive from it, and `Soundtrack.tsx` puts each stroke and stamp on the same frames. Use `p(vertical, horizontal)` to pick the template per format.

## 11. In a product video

- **The brand's main colour is the accent ink**: the product, the route to it, the key number. A brand with a palette maps its colours onto the roles (sky, far, mid, near), six inks at most; colourways or plans become a re-ink.
- **The product is printed, then shown**: a flat silhouette of the device or pack is pulled in the accent, its screen knocked out to bare paper; the real screenshot then sits in that window as a flat `Phone`, clean and unshadowed (`Phone` casts a soft shadow and glow by default: pass `style={{ boxShadow: "none" }}`), like a photo tipped onto the sheet. Push into it rather than tilting it.
- **Real facts fill the info band**: price, hours, counts, dates, each listed in the video's `facts.md`. Illustrative data carries `<ExampleBadge />`.
- **End card**: the last pull prints the logo and one line on how to get it into the info band, then the log-zoom pull-back to the wall, held 3 s or more.
- **Arabic**: pulls and info items run right to left; words print whole, never letter by letter, and are never letter-spaced (a wide header can take a kashida instead, with `addKashida`). A bold poster Kufi for names and values, a geometric Arabic sans for labels.
- **9:16**: use the portrait sheet; lift the info band above the bottom 22% (the near ink continues below it), keep the header in the sky below the top 12%, and nothing to read in the right 12%.

## 12. Variation space

You decide the subject, the scenes and their planes, the palettes within the colour logic, the number of posters, the camera path, the opening, the ending, the set of sounds and the length. Use cases are **grammar for the order and duration of information**; build your own timeline from the user's facts.

| Use case | Information order (print order) | Hold per layer | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Trail / attraction guide** | place → per item: scene → name → difficulty → distance → time → footer | title about 3 s; stat row 2.2–3 s after its last item | 30–40 s, 3 items |
| **Event / fair poster** | scene pulls → headline → date → venue → call to action | headline 2.5 s, date + venue 3 s, final 3 s | 15–20 s |
| **Destination series** | one template, a palette per place: name → one fact → series wall | 2.5 s per poster + 4 s wall | 20–30 s |
| **Product drop** | layers around a flat product silhouette → name → colourway ×3 (re-ink) → price / date | 1.8 s per colourway | 12–18 s |
| **Seasonal / hours notice** | one poster re-inked per season → season + hours | 2 s per swap | 10–15 s |

- Structures: **one poster, many runs** (re-inked for each of four market days, the programme changing in the band); **a street walk** (one multi-plane truck down a street, each shop sign printed as we pass); **a misprint story** (registration drifts until the last pull lands true on the date).
- Openings: **the empty screen** (light through a blank mesh, a stencil being exposed); **the accent first** (a lone product in the bright ink on bare paper); **the finished wall at night**, then back to the table.
- Endings: **the drying rack** (prints clipped in a row, swaying); **the poster in use** (pasted on a bus shelter, rain starting); **the last pull is paper** (the squeegee runs dry, leaving a white silhouette).

A new place is a new scene drawn for it; reusing another video's places or timeline is not a way to make a film.
