# Caption News

> Social-video news that reads with the sound off. Real footage, photos and the source document are the ground; huge captions are the lead actor, slammed in a chunk at a time with one keyword on a yellow block. Hard cuts on a pulse grid, numbers that roll, a source tag that slides in whenever a figure appears, and the original document highlighted on screen before its number is believed. Built for a feed.
> References (grammar only): the captions-first social news of the mid-2010s (NowThis, AJ+), horizontal explainers that set big type over footage (Bloomberg Quicktake). Never copy their logos, fonts, colour ratios, idents or shots, and never name them in the film.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

1. **Sound-off first.** Every moment must be understood from the picture and the captions alone; the voice adds pace, not information.
2. **The caption is the shot.** One short line, set huge, slammed in two or three chunks, one keyword in a yellow block; one small line under it. The footage is chosen to sit under the line.
3. **Evidence on screen.** Each number arrives with its source: a source tag slides in; key figures are first seen highlighted in the original document; per-shot footage credits sit in the corner like a news "Video:" tag.
4. **A hook in the first frames.** The film's largest number lands within the first 5 frames and rolls to its value.
5. **One turn.** The bad-news hook is answered by a fact that changes its meaning; the rest of the film explains that turn, one new fact per shot.

Not **Data Storytelling** (charts are short inserts between footage, never the whole film, never a dashboard). Not a **TV news package** (no anchor, no lower-third ticker, no studio, no scrolling crawl). Not a keynote: no headers, no centred title-and-bullets, no page turns.

## 2. Frame and layout

Positions for 16:9, as shares of the frame; the mirror of them for a right-to-left language.

| Zone | Position | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Footage | full bleed | video, photo, document or data card; never a vertical clip pillar-boxed in the middle |
| Caption card | flush to the reading side at a 6% margin; big line's baseline at about 88% of the height, small line's at 94% | one line each, never wrapped |
| Channel bug | top corner on the reading side, 5% in, about 4% of the height | accent block with the channel's name + the topic in small caps |
| Source tag | under the bug, a little smaller | a white "Source" + dark label (`Annual report · 2026.08.03`, `… · page 13`); only on data and document shots, slides in over 6 frames |
| Footage credit | opposite top corner, small, on a 50% dark chip | `Video: author · CC BY 3.0`; every CC BY shot carries it |
| Lower third | reading-side margin, at about 69% of the height, 9% high | accent bar + label + one line; for a place, date or speaker, never for the main sentence |

Under the caption: a bottom gradient (clear at mid-height → 84% dark at the bottom) plus a soft dark radial from the bottom corner on the reading side; a top gradient (55% → clear over the top fifth) under the bug and the credit. Data cards use a lighter gradient that starts lower. Graphics and charts sit on the far side of centre and bleed to the frame edge; their title and source sit in the top reading-side corner, never centred.

**9:16 is a different layout, not a crop**: footage fills the frame (a horizontal source sits in a band across the upper two thirds, scaled at most 1.5×); the caption stacks its chunks on two or three rows in the lower-middle, above the bottom 22%; the bug and source tag sit just below the top 12%; nothing in the right 12%.

## 3. Editing grammar

- **Cuts on a pulse grid**: one pulse is 15 frames (half a second), and every cut lands on a multiple of it. Footage shots 1.5–3 s; data cards and documents 3–4.5 s; a shot that holds a comparison stays until every item has been fully on screen at least 2.5 s.
- **Captions cross cuts.** A caption can run over two shots (picture changes, words don't); captions change at most every 2.5 s or so even when shots change every 1.5 s. Reading time, not shot length, sets the caption clock.
- **The order of a fact**: footage (feeling) → the number on a card → the document line that says it → the consequence. Never a number without its source tag in the same shot.
- **Transitions are few and fixed**: hard cut (default); a 1–2-frame white flash on the turn and on the freeze; a card sliding in from the far side with speed streaks (5–6 frames); a split screen opening from its divider; grid panels rising staggered (3 frames apart); a punch-in (9 frames, ease-out, to about 1.25×) on the culprit; a **mask transition** where a photo grows out of a map shape. No dissolves, no page turns, no 3D flips.
- **Pace**: a strong action within half a second of every shot; no frame flat for more than 1 s (rolling counters, line drawing, highlighter sweeps, slow pushes keep it alive); one held breath (2–3 s) after the turn; two real silences.

## 4. Caption card (the narrated line *is* the graphic)

- **Big line**: a heavy sans (weight 800–900), about 8% of the frame height (verdict lines 10%), ivory `#F7F5EF`, with a soft dark shadow. Split into 2–3 chunks; each chunk slams 4 frames after the previous: scale 1.3 → 1 in 4 frames with a slight overshoot, fully opaque after the first frame.
- **Keyword**: one word or number inside a chunk; a honey-yellow block `#FFC21A` wipes in behind it in the reading direction over 4 frames, and the text turns ink `#0E0E0C`. One keyword per caption, at most.
- **Small line**: a medium-weight sans at about 3% of the frame height, ivory; it fades and rises a few pixels over 5 frames, 3 frames after the first chunk. It is the spoken sentence in full (numbers as digits), timed to the voice; the big line is its short paraphrase, five or six words at most.
- **One row each in 16:9, always.** If a line would wrap, split the sentence into two captions.
- **Faces**: a brand's own heavy sans comes first; without one, a tight grotesque of the kind Inter Tight is.
- A text-only moment (a caption with no voice) is native to the style: use it for the breath after the turn.

## 5. Graphics

- **Ledger card** (the data-card ground): a `#151512` sheet, faint ruled lines at even intervals (4.5% white), a thin red double margin on the reading side: the "account book" that ties every chart together. It slides in from the far side over 5–6 frames; inside, a slow push (×1.025–1.03 over the shot).
- **Colour meaning**: honey `#FFC21A` = the point / gains / the highlight; red `#FF4A3D` = losses, the spike, the bad number; ivory = everything else; grey `#8D8B83` = labels and units; `#5E5C55` = the bars that don't matter.
- **Numbers**: a heavy sans with fixed-width digits, so a rolling counter never jitters. Counters roll with ease-out and turn their colour when they arrive. Units are set smaller after the number.
- **Charts**: titled in the top reading-side corner (three words at most, so it can be read in the shot) with one grey line under it; lines draw segment by segment; the key point gets a bigger dot, an expanding ring (about 0.7 s) and a block label; bars grow from the reading side one after another (the first one slower and longer if it is the point).
- **Map**: region shapes from public-domain geometry, one projection, a sequential fill in five steps of one hue, regions without data in dark grey with a legend swatch; the subject region outlined in yellow with a leader and a label; the map pushes in on it. Check disputed borders before showing them.
- **Documents**: the real page as white paper on a dark desk (1–2° tilt, soft shadow, vignette), pushed in slowly; highlighter rectangles sit exactly on the printed line, multiplied onto the paper and swept in the reading direction in 0.3–0.5 s; the number read from it is re-set as a big yellow block beside the page. The printed text is picture; what must be read is re-set in type.
- **Split screen**: two halves with a thin yellow divider that drops first; the "bad" half desaturated, darkened and tinted red, the "good" half in full grade; a label block and a counter in each; a shared stamp centred on the divider.
- **Grid**: three panels with narrow gaps rising staggered (side by side in 16:9, stacked in 9:16); each one lights up (yellow border, label turns yellow) on the word that names it; numbered 1–2–3.
- **Freeze frame**: the shot stops on a frame, a 4-frame white flash, punch 1.0 → 1.12, saturation drops to 45%, yellow corner brackets close in on the subject, a timecode chip with a pause mark appears in the top far corner.
- **Title sting**: a yellow ruled page (the ledger again, in yellow) wipes across 45% of the frame from the reading side over live footage; the title slams, a second line and a dateline chip follow; the page wipes off and the title becomes the channel bug.

## 6. Grade and image quality

- One grade for every piece of footage: `contrast(1.07) saturate(0.92) brightness(0.98)` plus a 6% warm soft-light layer; documents 4%. Light grain over everything.
- **Scale limits**: modern footage at most 1.5×; photos at most 1:1 of their source; archive material at most 2.5×. Pushes stay inside those limits: a small source already at 1.5× gets no push.
- Leave out any shot that carries a station logo, a watermark or burned-in text: never crop, blur or cover someone else's mark. Blur legible private details (phone numbers, names, number plates).
- Captions, numbers and charts are always live type and vector, never scaled footage.

## 7. Sound palette

- **Every graphic action has a sound**: a chunk slam (a click with a thump under it), a counter rattle (a tick every frame or two while it rolls), a whoosh into each card, a highlighter squeak, a camera shutter on the freeze, pops for labels, ticks as map regions fill.
- **Weights**: pops and ticks are light, the chunk slam medium, and a stamp thump on the biggest numbers the heavy one. The hook's hit lands with the number in the first frames.
- **Natural sound**: the subject's own location sound, low under the footage and never ducked out of hearing; it is the only bed, and it returns under the ending. A clip's own sound is kept only when the clip is the user's own and nothing in it is a tune, a jingle or singing; otherwise mute the clip and build that place's tone by code (a quiet room or street tone).
- **Two silences**: a hard stop before the turn, leaving only the natural sound and the question on screen; a second, shorter one before the verdict.
- **Sound across a cut**: the natural sound of a shot starts about 10 frames before the cut to it, most of all before the freeze.
- **The one riser** leads into the hero graphic (the map push or the verdict), peaking on its cut.
- **Tonal accents**: a single bright "ping" on the turn and a fuller bell-like hit on the verdict, each a single sound. Never a tune.
- **Voice**: one brisk narrator, numbers written out in the script so they are spoken cleanly.

## 8. Native moves

1. **First-frames hook**: a kicker line, then a giant number in a yellow block rolling to its value in about 14 frames, then the unit and the subject word.
2. **Chunk slam with one yellow keyword.**
3. **Proportion by darkness**: a yellow line sweeps across the footage to the percentage; what it passed goes grey and dark; the figure appears on the dark side. Works for any "X% of …".
4. **The document says it**: a headline or sentence highlighted on the real page, then the number popped as a yellow block beside the paper.
5. **Freeze and question**: the image stops on the viewer's question, and the sound cuts to silence.
6. **Ledger split**: two counters racing side by side, the difference stamped after both settle.
7. **Lit grid**: three ways or three causes, each panel lighting on its word.
8. **Punch and circle**: a 9-frame punch-in on the culprit, a circle drawn round it, a leader to its name.
9. **Map to ground**: the map pushes into a region, then a photo of that place grows out of the region's outline.

## 9. Material

1. **The user's material comes first**: their footage, photos, documents and data (with the rights to them), and screenshots and screen recordings of the product. The sources below are the fallback.
2. **Where to look** (judge every file on its own page, not the search result):
   - **Wikimedia Commons** (video and photos): read the licence on each file page. Fine: CC0, public domain, CC BY (credit author, title and licence on screen). Not used: CC BY-SA, "no known restrictions", unclear licences, re-uploads, colourised or "restored" versions.
   - **Public agencies and archives** that release their own work to the public domain: check that the page does not credit a contractor or a third party.
   - **Official documents and data** (reports, statistics) from the publisher's own site: the source of every fact.
   - **Map geometry**: Natural Earth (public domain).
3. **Facts**: every claim, figure and derived sum goes in the video's `facts.md` with its address, the quoted wording and the arithmetic. List each borrowed file there too, with licence, author and page. Say in the brief what will be fetched, from where and under which licence.
4. **People on screen**: no close-up or slow motion of an identifiable private person. No real personal data: names, numbers and accounts that are not public are blurred or replaced by fictional stand-ins with `<ExampleBadge />`. Never use generated images as footage.

## 10. Building it here

- **Layers**: footage at the bottom (`OffthreadVideo` with `trimBefore` / `trimAfter`, or `Img`), one CSS `filter` for the grade, the gradients as `div`s, graphics above, and the caption card on its own layer above the scene track, because captions cross cuts.
- **Hard cuts**: scenes with no entry or exit animation and `<SceneTrack sweeps={false} />`; the white flash is a full-frame `div` for 1–2 frames. This style overrides the engine's soft scene entries. A hard cut needs the timeline built with `{ overlap: 0 }`: the engine's default leaves the old scene under the new one for 8 frames.
- **Chunk slam**: `prog(frame, at, 4, ease.back)` mapped to scale 1.3 → 1, not `MaskRise` and not a spring. It deliberately breaks the engine's habit of starting entrances just under full size. The keyword block is a `div` behind the word, wiped with `clip-path: inset(…)` from the reading side.
- **Counters and charts**: `fontVariantNumeric: "tabular-nums"`, value from `prog(…, ease.out)`; charts are SVG, lines drawn with `stroke-dashoffset`, bars grown with `scaleX` from the reading side.
- **Documents**: a page image (uploaded, or captured with `npm run shot` and `full`) on a dark `div` with a slight `rotate`; highlighters are `div`s in `mix-blend-mode: multiply` placed in percentages of the page.
- **Freeze and map**: Remotion's `<Freeze>` around the footage, then the punch, a `saturate(.45)` filter and SVG brackets; the mask transition is the photo inside an SVG `<clipPath>` of the region, scaling up.
- **Leans on**: `Finish` for the light grain; `Device` when a phone recording has to sit inside a 16:9 frame; `isolateNumbers` for ranges.
- **The pulse lives in `timeline.ts`** (15 frames): every cut, slam and counter start is a multiple or a named offset of it, and `Soundtrack.tsx` uses the same numbers. A clip's own sound plays from the footage layer at a set volume.

## 11. Pitfalls of the medium

- **Text that can't be read in its shot.** Chart titles of three words at most; small ornamental labels (datelines) must recur readably elsewhere; a counter must hold its final value long enough to read.
- **Two-row big lines in 16:9** read as a slide. Split the sentence instead.
- **The voice running to the cut**: lines that end on the cut feel rushed; shorten the line or move the cut one pulse later.
- **Words that sound like other words**: if `npm run review` hears a word wrong, a viewer may too. Rewrite the line with plainer words.
- **Words stronger than the source**: if the report says "stressor", the caption does not say "killer"; quarterly figures added up are not "the annual figure". Write what the source says.
- **Footage from elsewhere**: illustrative footage never carries a place label it doesn't have; the date of an older photo is stated in its credit.
- **Blank first frames on transitions**: start split, grid and sting animations 3–4 frames before the cut so the first frame already has picture.
- **Station logos and watermarks**: choose another shot; never crop one out, blur it or park a caption over it.

## 12. In a product video

- **The brand's colour is the one accent** (the honey yellow's role): the keyword block, the bug, the point on every chart. Ivory, ink and the dark ledger stay. Red keeps meaning "the bad number"; if the brand is a red, give losses a cold grey instead.
- **The bug carries the brand's own name**, not an invented newsroom: this is the brand reporting on itself, and it should look like that.
- **The product is the footage.** Screen recordings and real screenshots, graded like everything else. A vertical recording fills a 9:16 frame as it is; in 16:9 it sits in a `Device` on the far side of centre over a ledger card, never pillar-boxed. "The document says it" works on the product's own page: its site or store listing on the desk, the line highlighted, the number popped.
- **Every figure has its source tag** (the site, the store listing, a report, with its date) and its line in `facts.md`. Sample accounts and illustrative charts carry `<ExampleBadge />`.
- **End card**: a two-part verdict caption over the product in use, the logo where the bug was, and a lower third with one line on how to get it.
- **Arabic**: the whole layout mirrors: card flush right, bug top right, credit top left, cards sliding in from the left, bars growing from the right, the keyword block wiping right to left. Chunks slam as **whole words**, never letter by letter, with no letter-spacing, in a heavy Arabic sans (a bold Kufi-based or modern naskh sans). Counters and ranges go through `isolateNumbers`.
- **9:16**: the layout in section 2: chunks stacked in the lower-middle above the bottom 22%, bug and tag under the top 12%, credit chip moved off the right 12%, grid panels stacked.

## 13. Variation space

You decide per film: topic, the turn, which formats are spent where, footage, the accent colour (keep it to one), the title.

- **Three structures:** the inverted pyramid with a mid-film turn (bad number → "really?" → the document says otherwise → why); a list of three that turns on the third; a countdown of numbers that ends on the one that matters.
- **Three openings:** the biggest number in the first frames; the document headline highlighted in the first second; a freeze on the event with a question.
- **Three endings:** a two-part verdict caption on footage with the natural sound returning after a silence; back to the hook's number, now re-read; the last data card's key point becoming the closing caption.
