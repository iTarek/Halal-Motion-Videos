# Liminal Found Footage

> A camcorder tape of a walk through empty, endless, fluorescent-lit interiors after hours. Printed notices and the camera's own machinery tell the story; nobody is in danger and nothing jumps out.
> Genre reference (grammar only): liminal-space photography (familiar interiors with nobody in them) and 1990s consumer camcorder footage. **Never** use community names, numbered "levels", or any existing organisation or logo inside the film. Everything on screen (notices, forms, the company voice) is original.

Suits short films; the length follows the walk, and the interest thins out once nothing new turns up.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

- **One first-person camcorder recording** (1990s VHS, 4:3), handheld, continuous.
- **An empty, familiar interior** that repeats: an office, a hotel floor, a school, a mall, a parking level, a clinic wing. Humming fluorescent light, no people.
- **Interest comes from emptiness, repetition, polite bureaucratic notices, and the camera's own machinery reacting at the right moment.** Nothing chases anyone. The mood is a strange calm and a growing curiosity: what is this place for, and what is at the end of it?
- **The medium is the storyteller**: focus, exposure, tape damage and the on-screen display carry the plot.

Not a thriller (no creatures, no threat, nothing leaping at the lens), not an ASCII/CRT terminal piece (the artefacts are tape and lens, not phosphor and scanlines), not a ruin (clean is part of the style).

## 2. Materials & rendering

- **Frame**: a 4:3 picture, pillarboxed in 16:9; in 9:16 it runs the full width in the upper-middle of the frame, with black above and below. Keep the picture soft: sharpness gives away the computer.
- **Space**: axis-aligned partitions on a grid with gaps and stubs, pillars, a drop ceiling of tiles with recessed light panels at regular intervals. A hand-authored route through a space that keeps going, so every doorway shows more emptiness.
- **Lighting = many lights, no shadows**: every ceiling panel throws a soft pool on the floor and walls beneath it, and nothing blocks anything: this light is flat. Per-light variation (a few percent warmer, cooler or greener), a few percent dead, a few percent flickering. Seams and corners darken slightly.
- **Haze follows local light**: where lights are dead the haze is dark, so an object standing there reads as a *shape cut out of the light*. The far end of any corridor falls to about 15% of the haze colour, so corridors dissolve into darkness, never into a bright wall.
- **Restrained wear**: tide-marks at the foot of some walls, a discoloured band under the ceiling, rare peeling seams, a few yellowed or missing tiles. Percentages in single digits.
- **Printed matter**: office laser-print on yellowed paper, condensed sans headers, form numbers and revisions, numbered rules, a polite closing line from "Management". Pinned or taped.
- **VHS camcorder pass** (not a CRT): small line jitter (a low-frequency wobble of a quarter pixel at most), a head-switching band at the bottom, tracking tears on demand, vignette on the image but **not** on the display text, luma blur with edge ringing, chroma smeared to the right, light smear, drifting white balance, gain noise in the darks, dropouts, lifted blacks, a soft clip. New noise on every frame. No scanlines, no RGB mask.
- **On-screen display (OSD)**: a hand-made bitmap font with a black outline: REC dot, battery, time and date. A camcorder title generator uses the same font, larger.

## 3. Colour logic

- **One dominant, slightly off hue** from the location's materials (mustard wallpaper, institutional green, clinic blue, beige laminate), with a darker trim and a mottled floor of the same family; ceiling and light panels near-white and slightly warm.
- **Exactly one saturated colour**: a signal such as a red call lamp, a green exit sign, an amber indicator. It is the only thing that blooms.
- White balance drifts; colour is never graded clean.
- Example material palettes: wallpaper `#d5bd66`, trim `#4d3d26`, carpet `#8c763c`; pool tile `#9fc7c0`, grout `#e6ece6`, water `#5e8f96`; school corridor `#b9c49a`, lockers `#6f7f66`, linoleum `#a89a7d`.

## 4. Type & on-screen text

- **Narrated lines are closed captions (CEA-608 style)**: a semibold monospace (the kind IBM Plex Mono is), white on a solid black box per row, at most 32 characters a row, centred inside the 4:3 picture above the OSD. Untouched by tape noise (the television decodes them). Each row appears as the voice reaches it.
- **Speaker marks**: `[PA]` for announcements, italics for the operator, sound descriptions in brackets (`[LIGHTS FLICKER]`, `[TAPE NOISE]`, `[SILENCE]`).
- **Notices are not captioned**: they are the picture. Readable at phone size and uncovered for at least 3–4 s. For close-ups **lower the camera so the lens is parallel to the page**; pitching down at a vertical sheet shears words into fake italics.
- The **title** is camcorder title-generator text over the first shot.
- **Faces**: the caption monospace and the bitmap OSD font are the style's own and stay whatever the brand uses. A brand's face appears only where the brand itself is printed: on a notice's letterhead, on the product.

## 5. Motion quality

- **One continuous handheld take.** Cuts happen only where the tape breaks: power-on roll-in, tracking tear, REC off.
- **Handheld** = walking gait (head bob of a few centimetres, sway, small roll) + breathing (one breath every 3–4 s) + tremor at a few frequencies whose size follows an **alertness** curve. At a held breath, **freeze all of it**.
- **Looking down** shows only shoe toes and floor, never legs.
- **Autofocus** = focus distance animated with overshoot: blur → lock → slip → lock. Blur comes from pulling focus *near*. Zooming narrows the view and softens everything off the focus distance.
- **Lights flicker** as short blackouts (about 3 frames); auto-exposure catches up with a lag (about 0.45 s) and gain noise pumps.
- Things that move by themselves move slowly (a door over more than a second, eased), never snap.

## 6. Camera grammar

A vocabulary, not a route. Opening and ending come from the topic.

| Move | What it expresses | Can serve |
|---|---|---|
| Walking POV down a corridor | routine, the endless | establishing the space; time passing |
| Slow pan off a detail to the space | scale; the space goes on | a reveal; a first look |
| Glance down at the feet | following an instruction | a floor marking; a line to stay behind |
| Crouch and hold on printed text | reading; bureaucracy | a rule; a contradiction |
| Zoom + focus hunt into the distance | seeing / not yet seeing | a far detail; a doubt |
| Identical framing after a tear, only the OSD changed | time removed | a jump; a long wait skipped |
| Turn a corner, hold still | a threshold | an exit; a choice |
| Slow push-in with no sound | the pull of something | a door; a sign; the one lit thing |
| Backing away while filming | giving something room | leaving a room; taking in the whole of it |
| Camera set down on a surface, static, recording alone | patience | a long wait; the operator busy off-frame |
| Whip toward a sound, then nothing | expectation relaxed | a noise that was only the building |

Framing: text readable and uncovered; the thing the walk is heading for stays small, far, and in a region of dead lights until the end. Transitions: tape events only.

## 7. Sound palette

- **Only what the tape would have recorded.** Foley by material: carpet steps (a low thud) or hard-floor squeaks, cloth rustles, the camcorder's clack and motor, the zoom servo, autofocus ticks, a ballast "tink" on every flicker, a stick-slip hinge, a trolley rolled across the floor above, a vending machine, a pipe.
- **Ambience bed**: the fluorescents. A mains hum with its harmonics, a buzzy band on top, a faint ballast whine, bursts from a bad tube; it follows the same light curve as the picture and sits closer in small rooms. It is a room, never a note.
- **What gets a sound**: every flicker, every tape event (roll-in, tear, REC off), every focus lock, every step. A slow push-in toward the goal gets nothing at all.
- **Weights**: autofocus ticks are the lightest, a door or the camcorder's clack the middle, and the fluorescents restriking together after a blackout the one heavy moment.
- **Silence**: complete silence for a second or more, tails included, captioned `[SILENCE]`, is the strongest tool. Spend it once, just before the walk reaches its goal.
- **Sound across a cut**: the hum of the next room arrives a few frames before the tear clears, or the tape motor runs on over the black after REC off.
- **Tonal accents**: one short original chime before a PA announcement; after a time jump it may sound slightly flat. Nothing else is pitched.
- **Voice**: the PA is calm, corporate and dry, every word clear. The operator has a few short, quiet lines, close to the microphone, with breath: breathing that speeds up with effort, one held breath, one long exhale.

## 8. Native moves

A menu: use the ones your story needs.

- **Autofocus / auto-exposure hunting.** Something is there only while focus is correct. *Fits content like:* a missing pet on a hotel floor; a lone groundskeeper in an empty stadium; a sign at the end of a long corridor.
- **Tape tracking damage.** A tear is a cut the tape made; same framing after, but the clock jumped, the battery drained, a light died. *Fits content like:* a night-watch log; a weekend stocktake in a mall; a house that ages between tapes.
- **The OSD is plot.** REC, battery, date and time code are evidence. *Fits content like:* proof of a delivery; a countdown; a dated inspection.
- **Rules as text in frame.** Notices and PA announcements start ordinary, grow oddly specific, contradict each other, and finally address the viewer. *Fits content like:* a pool's safety rules; a school fire drill; a museum after closing.
- **Emptiness and repetition.** The same notice in a different place; the same room with one detail changed; a corridor into haze. *Fits content like:* a moving-day walkthrough; an airport at 4 a.m.; an office the night before opening day.
- **The one thing left on.** Far down a stretch of dead lights, one lit object: a desk lamp, a screen, a kiosk, a parcel on a chair. Small and far at first, a shape cut out of the light, then the walk reaches it. *Fits content like:* the last desk still working; a locker with something waiting in it; the product at the end of the corridor.

## 9. Pitfalls of the medium

- **A fully bright scene reads as an ordinary corridor** → partitions with gaps; far areas fall dark.
- **Light with no shadows leaks everywhere** → a dark zone needs every panel near it dead, and the space behind it dim.
- **The corridor's end shows as a bright rectangle** → the far end at about 15% of the haze colour.
- **Uniform haze lifts dark shapes to grey** → haze follows local light.
- **Line wobble skews text; pitching down italicises it** → a quarter pixel of wobble at most; lower the camera instead of tilting it.
- **Legs built from simple shapes read as tubes** → show only shoe toes.
- **A pure red lamp looks dull and never glows** → give it a hot orange-white core and a wide soft halo.
- **A hum loud enough to notice hides every small sound** → keep the bed low and let it dip with the lights.
- **Retiming a finished walk** breaks every cue → keep all keys in one time base and stretch them through one function, sound cues included.
- **Text on a tilted plane goes soft** → author notices large and scale them down, and hold the lens square to them when they must be read.

## 10. Building it here

- **The original was true 3D; here it is 2.5D in CSS.** No 3D library and no shaders. The corridor is a set of flat planes (floor, ceiling, walls, partitions, door leaves) under one `perspective` with `transform-style: preserve-3d`. The camera is the stage's inverse transform: `translateZ` to walk, `rotateY` to turn, small translations and rolls for the hand.
- **Keep the route simple**: straight runs with side openings and one or two right-angle turns, not a free maze. Split long walls into segments and drop the ones behind the camera. Leave out real shadows, reflections and anything seen from all sides.
- **Surfaces and light**: wallpaper, carpet and ceiling tiles are CSS gradients and SVG patterns; each light pool is a `radial-gradient` on the floor and walls under its panel, with its own tint and its own on/off curve. Haze is a stack of translucent planes down the corridor, each darker where the lights near it are dead.
- **Focus and exposure**: depth of field is `filter: blur(…)` per depth band (a `filter` flattens the 3D inside the element it sits on, so each band is its own `perspective` wrapper with the same camera transform, blurred as a whole); the autofocus hunt is the one place this style leans on springs (`springTo` with `SPRING.bouncy` for blur → lock → slip → lock). Exposure is `brightness()` easing toward its target over about 0.45 s.
- **Handheld**: sums of `noise(frame * f, seed)` at a few frequencies for position and roll, a sine for the gait, all multiplied by the alertness curve from `timeline.ts`. Never `Math.random`.
- **Tape pass**: one SVG filter on the 4:3 picture: `feTurbulence` (seed from the frame) into `feDisplacementMap` for line wobble, a horizontal `feGaussianBlur` for luma softness, `feColorMatrix` + `feOffset` to smear chroma right, plus CSS for lifted blacks and a vignette. Tears and the head-switching band are horizontal strips cut with `clip-path` and shifted sideways. Leave out lens barrel distortion.
- **Overrides**: no `SceneFrame` entries, `<SceneTrack sweeps={false} />` if scenes are used at all, no `Finish` (the noise is on the tape), no `MaskRise`. OSD and captions are plain text layers above the picture: the OSD inside the tape pass, the captions outside it.
- **Timing**: the walk's keys, flickers, tears and focus locks live in `timeline.ts`; `Soundtrack.tsx` lands ticks, tinks and tape sounds on the same frames.

## 11. In a product video

- **The brand's colour is the one saturated signal**: the lamp, the sign, or the glow of the product's screen at the end of the walk. The location keeps its own muted palette; that palette is the look.
- **The product is "the one thing left on".** The walk ends at a desk, a kiosk or a phone on a chair; autofocus locks, and the real screenshot or screen recording is on that screen (a `Device` with `float={0}` set square to the lens, or a `Phone` for a still screenshot, under the tape pass but sharp enough to read). The empty building is the problem; the lit screen is the answer.
- **Notices carry the copy.** What the product says about itself, in its own words, printed as office notices; the invented frame around them (form numbers, "Management") stays plainly fictional, with no real organisation named.
- **Real numbers only**: any figure on a notice, on the OSD or on the product's screen that is a claim is in `facts.md`; sample accounts and made-up figures carry `<ExampleBadge />`.
- **End card**: REC off, then the title generator sets one line on how to get the product over the frozen last frame; the logo sits clean above it, outside the tape noise like the captions.
- **Arabic**: captions stay centred rows in a plain monospace Arabic face; notices are set right to left in an ordinary office naskh; the title generator shows whole words in a square Kufic-style bitmap face. Words appear whole, never letter by letter, with no letter-spacing. The OSD stays as a camcorder draws it (REC, digits), wrapped with `isolateNumbers` where it sits beside Arabic.
- **9:16**: the 4:3 picture sits in the upper-middle band, clear of the top 12%; captions go in the black below it and above the bottom 22%; nothing important in the right 12%. For a fuller frame, crop the tape to 3:4 and keep the goal of the walk on the centre line.

## 12. Variation space

You decide the location and its materials, the reason for filming, the notices, the route, what waits at the end (or nothing), the signal colour, the opening, the ending and the length.

- Structures: **two tapes** (the same route filmed twice, a year apart; the second tape finds what changed); **a property walkthrough** where every room is described cheerfully by the operator while the building keeps adding rooms; **a checklist read aloud** item by item, each item ticked on a clipboard in frame, until an item is not on the list.
- Openings: **mid-sentence** (the tape starts with the operator already talking, tracking still rolling in); **a test pattern** and a date slate before the camera is picked up; **the lens cap on**, sound only, until the cap comes off.
- Endings: **battery dies** (the icon blinks, the frame freezes on the empty cell); **the camera is set down** and keeps recording an empty room; **the tape loops** back to the first shot, now with one more light on in it.
