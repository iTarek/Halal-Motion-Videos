# Copperplate Engraving

> An intaglio plate that engraves itself: a burin cuts swelling lines into copper, a proof is pulled, and the figure builds up (outline, hatching, cross-hatching) until engraved lettering, magnified details and a hand-laid wash make a finished plate.
> References (grammar only): 19th-century natural-history plates (Haeckel, Audubon, Westwood, the *Britannica* copperplates) for the centred subject, numbered roundels, roman title over an italic name, "del." / "sculp." credits and the plate mark; the book engravers of the 18th and 19th centuries for the burin line (hatching that follows the form, crossing only in half-tones); hand-coloured plates for washes laid after printing. Never copy a plate, pose, border or title.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

An **intaglio print**: the line is ink held in a groove a burin cut into copper. Three rules:

1. **Tone is made of lines, never of fills.** Light is bare paper; half-tone is one family of parallel lines; shadow a second family crossing it; the deepest dark a third family that makes lozenges. No grey fill anywhere in the ink.
2. **Every line swells and tapers.** The burin enters fine, bites deeper mid-run and lifts out to a point. Lines thicken where the form turns from the light and vanish where it faces it; contours are cut in several runs and thin where runs hand over. A line of uniform width reads as a pen or vector drawing: never.
3. **Colour comes afterwards, by hand**: transparent watercolour laid flat region by region over the finished black print, spilling past the lines, pooling at its edges, ink always on top. A film may stay monochrome long; colour arriving is an event.

Not a woodcut (black block, white gouges), not a pen sketch (loose broken lines), not an engineering drawing, not a sepia filter.

## 2. Materials & rendering

- **The sheet is the world**: a sheet of laid paper in the frame's own shape (a landscape plate in 16:9, a portrait plate in 9:16), cream (about `#f1e8d2`), with texture fixed to the sheet (fibres, laid lines, sparse foxing) that zooms with the camera; a pressed, bevelled **plate mark** and a ruled border (a rule and a hairline) frame it.
- **Ink** is one warm near-black (about `#1c1510`). Width ratios: outlines heaviest (swelling from about 0.45× to 1.35×), first hatching from a hair to about ten times that, the crossing family about 0.8× of the first, the third about 0.6×, hair and stipple finest. Spacing is **relative to the figure** (about 1/180 of its height, a little wider per later family) so small figures never go grey.
- **Swell law** for every hatch line: thin at both ends, fullest in the middle, tapered over its first and last fifth. Families switch on by tone: the first at light half-tone, the second at mid shadow, the third only in the deepest quarter.
- **One light direction** for the whole film (upper left by convention); heavier outlines away from it; tone comes from the shape (a pillow, a sphere, a cylinder), never from texture. **Occlusion** is geometric: lines stop at the shapes in front.
- **Roundels**: a double-ruled circle, a machine-ruled ground with a white halo round the subject, content drawn large and scaled in. **Leaders**: hairlines from a dot on the part to the rim, routed with clearance, a small italic figure number at the root.
- **Hand colouring** multiplies over paper and under ink: a little spill, slight misregister, a tide-line at the wet edge, uneven density, an occasional backrun, granulation; each region blooms from where the brush touched.
- **Copper** (when shown): polished warm metal, polishing hairlines, a soft reflected window; a groove is a dark trough with a bright burr on the side of the light. The **burin** (a lozenge steel shaft, bevelled face, mushroom handle) is held low and turned at most 15° off the line so the fresh groove shows; **swarf** is a narrowing ribbon curling ahead of the point, one edge lit.

## 3. Colour logic

- The print is two values, paper and ink; all tone is line density.
- Colour is **transparent wash only**, in muted earth and mineral pigments, one stronger pigment at most. Never opaque, never on lettering, rules or paper outside the figure.
- Reserve it: the whole subject at one moment, or only the part that matters, or none.
- Example wash sets: an amber insect with blue-grey wings; a map with a sea-green coastal band on buff land; one madder-rose organ on an otherwise black anatomy plate.

## 4. Type & on-screen text

- **Lettering belongs to the plate**: roman capitals in a high-contrast face (the Bodoni kind; the title about 4–5% of the sheet's short side, widely tracked in Latin script; labels about a third of that), italics for scientific names, a copperplate script for notes, at a fixed size, balanced over two lines, never shrunk.
- **This style needs its own faces**: a brand's font would read as modern print. The brand's face appears only in its logo.
- **Roman letters are cut one after another; script is written in one stroke in its reading direction.** Nothing fades in.
- **Narrated lines**: script on a deckled paper slip, low and centred, at most 2 lines, never over a label, each word written as it is spoken.
- **Labels are small on the sheet**, so the camera comes to reading zoom for each one and holds there.

## 5. Motion quality

- **Cutting order is the animation**: outline → first hatching sweeping across each form → crossing → darks, hair, stipple. Strokes run at constant burin speed, many at once (a few for outlines, dozens for hatching).
- A long single stroke may be followed by the camera; start it partly cut so no frame is empty. Keep a full build to a few seconds.
- Roundels fly on an eased arc with a small lift, growing steadily to size. Colour blooms from one point with a ragged front and a wet rim that dries; regions are staggered.
- Nothing fades or slides like UI: lines are cut, text is cut or written, colour blooms, sheets are pressed, peeled or laid down.

## 6. Camera grammar

The camera is a **loupe over a sheet**. A vocabulary, not a route. Opening and ending come from the topic.

| Move | Expresses | Can serve |
|---|---|---|
| Loupe following the burin tip | One point of attention; making | a first mark; a signature |
| Long even pull-out | The line was part of something larger | scale reveal; context |
| Push to a part, ride with its roundel | Where → what, in one take | an invisible detail; a mechanism |
| Tilt or pan along the sheet at reading zoom | Labels in order | a parts list; a coastline |
| Pull back while something flies | Everything placed in relation | acceleration; summary |
| Locked frame, or a breathing push of 2% at most | The plate as document | reading time; colour moving |
| Raking view across the copper | The groove as depth | the cost of craft |
| Cut from mirrored copper to true proof | Reversal | a hidden truth; before/after |

Framing: the subject inside the plate mark, labels and roundels in the margins; order details so push-ins never crop a note early. Transitions come from printmaking only (a proof pulled, a tissue guard laid over, a region burnished out and re-cut, a new sheet on the bed); no dissolves or wipes.

## 7. Sound palette

- **Foley by material.** Paper: peel crackle, a slip tapped down, tissue rustle, a sheet's whoosh. Press: roller rumble, a thump. Copper and steel: a high, thin burin hiss with chatter and a faint ring of the plate, swarf pings, a tick on lift, a circular scribe, a burnishing rub. Ink and water: pen scratch, ticks for cut letters, a water plip and a wet brush swish.
- **Hatching is micro-scratches**, denser with each pass; one sound per pass, not per line. A held, finished plate is silent.
- **Three weights**: a tick for a letter or a lift, the hiss for a run of lines, one heavy press thump for the proof.
- **A bed of room**: a quiet workroom with a faint clock, then the softer hush of a paper room once the print exists. Never tonal.
- **Small accents only**: a tiny ting as a scribed ring closes, a soft low knock as a roundel lands.
- **Silence is real**: before the proof is pulled, cut the room tone too and keep at most one small sound.
- **Sound across a cut**: the roller's rumble begins over the last frames of copper and ends on the first frame of paper.
- **Voice**: a quiet curator or naturalist.

## 8. Native moves

A menu: use the ones your story needs.

- **The cut.** Burin mid-stroke, swarf rising, nothing else. *Fits:* the first line of a treaty; a watchmaker's scribe; a coast being charted.
- **The three passes.** Form gains volume pass by pass. *Fits:* a building from its plan; a portrait emerging; a skill improving.
- **Magnification roundel.** A ring scribed, burnished, re-cut larger, flown to a margin with a leader, its label cut on landing. *Fits:* a gear in a clock; a pollen grain; a harbour inset on a chart.
- **The proof.** The sheet peels off; mirrored copper becomes true paper. *Fits:* a draft made final; a design made public; a coin's die and strike.
- **States of the plate.** A region burnished out and re-cut. *Fits:* a border redrawn; before/after; a design revision.
- **Hand colouring.** Washes bloom after the print. *Fits:* one organ lit; a region on a historical map; a ripening fruit.
- **Natural size.** A tiny true-scale figure beside the engraving. *Fits:* a seed by its tree; a chip by its circuit; a stamp by its enlargement.
- **The finished plate.** Every label and credit, locked. *Fits:* a museum label; a frontispiece; a certificate.

## 9. Pitfalls of the medium

- Uniform width reads as pen → swell every hatch, pinch contours between runs, keep the thinnest line hair-fine.
- Overlaps without occlusion → every form hides the lines behind it.
- Outline-only parts beside a hatched body → hatch along each part's axis, a dark at every joint.
- A burin lying along the cut hides the groove; a big skew looks like poking → 15° at most. A regular swarf helix reads as a spring → vary its winding and twist, light one edge.
- Reflections over grooves wash them out → paint the copper's light first, grooves on top. Roundels drawn small turn muddy when enlarged → draw large, scale in. Leaders hugging the subject read as crossing it → clearance.
- A long early stroke is still drawing when the camera leaves → start it partly cut.
- A push-in cropping a fresh note breaks its reading time → put consecutive details on one side.
- Hair-fine lines vanish or shimmer at phone size → check the phone review sheet; thicken the thinnest lines in wide views and let them go fine only up close.
- Scientific names trip the voice → give the voice line a plain spelling and check what the review heard back.

## 10. Building it here

- **It is all SVG, which suits engraving.** A swelling line cannot be a stroke (strokes have one width): generate each hatch as a thin filled lens-shaped `path`, and each contour as a filled ribbon in several runs. Generate them once from the shape and its tone function, with `noise(i, seed)` for small irregularities, never `Math.random`.
- **Cutting on**: for outlines and hero strokes, a `mask` holding the stroke's centreline drawn on with `stroke-dashoffset`; for hatching, one mask per form that sweeps across it, so dozens of lines appear in order without a mask each. Merge each family into one `path` to keep the frame light.
- **Occlusion by painter's order**: each form carries a paper-coloured fill under its own lines, hiding what lies behind.
- **Paper and plate**: `feTurbulence` with a fixed `seed` for fibres, a `repeating-linear-gradient` for laid lines, an inset shadow for the plate mark, all inside the camera wrapper so they zoom with the sheet. The camera is one `transform`; for a long pull-out interpolate the *logarithm* of the scale so the speed looks even.
- **Wash**: region fills under the ink with `mix-blend-mode: multiply`, revealed by a growing `clip-path: circle()` roughened with `feDisplacementMap`, plus a darker blurred rim that fades as it "dries".
- **Copper, burin, swarf**: CSS gradients for the metal and its window reflection, each groove a dark line with a light line beside it, the tool and the ribbon small SVG groups moved along the stroke.
- **Overrides**: no springs, no `MaskRise` (lettering is cut or written with a `clip-path` wipe), no `Finish`, no `Backdrop`, and `<SceneTrack sweeps={false} />`; moves use `prog` with `ease.inOut` or linear.
- **The stroke schedule lives in `timeline.ts`**; `Soundtrack.tsx` starts each pass's hiss on the frame its first line starts and each letter's tick on its frame.

## 11. In a product video

- **Paper and ink are the look; the brand colour is the one stronger pigment** in the wash, laid only on the part that matters. Until it arrives the film is black on cream.
- **The product is engraved as an object** (its outline, hatched in three passes), with its features as numbered roundels and real feature names as labels. The **real screenshot arrives as the colour event**: multiplied onto the paper inside the engraved device outline or a roundel, slightly muted, with the ink lines on top, so it reads as a hand-coloured print. One screen at a time; no screen recordings (nothing here slides like UI).
- **One real number may stand alone** as an engraved figure; it and every label claim are in `facts.md`. Illustrative figures carry `<ExampleBadge />`.
- **Credits become the brand's**: where a plate says "del." and "sculp.", put the product's name as the publisher's line.
- **End card**: the finished plate. The title in roman capitals, the logo (unaltered) under the border, one line on how to get it in the script.
- **Arabic**: mirror the plate (roundels and figure numbers swap margins); words are struck whole, one at a time, never letter by letter or stroke by stroke, with no tracking; a classical Naskh with strong thick-and-thin contrast for titles and labels, a flowing handwritten Ruq'ah for notes.
- **9:16**: a portrait plate with the figure in the upper middle and roundels stacked in the lower margin; the narration slip sits above the bottom 22%, and the title stays below the top 12%, clear of the right 12%.

## 12. Variation space

You decide subject, details, order, opening, ending, camera path, colour and pacing. The use cases below are **grammar for the order of information**; build your own timeline from the user's facts.

| Use case | Information order | Holds | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Museum label / case screen** | title and scientific name → detail, term and note (several) → where to see it | title 2 s or more; final plate 4 s or more | 30–40 s |
| **"One minute on …"** | question → name → 2–3 facts → one number → follow prompt | the number alone for 2.5 s or more | 35–60 s |
| **Textbook figure** | figure → parts labelled one by one → caption | each label for its reading time | 20–45 s |
| **Craft heritage** | object → 2–3 craft details → maker / edition | as above | 30–45 s |
| **Map / site plan** | place → insets → legend → visiting info | an inset 3 s or more; the legend 4 s or more | 30–45 s |

- Structures: **states of one plate** (one view re-cut three times as a place changes); **a book of plates** (four small plates turned like pages, one fact each, no roundels); **a dissection** (parts labelled in teaching order, colour only on the part taught).
- Openings: **the finished proof burnished back** to its first line; **on paper at natural size** (title cut first, figure grows below); **raking light over blank copper** as the design is traced.
- Endings: **plate beside its proof**; **the natural-size figure** held alone; **a blank new sheet** laid on the stack.
