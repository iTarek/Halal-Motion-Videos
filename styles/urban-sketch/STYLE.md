# Urban Sketch · Pen & Wash

> A sketchbook page drawn on location: fine sepia fineliner lines that wobble, overshoot and never quite close, with loose transparent watercolour dropped on top. The colour sits slightly off the lines, spills over them, and dissolves into cream cold-press paper at the page edge.
> References (grammar only):
> - Urban-sketching sketchbook pages: line first, then wash; colour off-register; handwritten date and place in the corner; the page edge breaking up into dabs.
> - Hand-drawn films where a monochrome world holds one coloured object.
> - *The Red Balloon* (1956): an object as the protagonist, the camera following it across a city.
>
> Nothing is copied from these: no characters, layouts or type.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

Reportage drawing, made on location in a few minutes. Two layers that stay visibly separate:

1. **Ink.** One thin, near-constant line (a 0.3–0.5 mm fineliner) in dark sepia. It wobbles slightly, overshoots corners a little, breaks mid-stroke, and suggests rather than describes: a few window marks instead of a grid, scalloped fragments instead of a whole tree outline.
2. **Wash.** Transparent watercolour glazed on top, **misregistered** from the line by about a line's width, sometimes spilling past it, leaving paper white for highlights and figures (reserves). Dried edges are darker (pigment pools at the rim), and pigment granulates into the paper tooth.

**The page is part of the image**: warm cream cold-press paper with large-scale mottling and fine tooth. Nothing is painted edge to edge; the painting frays into leaf-sized dabs and white holes, leaving margins for handwriting.

Not Watercolor Brush (no line-free wet-in-wet painting, no painterly edges without ink), not Whiteboard (no marker, no explainer diagrams), not Chinese Ink Wash (no brush calligraphy, no monochrome ink tones). Line and wash, both, always.

## 2. Materials & rendering

- **Paper**: two scales of soft mottling (a few percent), and a tooth multiplied over everything, figures included.
- **Ink**: about 96% opaque; the line is thinner far and thicker near (roughly 0.12–0.3% of the frame's short side); a wobble of about a third of its width; corners overshoot by 0.3–0.7% of the short side; a third to half of long strokes get one small gap. Every stroke has its own draw time, so the page plays back in real stroke order.
- **Wash**: 2–3 rough-edged layers, multiplied, each at about 0.4–0.65 opacity, offset from the line by about one line width, with a dried edge 30–40% darker; pigment granulates (its density varies by about ±18% with the paper).
- **Colour arrival**: every part of the page has a time at which colour reaches it. The wash is revealed through it with a ragged wet front (a darker band just behind the front) and noisy edges. Figures bloom from a point inside a noisy circle.
- **Foliage**: pale base wash, then light and shadow masses, then small leaf dabs coloured by the light's direction, holes at the rim, scalloped ink only on the outline.
- **Sky**: a few big soft washes at low opacity, fading upward; clouds are reserves with a tinted belly.
- **Buildings**: local colour; the shadow side mixed about 45% with a cool violet-grey; distant blocks mixed about 50% with a haze colour.
- **Figures**: a simple jointed body seen flat; torso and limbs as soft tapered shapes; hair and head as one mass; the face left blank (one nose tick in profile); the shadow side mixed with violet; clothes loose and modest.
- **Page edge**: a noisy boundary, a band of white dabs inside it, a spray of coloured dabs outside.
- Deterministic: same seed, same marks.

## 3. Colour logic

- **Ink is one colour** (dark sepia or warm black); it never changes.
- **Wash is local colour, lightly**: few pigments, mixed on the page; shadows are the local colour mixed toward one shared cool violet; distance mixes toward one haze.
- **Paper white is the brightest value**; it is reserved, never painted.
- **Colour can be withheld**: a page may be ink-only with colour as an event (one tinted object, colour arriving with a cause).
- Example palettes: park in summer (lawn `#c3c96f`, leaves `#7fa04a`, sky `#9fbcd6`, limestone `#d8c6a0`); harbour morning (sea `#7fa6b8`, hulls `#c9573f`, rope `#c8a86b`, sky `#bcd0dc`); winter market (roofs `#8b93a6`, lights `#e2b04a`, awnings `#b8483e`, snow = paper).

## 4. Type & on-screen text

- **All text is handwriting on the page**, written on in reading order with pen sound, and it moves with the camera: a corner note in a quick thin hand (the Reenie Beanie kind: day, time, weather, place), titles and a closing note in a casual hand (the Caveat kind), aligned to the reading side in the blank margin.
- **Narrated lines**, if there is a voice: handwritten lines in the page margin, or a caption strip written into the lower margin as the words are spoken. Never a generic overlay box.
- **The hand is the look**: this style keeps its handwriting faces whatever the brand's typeface is; the brand's face appears only inside its logo.
- The style works well without any voice.

## 5. Motion quality

- **Figures boil**: their line wobble and wash seeds change every 2–3 frames (about 12 a second). The static page does not boil (it is a real page); the camera moves every frame, smoothly.
- **Performance**: anticipation, action, follow-through; footsteps on the pulse.
- **Invisible forces are drawn**: wind as ink curls that draw on and erase from the tail, with trailing dabs; sound as ink lines; heat as wavy strokes.
- **Colour never snaps**: it blooms from a point or arrives through the page.
- Stroke-order draw-on for anything that "appears".

## 6. Camera grammar

A 2D camera over a page two to three frames wide and tall. Designed moves ease in and out; follow shots chase a point a little ahead of the subject on a soft spring that never bounces, so the camera lags like a hand-held follow. A vocabulary, not a route. Opening and ending come from the topic.

| Move | What it expresses | Can serve |
|---|---|---|
| Push-in | attention; a detail observed | a face; a sign; a small event |
| Lateral follow on a spring | travel, pursuit | an object carried; a walker; a vehicle |
| Crane up | a new level, a view opening | a skyline; a tree; a balcony |
| Whip-tilt with slight roll | sudden change of direction | a fall; a gust; a surprise |
| Punch-in with a short shake | impact | a catch; a collision; a door slam |
| Pull back to the whole page | scale; the sketch as an object | a summary; a place in full |
| Tilt into the margin | from picture to words | notes; a date; a title |
| Slow pan across a line-only area | the world before colour | a quiet start; memory |
| Hold while the pen draws | the act of noticing | a subject being drawn |
| Page turn to a new spread | another time or place | a travel diary; a sequence of days |

Framing: subjects fill at least a third of the frame height at story moments; a small key object gets a faint paper halo (a reserve) so it reads over busy foliage. Transitions: camera travel on the page, page turns, or colour arriving; no dissolves.

## 7. Sound palette

- **Pen on cold-press paper**: band-passed noise (about 1.6–7.5 kHz) with a fibre texture and grain clicks, one burst per real stroke; many overlap into a hiss during a fast sketch, and short bright strokes tick.
- **Water and brush**: a wet brush "shhh" for each bloom, a jar rinse, a drip. A page turn is one soft flap.
- **Ambience from the place**: paper room tone first, then the location once it has colour (birds, traffic, harbour gulls, a market's murmur). Quiet, never tonal.
- **Foley follows objects** at sketch scale: fabric or paper flapping for things in the wind (rate and level from speed, left or right from where it is on screen), steps on the pulse, a bicycle bell, a splash.
- **What stays silent**: the camera, the paper's texture, and the boil.
- **Silence as a tool**: half a second of true silence, ambience included, at the height of suspense; the first sound after it is the next gesture.
- **Sound across a cut**: a place's ambience starts a second before we see it; the pen keeps scratching over a tilt into the margin.
- **Voice**, if any: unhurried and personal, like someone reading their own diary notes.

## 8. Native moves

A menu: use the ones your story needs.

- **Line before colour.** The pen draws a subject in real stroke order, then the wash drops in; the drawing is the spectacle. *Fits content like:* an old library's façade; a chef plating a dish; a street vendor's cart.
- **Colour as arrival.** Wash spreads from a source with a wet front, so an event paints the page; figures stay frozen line drawings until colour touches them. *Fits content like:* a sunrise over a harbour; a parade moving down a street; the lights of a market switching on.
- **The one coloured thing.** A colourless sketch with a single tinted object that becomes the protagonist. *Fits content like:* a red umbrella in the rain; a yellow taxi; a child's kite.
- **The page as frame.** Pull back until the whole page shows, edges dissolving, handwritten notes in the margin. *Fits content like:* a travel diary entry; a neighbourhood portrait; a day in a place.
- **Carrot people.** Distant crowds as one wash blob, two leg lines and a head dot. *Fits content like:* a station at rush hour; a stadium; a harbour promenade.

## 9. Pitfalls of the medium

- **Straight edges in the arriving colour** from clipping by a box → clip by the real noisy front.
- **Rectangular masks around complex silhouettes** leave notches → mask with the real silhouette.
- **Characters losing colour** when they move into unpainted areas → decide a figure's colour once (or tie it to the event), never from where it stands now.
- **Static ink can't be hidden by later washes** → erase far ink inside nearer shapes before drawing; use carrot people for far crowds.
- **Feet sliding while poses blend** → pin the standing foot and blend the other toward under the hip.
- **Front-facing heads go bald** → draw the hair mass, then knock the face out of it.
- **Thousands of live strokes slow every frame** → keep finished ink as one still layer and animate only the strokes being drawn.
- **Painting edge to edge** kills the sketchbook feel → leave margins and fray the edge.
- **A boiling page** reads as video noise → only figures and moving things boil.

## 10. Building it here

- **Ink is SVG**: a small deterministic pen function turns a shape into `<path>`s with wobble from `noise(x, seed)`, overshoot and gaps. Each stroke has a start frame and draws on with `stroke-dashoffset`; finished strokes move to a memoised still layer.
- **Wash is SVG shapes under `mix-blend-mode: multiply`**, their edges roughened by `feTurbulence` + `feDisplacementMap` at a fixed `seed`, offset a line's width from the ink, with a darker stroke of the same shape for the dried edge and a fine turbulence for granulation.
- **Colour arrival is a `mask`**: one growing, displaced blob per source, with a darker ring drawn just behind its front. Figures get a small noisy circle of their own.
- **Paper** is a cream `div` with two turbulence layers, multiplied over the whole page; the frayed edge is a `mask-image` plus dabs placed with `noise`.
- **Boil**: figures use a filter whose `seed` is `Math.floor(frame / 3)`; the page's filters never change.
- **Camera**: one `transform` on the page (`prog` with `ease.inOut`); the follow is worked out once for all frames, with the feel of `SPRING.soft`; a punch-in can use `SPRING.snappy`. Handwriting is text in the hand face revealed by a `clip-path` wipe along the line.
- **Overrides**: no `Finish` (the paper is the grain, and a page has no vignette), no `Backdrop`, no `MaskRise` or `Headline` (words are written, not risen), `<SceneTrack sweeps={false} />`. Nothing is 3D; a page turn is a flat `clip-path` peel. Scenes take no `SceneFrame` entry or exit: its blurred push is the engine's default look, not this one. The timeline's `overlap` is how long the old scene stays mounted under the new one (8 frames by default): build it with `{ overlap: 0 }` for a hard cut, or give it the length of this style's own transition.
- **Stroke start frames live in `timeline.ts`**; `Soundtrack.tsx` gives each real stroke, or each burst of fast ones, its pen sound on the same frames.

## 11. In a product video

- **The brand's colour is the wash that arrives, or the one coloured thing** on an ink-only page. Ink stays sepia and the other washes stay pale, so the brand colour leads without a second accent.
- **The product is sketched in place, then shown for real**: the phone or laptop is drawn in ink on the table or in a hand, its screen left as paper white. The real screenshot appears inside that reserve, flat and clean, and a push-in reads it. For a walkthrough, cut the page's phone to a `Device` held flat (`float={0}`), then return to the page.
- **Real numbers are margin notes** in the hand (a price, a count, a time), each listed in `facts.md`; anything illustrative carries `<ExampleBadge />`. Sketch no recognisable real person.
- **End card**: pull back to the whole page; the logo sits clean in the blank margin like a stamp, with one handwritten line on how to get it.
- **Arabic**: the blank margin moves to the right and notes align right; lateral follows run right to left. Handwriting is wiped in from the right a whole word at a time, never letter by letter, with no letter-spacing. Use an informal Arabic hand (a ruq'ah-style face).
- **9:16**: the page is a tall sketchbook page and the camera travels mostly up and down. Keep the corner note below the top 12%, the caption strip above the bottom 22%, and notes out of the right 12%.

## 12. Variation space

You decide the place, the subjects, what colour does (always there, withheld, arriving), the voice (or none), the camera path, the opening, the ending and the length.

- Structures: **a travel diary** of five pages, one per day, each page turned by the wind or a hand; **a single street corner** drawn at four times of day, the wash changing while the ink stays; **a recipe** where each ingredient is drawn from life at the market and the final dish is painted last.
- Openings: **the blank page** with only the handwritten date, then the first line of the horizon; **a close-up of a coffee cup** on the page's edge, pulling back to find the city drawn behind it; **rain dots** landing on the paper and blooming into the first washes.
- Endings: **the sketchbook closes** on the page; **the last figure walks off the page** into the blank margin; **the colour drains** back to ink as the evening comes, leaving one lit window.
