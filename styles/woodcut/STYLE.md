# Woodcut Print

> A relief print that carves itself: every frame is a black wood block, and light only exists where a knife has cut it away. One colour at most, and only for the thing that glows.
> References (grammar only): the wordless woodcut novels of the 1920s (Frans Masereel, Lynd Ward), told as a sequence of self-contained plates; Käthe Kollwitz's woodcuts, where feeling lives in the direction of the gouge; Gustave Doré's engraving, where line spacing and direction build light and volume. Never copy their plates, figures, borders or titles.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

Each shot is **one printed plate**: black ink on laid paper, framed by a paper margin. It follows three rules of real relief printing:

1. **Black is the wood, white is what the knife removed.** Light is *carved*: a bright area is many wide cuts, a dark area untouched wood.
2. **Pictures appear by being carved.** Nothing fades in: cuts grow stroke by stroke along the form, coarse gouges first, fine lines after.
3. **The block is a mirror.** Letters read backwards until the paper is pulled; that reversal is the style's own transition.

Not copperplate engraving (no swelling burin lines on white, no crosshatch built up from white ground), not linocut poster art (no flat colour areas), not a black-and-white photo run through a threshold filter (the cuts must follow the form).

## 2. Materials & rendering

- **Print frame**: the printed image area sits inside a paper margin; the wider bottom margin holds captions. The image edge is the block edge: ragged ink, never a clean rectangle. In 9:16 the block is tall and the bottom margin deeper.
- **Knife classes** (widths as a share of the frame's short side, at 1× camera):

| Tool | Width | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Knife | about 0.15% | contours, the white outline that separates a black figure from a black ground |
| V-gouge | 0.2–0.75% | hatching, hair, beard, fine light |
| U-gouge | 0.9–2% | rays, sky sweeps, the first rough-out |
| Stab | dots 0.3–0.75% in radius | snow, sparks, flecks of loam, wood chips |

- **Hatching (Doré's grammar in wood)**: line **direction follows form** (a round object is wrapped in arcs, slopes follow the fall line, fire and light radiate, cloth follows its folds). **Width follows tone**: bright = wide cuts almost merging into white, mid-tone = thin cuts, dark = no cuts. Far = dense and fine, near = sparse and coarse. Every line is a separate cut (3–15% of the short side long, blunt entry, tapered exit, chipped edge), never a continuous machine line.
- **Figures**: black silhouette + white knife halo + hatching on the lit edge only, under a low grazing light, so flat areas stay black. **Skin carved white, features left black** (the wordless-novel rule).
- **Close-ups (hands, objects)**: think of the form as a small lit sculpture and let the cuts follow its bands of equal light, like an engraver's lines, wide where it is bright.
- **Printing**: the cut image is printed, not shown raw: a knife burr on edges, about ±6% ink unevenness, wood-grain streaks, specks where ink didn't take, and a mottled colour plate with a small fixed misregistration. Block mode: glossy inked wood with a grain sheen, pale wood in the grooves.

## 3. Colour logic

- Two values carry everything: near-black ink and warm laid-paper white. The raw wood colour appears only in shots that show the block itself.
- **At most one colour plate**, printed only where white has been carved, so it glows out of the cuts and never sits on the black. A colour plate over ink is nearly invisible, so plan it on carved white.
- The colour is reserved for the one thing that matters (a flame, molten metal, an ember, a signal lamp). It can spread at a peak and shrink to a single highlight afterwards; it is never decoration.
- Examples of a plate: a burnt copper-orange for heat; a cold Prussian blue for water or night; a deep oxblood for a wax seal or a ripe harvest. Choose it from the topic.

## 4. Type & on-screen text

- **Narrated lines are letterpress captions in the paper margin**, below the plate, like a plate caption in a woodcut novel. They never cover the image. A letterpress old-style face (the IM Fell English kind) at about 4% of the short side, in ink, printed with the same unevenness as the plate. A caption is a print too, so it arrives almost at once: full ink within 3 frames, no slide, and gone within 4–5.
- **Titles are carved, not typed**: cut into the block (small capitals in Latin script), mirrored if the block is shown before the print is pulled.
- Any closing text is a fresh print: letterpress in the margin or carved out of a black panel.
- **The letterpress face is the look** and stays whatever the brand's typeface is; the brand's face appears only inside its logo.

## 5. Motion quality

- **Characters are stepped**: hold each drawing 2–3 frames (about 12 a second); camera, particles, fire and light move on every frame. Hatching belongs to each body part, so cuts move with the limb and don't boil; only fire and liquids are re-cut every 2–3 frames, as deliberate boil.
- **Anticipation / action / follow-through** on every key action: a few frames back → a 2–3-frame strike → settle + a short shake.
- **Carve reveal**: each stroke has its own start time and speed (0.08–0.35 s). Reveal orders: by light (bright areas first, coarse to fine), radial (from a point: rays, a pour), top-down, or any rule you write.
- **Hard cut = a new impression**: two or three frames of misregistration (a hair's width, then back).
- **Paper transition** (the one transition grammar): a brayer rolls ink (a wet highlight sweeps) → paper lays down → the baren spirals, the image soaks through → the sheet peels toward camera, heavy then fast, its back showing the mirrored show-through.
- What never moves: the paper margin.

## 6. Camera grammar

The camera moves over the paper; the margin stays fixed. The knife tip, or the brightest cut, is the focus.

| Move | What it expresses | Can serve |
|---|---|---|
| Track with the knife tip | Making; the line is the story | a first mark; a route drawn on a map; a signature |
| Continuous pull-back as cuts spread | A line becomes a world; scale | a city from one street; a family tree; how far news travels |
| Locked plate | A page of a wordless novel; reading time | a decision; a portrait; the aftermath |
| Slow macro push on a sculpted close-up | Patience, touch, labour | hands at work; a tool; a letter being read |
| Snap zoom and hold | Shock; the detail that breaks | a crack; a wrong number; a face turning |
| Frame in a frame (door, window, arch) | Distance, exclusion, waiting | a crowd outside; a sickroom; a border |
| Hard cuts on the pulse, each a new impression | Accumulation, a series of prints | gifts; days passing; a list of names |
| Crane up from a detail to the whole | Consequence | a flood reaching a village; a light reaching faces |
| Rack between two plates via the peel | One state printed over another | before/after; a promise kept; generations |

Opening and ending come from the topic. Framing: black masses on carved white or white on uncut black, never grey on grey; text only in the margin. Transitions: hard cut (new impression) or the peel; never a dissolve.

## 7. Sound palette

- **The workshop is the palette**, all made by code: knife into wood (a transient + band-passed fibre tearing tied to the knife's speed), chips falling, the brayer's tack, paper air, the baren's rasp, the wet peel.
- **Three weights by tool**: the knife is a thin scratch, the V-gouge a tear, the U-gouge a deep scoop; a stab is one dry tick. Coarse cuts first means the heavy sounds come first.
- **Objects of the story are struck, not played**: metals by size (large = low, small = high and pure), a resonant thing as one hit with echoes for distance. Withhold the sound of the story's object until it appears.
- **Ambience**: one faint bed under a locked plate (wind, a workshop's room tone, far water); never tonal.
- **Silence is a tool**: near-silence with that one bed, or true silence, for half a second before the loudest moment; the first sound after it is chosen.
- **Sound carries across plates**: the next plate's bed arrives before the hard cut, and a strike's ring runs on over the peel.
- **Hard transients sit off-centre**, on the side of the frame where the knife is.
- **Voice**: few lines, low and plain, like captions spoken; the plates tell the rest.

## 8. Native moves

A menu: use the ones your story needs.

- **Light is carved.** A burst of radial cuts from a point. *Fits content like:* a power grid switched on; a scientific insight; a lighthouse restored.
- **Carving = time.** Patient work on screen, cut by cut. *Fits content like:* a year of training; a building rising; a craft apprenticeship.
- **Mirrored block → true print.** A backwards word reads true when the sheet is pulled. *Fits content like:* a vote result; a name cleared; a misunderstanding put right.
- **Stab cuts as texture.** White dots can freeze mid-air or be inked back in until the black is clean. *Fits content like:* stars going out over a city; rain stopping; a disease receding on a map.
- **The one colour plate spreads and withdraws.** *Fits content like:* a forge at work; a sunrise over a harvest; a single lit window in a blackout.
- **A photo or sculpt re-cut** into form-following lines, planned by hand from a picture the user has the right to use. *Fits content like:* a portrait drawn from a photo; a machine part; an archive image re-cut.
- **Wordless-novel sequencing.** Self-contained plates, few or no lines; a gap can be silent. *Fits content like:* a migration; a harvest season; a life in seven plates.

## 9. Pitfalls of the medium

- **White marks on white areas are invisible** → stab cuts need dark masses behind them.
- **Hatching every surface looks like fur or rain** → grazing light; 3–6 cuts on the lit edge only.
- **Black faces with white cuts don't read small** → skin white, features black, a black keep-line around white parts.
- **Close-up props drawn as flat outlines look like mittens and blobs** → decide the light first and wrap the cuts around the form.
- **Short hatch segments with a tonal wobble turn into zig-zag chevrons** → long cuts (14–50 × their spacing), one broad highlight band, a thin rim light, a stronger halo.
- **Steam or smoke on top of the subject reads as flames** → draw it behind, so only wisps escape.
- **A threshold filter on a photo** gives blotches, not cuts → lines must follow the form.
- **Fine hatching + ink noise + grain** turn to mush after compression and at phone size → fewer, wider cuts; check a 1:1 crop of a still.
- **Print texture that changes every frame** reads as video noise → burr, grain and specks are fixed to the plate; only a new impression shifts them.

## 10. Building it here

- **A plate is a black rect with white cuts on top**: each cut is an SVG shape with a blunt entry and a tapered exit, its chipped edge from `noise(x, seed)`. Dense hatching is drawn on a `<canvas>` from the frame number; a finished plate is kept as one memoised layer.
- **Carve reveal**: every cut has a start frame and a length in `timeline.ts` and grows along itself with `prog` (a `clip-path`, or `stroke-dashoffset` for plain lines). Sort the starts by the reveal order: light, radial or top-down.
- **Printing is an SVG filter over the plate** at a fixed `seed`: `feDisplacementMap` for the burr, a stretched `feTurbulence` for grain streaks and uneven ink, a fine thresholded turbulence for specks. The paper is a warm `div` with faint laid lines.
- **The colour plate** is a mottled colour layer shown only through the cuts (`mask`), shifted by a small fixed offset and laid on with `mix-blend-mode: multiply`.
- **The block and the peel in 2.5D, with no 3D library**: the mirrored block is the plate under `scaleX(-1)` with a moving gradient for the wet sheen; the peel is a `clip-path` edge crossing the frame with a curved gradient band as the curl and a faint mirrored copy as the show-through, or a few strips turned with CSS `rotateY` under `perspective`.
- **Stepping**: characters read `Math.floor(frame / 3) * 3`; the camera, one `transform` over the paper, reads `frame`. A new impression offsets the plate for 2–3 frames.
- **Overrides**: nothing fades or springs in (cuts grow; the only spring-like motion is the shake after a strike, from `noise`); no `Finish` (ink and paper are the texture), no `Backdrop`, no `MaskRise`, and `<SceneTrack sweeps={false} />`. **Left out**: a truly lit sculpt; plan the bands of light by hand. Scenes take no `SceneFrame` entry or exit: its blurred push is the engine's default look, not this one. The timeline's `overlap` is how long the old scene stays mounted under the new one (8 frames by default): build it with `{ overlap: 0 }` for a hard cut, or give it the length of this style's own transition.
- **`Soundtrack.tsx`** reads the same start frames: coarse gouges get their own cue, fine hatching one cue per group.

## 11. In a product video

- **Black ink and paper are the look. The brand's colour is the one colour plate**, printed only in carved white and reserved for the product or its effect; it spreads at the peak and shrinks back to one highlight.
- **The plates tell the story; the product arrives as a tipped-in plate.** Carve the problem and the work, with the product as a black silhouette whose screen is carved white. For the real thing, pull a fresh sheet: a flat screenshot mounted clean inside the print frame, like a photograph tipped into a book, with its caption in the margin. Never run a screenshot through a threshold filter.
- **Numbers and claims are letterpress captions or carved numerals**, each listed in `facts.md`; anything illustrative carries `<ExampleBadge />` in the margin.
- **End card**: a fresh print: the logo carved out of a black panel, or printed in the colour plate if it works in one colour, and one letterpress line in the margin on how to get it.
- **Arabic**: captions sit right-aligned in the margin and the knife travels right to left. A carved Arabic word is cut whole, its strokes growing along the joined word, never as separate letters; no letter-spacing and no small capitals. The mirrored-block move works unchanged. A sturdy, traditional naskh suits both caption and carving.
- **9:16**: a tall block with a deep bottom margin: captions sit at the top of that margin, above the bottom 22%; carved titles below the top 12%; the brightest cut out of the right 12%.

## 12. Variation space

You decide the story, characters (or none), plates, opening, ending, camera path, pacing and what the one colour is for.

- Structures: **a day in seven plates** (seven locked plates, one per hour, no moves inside a plate); **the same block recut** (carved deeper each scene, the image changing as wood goes); **two blocks printed in turn** (two places alternate impressions until one sheet carries both).
- Openings: **the finished print on a wall** (then back into the block to see it cut); **white paper** (an inked block pressed onto it: the first image arrives whole); **wood grain** that becomes water or a field as the first cuts follow it.
- Endings: **the block cleaned** (ink washed off, pale carved wood remains); **a stack of prints** (rising over many sheets of one plate); **an uncarved corner** (holding on black wood the knife never reached).
