# Engineering Briefing Board

> One real whiteboard wall, filmed: window light on enamel, faint traces of erased marker, frame and tray. Cut-out photographs of real things land on it with weight; tags, magnets, a ruler and stamps are pinned to it; marker ink draws itself in three colours with fixed meanings. The camera rides the lines from fact to fact and only at the end pulls back to show the whole briefing.
> References (grammar only): explainers filmed on a map wall; the long pull-back over a wall covered in work; drafting conventions (plan above elevation, dimension lines, hatching). Never copy them or name them in the film.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

1. **A physical board in a room**: enamel, window falloff, a sliding reflection, faint traces of earlier work, frame and tray.
2. **Real things on it**: cut-out photographs of the real objects, a few millimetres off the board, shadowed by one window, parallaxing. They are evidence: never a generated image presented as a real object.
3. **Ink that means something**: graphite for names and rules, blue for water and measurement, red only for the decision; rejected ideas erased to faint traces.
4. **One continuous wall**: no pages; the camera travels along lines, projections and leaders.
5. **The whole wall, once**: every shot is a crop of the final briefing, seen whole only in the last pull-back, as a poster.

Not **Whiteboard Explainer** (a hand drawing line art on a flat page): the board is a filmed object, subjects are photographs. Not **Data Storytelling**: comparisons are dimension lines in real units beside real objects. Not a keynote: no headers, centred bullets, page turns.

## 2. Materials & rendering

- **Board (flat on the wall)**: enamel with faint wiped strokes, smudges, a window falloff and a sliding diagonal reflection; the frame shadows a warm grey wall.
- **Ink (multiplied onto the enamel)**: flat colour at about 90%, a lighter core, round caps, a wet-tip glint on the stroke being drawn. Lettering: a marker face revealed in writing order, with a small fixed tilt and baseline jitter per glyph or word. Erasing leaves a blurred trace (about 7%).
- **Prints (a little off the board)**: photographs cut out at full resolution, clean edges, one shared grade (lower saturation, a touch warmer, lifted blacks). Thin structure (cables, wires, masts, railings) is measured in the photo and redrawn as clean lines in one colour. Shadow = a blurred silhouette along one light vector, softer the higher the object. **No frame upsamples a photo**: at least one source pixel per screen pixel at its largest; cap the zoom or find a bigger source.
- **Secondary layer** (only where the camera never goes before the end): the photos as pinned prints, a source card, sums on sticky notes, a small sketch. Texture and proof for the final wall, lighter than the main line.
- **Props (higher off the board)**: magnetic tags, magnets, a steel ruler, printed cards, a pitted stamp impression; the tray is nearest the lens.
- **Camera**: parallax by height, blur on fast moves, light grain. Only the narrated-line strip is fixed to the screen.

## 3. Colour logic

- Ground: the enamel and its light falloff; the wall around is neutral warm grey.
- Ink: graphite = names, rules, facts; blue = water and measurement (dimensions, projection lines, measured numbers); red = the decision (a rejection, the key word of a verdict, the one mark to notice); grey for drafting only (extension lines, hatching).
- Red is rare: two or three red marks per frame at most, never on a red object. Full colour belongs to the photographs; props stay neutral; a red magnet is a verdict.

## 4. Type & on-screen text

- **Marker lettering**: a single-weight marker face covering every script the film uses. Content letters about 4–7% of the board's height; a verdict 10–17%. The marker face is the style's own and stays whatever the brand uses.
- **Printed labels** (tags, cards, stamps): a sturdy sans (a brand's own sans fits here); credit lines in a monospace. A tag holds one to three words.
- **Narrated lines**: a frosted acrylic strip at the bottom centre with a blue marker tick, the words arriving with the voice. If the film is bilingual, the film's language sits on top. **One line per language**, never wrapped; a long line becomes two. Key content stays above the strip.
- **Board text is read where the camera rests**: it stays fully in frame from when it appears until it has been read. In the final wall, text meant to be read is at least 3.5% of the frame's short side (about 38 px in either format); check it on the phone review sheet.

## 5. Motion quality

- Smooth motion on every frame; nothing is stepped.
- Camera: overlapping eased moves whose speed rises and falls like `u^a (1−u)^b` (a, b about 1.5–4), so speed and acceleration never jump; b > a gives inertia. A hand-held breath (a few pixels) underneath; shake only on an impact.
- Weight: prints are lowered from the camera side, land, bounce and settle as the shadow tightens; magnets clack on to pin them; tags swing; removed magnets pop toward the lens; struck things jolt and spring back. The camera may meet a landing close, briefly, on the object's most telling detail, then pulls out.
- Blurred in motion, sharp where words are read. **One pulse** (a unit in frames) sets the pace: every one or two pulses something lands, draws or swings; only silences and judgement holds go still.
- Ink draws at the speed of a hand; dimension lines land with an arrowhead tap.
- Never: dissolves, scale-pops on text, bouncy easing on text or interface, text fading in by itself.

## 6. Camera grammar

| Move | What it expresses | Can serve |
|---|---|---|
| Ride a line (with the pen or a dimension) | measuring; "how far does it go?" | lengths compared; a route; a timeline |
| Tilt along projection lines | the same quantity in another view | plan → elevation; a total → its parts |
| Slow push-in, slight Dutch tilt | tension, an approaching problem | a collision; a deadline; an overrun |
| Pull back and sideways | stepping back to state a rule | a constraint; a turn in the argument |
| Whip with the line, overshoot, settle | a result that exceeds | a record; an outlier |
| Rise along a leader line | from evidence to verdict | a conclusion; a recommendation |
| Staircase framing (each new item with the previous result) | comparison in sequence | rankings; tests; options |
| Judgement frame: the whole item, the limit and the verdict, held at least 2.5 s | a test the viewer can check | pass / fail against a threshold; a budget against a cap |
| Long pull-back to the whole wall | scale; everything at once | the end of an argument; a summary |

Framing: off-centre, cropped, close. The first frame holds a readable subject; the first strong action hits within half a second. Items tested against a limit each get a judgement frame (wider each time if they grow); the verdict lands after the camera settles. Before the reveal: at most about 60% of the board's width, never both side edges, never the reveal's zone. Transitions are moves; no cuts, fades or zoom-to-fit.

## 7. Sound palette

- **The board's own sounds**: felt-tip squeaks as long as their strokes, nib taps, magnet clacks, suction pops, print landings (a board thump with a paper slap), a ruler's ring, eraser felt, a stamp's thud.
- **Weights**: nib taps and tag swings are light, magnet clacks and print landings medium, the stamp the one heavy hit. Landings, stamps and arrowheads sit on the pulse.
- **What stays silent**: the camera's eased moves, the reflection, the breath. A whip may carry a short dull whoosh.
- **Ambience bed**: the room: air conditioning and traffic through the window, low but audible, not a hiss. Silences keep only this.
- **Silence**: hold the room alone before the verdict; the first sound after it is an impact or the subject's own sound.
- **Sound across a move**: the subject's own sound (an engine, water, a crowd of footsteps) arrives a few frames before the subject does.
- **Tonal accents**: magnets in a row clack at stepping pitches, one per magnet, resolving on the last. Never a tune.
- **Voice**: calm, briefing-room confident.

## 8. Native moves

- **The whole-wall reveal.** Design the wall as a poster first (hierarchy, reading path, filling the frame), then plan every shot as a crop of it; keep one zone (secondary layer, verdict, title card) for the reveal alone. *Fits:* a product roadmap; a household budget.
- **The subject passes through the answer.** The real thing moves through the chosen solution, its sound first. *Fits:* a parcel along the chosen route; a train over the new viaduct.
- **Ride the dimension line.** A quantity drawn as a real length to one scale; the camera rides the tip. *Fits:* battery ranges of four cars.
- **Projection lines.** Dashed lines carry an edge from one view to another so two drawings share a scale. *Fits:* a trail map above its elevation profile.
- **Magnets as countable things.** Units clack on one per pulse, pop off when a rule removes them. *Fits:* seats in a vote.
- **Erase to a trace.** A rejected idea wiped but faintly visible. *Fits:* last year's plan under this year's.
- **The stamp.** A verdict lands with a thud and a jolt. *Fits:* passed / failed tests.
- **The physical answer.** A real prop laid down as the solution. *Fits:* one cable instead of five.
- **Leader to the verdict.** An arrow from the deciding number to the conclusion. *Fits:* the bottleneck in a process.

## 9. Pitfalls of the medium

- **Text glimpsed in passing** is never read. Fix: words where the camera rests; frame each new item with the last result.
- **Only close-ups.** The viewer never sees the whole object against the limit. Fix: detail → judgement frame → hold → verdict → move on.
- **Photos that don't belong together.** Fix: one grade, one light vector, one viewpoint family.
- **Thin parts lost or stair-stepped** by the cut-out. Fix: cut them away and redraw clean lines at the measured positions.
- **Upsampled photos** go soft in close-ups. Fix: full-resolution cuts, large sources, a check of every close framing.
- **Lettering in photos** (signs, names, brands seen in the scene). Fix: paint out, cut, or choose another photo. A watermark or a source's own logo laid over a photo is different: never remove, crop or cover it; choose another photo.
- **The wall seen too early** spends the ending; **a sparse or tiny final wall** wastes it. Fix: know how much of the wall every framing shows; size the hierarchy for the poster; a quiet secondary layer, no empty middle.

## 10. Sources & facts

- **Photos**: first what the user uploads (their own product, site and people, with their permission), then screenshots and screen recordings of the product, then public sources: Wikimedia Commons (read each file page's licence and author), national libraries and archives (check each item's rights statement), agency media pages. Fine: CC0, public domain, CC BY (credit it). CC BY-SA, "no known restrictions" (not the same as CC0) or other terms: name them to the user and ask first. No usable photo of a real thing → draw it in marker as a sketch; never a generated image presented as the real object.
- **Facts**: every claim on screen or in the voice goes in the video's `facts.md` with its source (the address, the quoted wording, or how it was measured). List every photo there too, with source, author and licence.
- **People and private data**: no real personal data on the wall. Names, faces and contact details that are not the user's to show are replaced by fictional stand-ins with `<ExampleBadge />`.

## 11. Building it here

- **One world, one camera**: the wall is a large `div`; the frame has a CSS `perspective` and the wall is `transform-style: preserve-3d`, so each print, tag and magnet at its own small `translateZ` parallaxes for free as the wall translates and scales. No 3D library; everything faces the lens. The enamel and its ink share one flat child of the wall, so the ink's multiply has the board under it; prints and props are that child's siblings.
- **Board**: a soft gradient for the window falloff, `feTurbulence` (fixed `seed`) for smudges, pre-drawn faint strokes at about 7% opacity, and a diagonal `linear-gradient` reflection whose position follows the camera's x.
- **Ink**: SVG paths with round caps in `mix-blend-mode: multiply`, drawn on with `stroke-dashoffset` from `prog`; a small white dot rides the tip. Erasing animates opacity toward 7% with a blur.
- **Prints**: prepared cut-outs with transparency (supplied by the user, or cut here with a hand polygon in `clip-path`, or a mask image), each through the same CSS `filter` grade (on the print itself: a `filter` on a parent flattens the `translateZ` of everything inside it). The shadow is `drop-shadow(…)` whose offset and blur grow with height.
- **Motion**: this style leans on the engine's springs for objects only: `SPRING.bouncy` for a print landing or a tag swinging, `SPRING.snappy` for magnets. The camera uses `prog` with `ease.inOut`, or an asymmetric `Easing.bezier` for inertia. Never a spring on text. Fast whips get a brief directional blur; hand-held breath is `noise(frame * f, seed)`.
- **Lettering**: a clip-wipe in writing order, or spans appearing one by one with tilt from `noise(i, seed)`; not `MaskRise`.
- **Overrides**: one continuous scene, so no `SceneFrame` entries and `<SceneTrack sweeps={false} />` if scenes are used at all; no `Backdrop`; `Finish` for the light grain.
- **Timing**: camera keys and every landing live in `timeline.ts` on the pulse; `Soundtrack.tsx` puts clacks, thumps and the stamp on the same frames, and starts each squeak with its stroke.

## 12. In a product video

- **The three inks keep their meanings.** The brand's colour takes one ink role: the decision ink in place of red, if it stands clearly apart from graphite and blue; the measurement ink, if the brand is a blue. Full colour stays with the photographs and screenshots.
- **The product is a print on the wall.** Real screenshots land as pinned prints with weight and shadow; a `Device` kept flat to the board (`float={0}`, no tilt) plays a screen recording. The camera meets it close on its most telling detail. Use "the physical answer": the product laid down as the solution to what the wall has measured.
- **Dimension lines carry real numbers** in real units, to one scale, each in `facts.md`. An illustrative comparison carries `<ExampleBadge />` on the board.
- **End card**: the whole-wall reveal. The reserved zone holds the logo as a print, one marker line on how to get the product, and the single red (or brand-coloured) circle.
- **Arabic**: the wall reads right to left: the reading path starts top right, rides run leftward, the strip's tick sits on the right. Marker lettering lands **word by word**, each word whole with its own slight tilt, never letter by letter and with no letter-spacing, in an Arabic marker hand (ruq'ah-like). Wrap measured ranges with `isolateNumbers`.
- **9:16**: make the wall portrait and let the reading path zigzag downward; plan above elevation suits the tall frame. Lift the strip above the bottom 22%, keep verdicts out of the top 12% and the right 12%, and use fewer, larger items so the final wall still reads on a phone.

## 13. Variation space

You decide per film: structure, which objects are photographed, plan vs elevation, the wall's reading path, opening, ending, camera path, pacing.

- **Three structures:** one measurement that keeps growing until it breaks a limit; a before/after where the erased first plan stays as a faint trace under the second; a checklist stamped item by item as the camera moves down the wall.
- **Three openings:** a stamp on a finished drawing, then back to see why; one number written large, then the line that measures it; a photo dropped onto an empty corner.
- **Three endings:** the wall revealed with the answer circled; back to the opening mark, now corrected; the last tag flipped from a question to an answer, then the pull-back.
