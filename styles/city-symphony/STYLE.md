# Time-lapse City

> A film of a real place cut to one rising pulse: time-lapse turns traffic, crowds and clouds into rivers of light, the cuts get denser as the pulse subdivides, one thing is slowed almost to a stop, and the film ends on a hard cut to black. Few words. The cutting carries it.
> References (grammar only): the city films of the 1920s (a day in a city, told by its cutting alone) and the "Qatsi" films (Reggio / Fricke) for time-lapse as the subject, machines fast and people slow, and the long build to a stop. Nothing is taken from those films: no footage, no title lettering, no names in the film.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

- **An edit style.** The picture is real footage of a place (time-lapse, hyperlapse, aerials, slow motion, archive film, photographs). The style lives in *how time is bent* and *where the cuts fall*, not in a drawn look.
- **The pulse is the script.** It is planned first, with a clear arc: slow repetition → acceleration → climax → a sudden stop → acceleration again → hard stop → a quiet coda. Every cut sits on a pulse or a subdivision of it.
- **Machines fast, people slow.** Cars, trains, clouds and light are sped up until they become lines; the one slowed moment is something fragile (a soap bubble, steam, a flag, a flock), and people appear as crowds, tracks or long-exposure blurs.
- **Few words, each one earned.** On-screen text is a title, a handful of numbers that state the size of the place (each from an official source), an archive date, and one line from a real text at the end.
- **It is not** a travel reel (no drone showreel), a vlog, a news package, an explainer that talks all the way through, or a slideshow of postcards. No lower thirds with names, no headers, no centred title cards with page-turn transitions, no logo stings.

## 2. Materials & rendering

- Footage only (2D). Sources mixed freely: tripod time-lapse, long single-camera time-lapse (12 hours or more, sunrise and sunset in the same frame), hyperlapse, drone aerials (top-down is gold), high-frame-rate slow motion, archive film (pre-1931), photographs (a pair from the same camera position is gold), a scanned page of a historic text.
- Everything is unified into one **35 mm release-print** look: in 16:9 a 1.85:1 matte (thin black bars, about 2% of the height each), warm highlights, lifted blacks, highlight bloom, clumped mid-tone grain, a gate weave of half a pixel, a soft vignette. In 9:16 the footage fills the frame and the matte is dropped.
- Never generated images; never restored, colourised or speed-corrected archive copies; never re-uploaded mirrors of archive films.
- Scale limits: archive up to 2.5× its own size; modern footage up to 1.5×. Past that the grain turns to mush.

## 3. Colour logic

| Use | Value |
|---|---|
| Type (warm white) | `#EDE8DF` |
| Secondary line (labels, sources) | `#B0AAA0` |
| Sodium orange, the only accent (rules under numbers, source lines, crowd tracks, the day ring's hand) | `#D9772F` |
| Matte and black | `#000000` |

- **One grade for every shot**, so mixed sources read as one print: a gentle S-curve, blacks lifted to about 3%, whites held just under full, highlights a touch warm and shadows a touch cold, saturation about 0.86.
- **White balance per shot before the grade**, and only by day. Night shots keep their sodium orange and blue untouched.
- **Bloom**: the brightest areas, blurred wide and added back warm; light by day, about twice as strong at night.
- **Grain** sits heaviest in the mid-tones, lighter in deep shadow and bright sky.
- **Archive film stays black-and-white** and goes through the same grade, so it reads as part of the same print.

## 4. Type & on-screen text

- **One wide, squared technical sans in capitals** (the Michroma kind), tracked wide in Latin script. A brand's own face comes first if it is a clean sans; this style never needs a second family.
- **Aligned to the reading side**, with a side margin of about 9–10% of the width; never centred, never on a box. Legibility comes from a very soft elliptical darkening behind the block (at most 60% opacity), not a panel.
- **Number card**: the number at about 8% of the frame height in 16:9 (about 14% of the width in 9:16; half that when three numbers stack); a thin sodium rule grows under it; one line says what it counts and a smaller line gives the source and year. A census-type number locks digit by digit, one digit per quarter-pulse, with one tick per digit; a flow number counts up from 0 in 0.6 s, easing out. "About" numbers carry `≈`.
- **Archive date**: just the year, small, in the reading-side bottom corner of the archive panel.
- **Closing line**: a short fragment over the last moving shot, large, with its source line small in sodium; it fades in within a quarter of a second and holds past its reading time.
- **One line per block, never wrapped.** Text may stay up across the cuts underneath it, but only while the shots under it belong to what it counts.
- **If the film is narrated**, the few spoken lines appear as on-screen text in the same face, each word arriving as it is spoken, only at the start, in the brake and at the end.

## 5. Motion quality

- **Speed is the main instrument.** Shot speeds run from 0.3× to 75×; every speed above 1× uses **frame blending** (a fully open shutter): each output frame averages the source frames it covers. Headlights become continuous lines, crowds become fog, clouds become water.
- **Speed ramps** are smooth and always mean something: a corridor accelerates out of real time (1× → 6×), a time-lapse ramps into a frenzy (1× → 7×), a fast shot brakes into slow motion (2.2× → 0.3× in about half a second) on the frame where every sound stops.
- **Camera moves are added sparingly**: slow push-ins (1.00 → 1.05–1.10 over the shot), a falling push on the opening still (1.0 → 1.3 in a third of a second, easing out, then accelerating to 2× by the cut). No shakes, no whip pans, no zoom past the scale limits.

## 6. Camera grammar: editing

**Cut-point table.** The pulse decides. Numbers for a pulse of 12 frames at 30 fps (half = 6, third = 4, quarter = 3); a phrase is four pulses.

| Texture of the pulse | Cut length | Frames | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening hit, then slow whole pulses | 2 phrases → 1 phrase | 96 → 48 | the opening still, the first single-camera time-lapse |
| Whole pulses | 2 pulses → 1 pulse | 24 → 12 | dawn, match cuts |
| Thirds against halves | 1 pulse → ½ pulse | 12 → 6 | morning, the montage of streets |
| Quarters, everything sounding | grid cells light every 1–2 frames; cells re-cut every quarter | 1–3 | the grid |
| The ticking stops, one long hold | 3–4 s, one or two shots | 90–120 | the brake: the slowed thing, the archive split |
| Quarters and thirds, rising | ½ pulse → 4 frames → 2 frames | 6 → 4 → 2 | dusk to night, the reverse flashback |
| Everything cut dry, the tail left ringing | hard cut to black on the phrase line | none | the hard stop |

Rules:

- **Cuts land on pulses.** All cut times come from one function of phrase and pulse; shot lengths are never typed in seconds. The sound cues come from the same function.
- **One long single-camera time-lapse is the clock.** It returns three or four times (night → dawn, noon clouds, a one-second nightfall, a day/night flicker), always from the same position, so the viewer knows the time without a caption.
- **Density follows the arc.** Never cut faster than the current subdivision, and put the slowest picture where the ticking stops.
- **Shape match cuts** on the pulse: clock → clock, arch → arch, grid of streets → grid of cells. A small-to-large punch-in on the same shape is allowed.
- **The grid** is the split screen of this style: 1×2 → 2×2 → 3×3 → 4×4 in 16:9 (2×1 → 2×2 → 3×4 → 4×6 in 9:16), one stage per half phrase or phrase, the new cells lighting up in a scattered order over the previous stage within a third of a second (never back to black), thin black gutters; in the last pulse the cells re-cut on every quarter.
- **Counterpoint split**: two halves, archive on one side and today on the other, the same kind of place or shape; the gutter grows from the centre in 8 frames. Side by side in 16:9, stacked in 9:16.
- **Day/night flicker**: the same camera, a noon frame and a night frame alternating every 2 frames; or a pair of same-position photographs alternating every 2 frames. Full frame, hold it to about 0.4 s (three light-to-dark flips); a longer flicker goes in one cell of the grid.
- **Reverse flashback**: in the last phrase before the hard stop, earlier shots return in reverse order, 4 frames each, then 2 frames each.
- Transitions: hard cuts only, plus the hard cut to black. No dissolves except fades from black for the page and the end card.

## 7. Sound palette

- **The place's own materials, made by code and laid on the pulse**: a rail-joint clack, a pedestrian-signal tick, a shutter click, a tram bell struck once, a pneumatic door hiss, a lift's soft thud, a flag snapping, tyres on wet road as a short filtered rush. No recorded street sound from the footage is used.
- **Every cut gets one short sound on its frame**, so the sound thickens exactly as the cutting does: clacks on whole pulses, ticks on thirds, clicks on quarters. Three weights cover the film: light (tick), medium (clack), heavy (a low thump on the first frame of each section).
- **A bed of city air** runs underneath: wide, low filtered noise like distant traffic, never tonal, rising a little through each acceleration.
- **Speed has a sound**: a time-lapse that ramps up is followed by a rising rush that peaks on the cut out of it; nothing else is narrated by sound.
- **The brake is the first silence**: every tick and the bed stop on one frame and only a thin breath of air remains under the slowed thing.
- **The hard stop is the second**: everything is cut dry on the frame the picture goes black, and one reverb tail is left ringing over the black. That tail is the one sound carried across a cut.
- **Numbers tick**: one small bright tick per digit that locks, a short soft chime when the last one lands. No tune, ever.
- **Voice**: the style prefers none; watching should not turn into listening. If the video is narrated, keep it to a few short, calm, low lines at the start, in the brake and at the end, never over the fast cutting.

## 8. Native moves

| Move | How it is made |
|---|---|
| **Light river** | a night shot at about 40× with heavy frame blending; clouds at about 37× with a lighter blend; a one-second nightfall at 75×. |
| **Speed ramp** | key speeds with a smooth curve between them; the source time is the running sum of the speed. |
| **Brake into slow motion** | ramp 2.2× → 0.3×. Soft subjects take real slow-motion footage; thin lines (cables, rails) take a crossfade of the two nearest frames, which never tears. |
| **Crowd → tracks** (people as flow) | over a fixed-camera crowd shot, thin sodium lines follow the paths people take and fade behind them, while the plate darkens and loses colour as the tracks appear. |
| **Traffic → light** | a static or slowly drifting top-down aerial at 5× with full frame blending. |
| **Grid** | short clips in equal cells, stages growing, light-up order scattered, the last pulse re-cutting cells every quarter. |
| **Counterpoint split** | archive in its own 4:3 shape beside today, a thin gutter grown from the centre. |
| **Day/night flicker** | two short clips from the same camera (noon, night), alternating every 2 frames. |
| **Photo that moves** | a same-position photo pair alternating every 2 frames. |
| **Number lock** | one digit locks per quarter-pulse while the others roll; or an eased count-up; one tick per lock. |
| **Day ring** | a thin 24-hour ring in a bottom corner with a sodium hand and a 3-hour arc: it runs with the film's day, **freezes and dims during the brake**, and **spins backwards during the reverse flashback**. It is a graphic, not a clock reading. |
| **Reading light on a page** | a scanned page, slowly pushed in, lit from the top corner, paper tinted warm, a soft band of light growing over the quoted line. |
| **Hard stop** | picture cuts to black on the phrase line; the sound is cut dry on the same frame; only the tail remains. |

## 9. Pitfalls of the medium

- **Watermarks and slates hidden in free footage**: corner logos, opening thank-you cards, credits at the end, title text over the first shots. Look at a frame of every second of a source before choosing in-points; cut around cards and credits, and leave out any clip with a corner logo rather than crop or cover it.
- **Hidden cuts inside sources.** Hyperlapse reels hide 2–3-frame jump cuts and whip transitions; frame blending across a cut makes double images. Keep every clip's source window inside one shot.
- **Too much blending** turns noon clouds into grey mush: about 40% of the full blend looks better than all of it.
- **A long full-frame flicker** between a bright and a dark picture is unsafe for photosensitive viewers and gets videos flagged: no more than three flips in any second at full frame.
- **White balance kills night**: it washes sodium light to white. None for night shots.
- **Tracks need a fixed camera.** On a drifting aerial every building moves; use blending there instead.
- **Slowing ordinary 30 fps footage stutters**, and synthetic in-between frames tear thin lines (cables, rails, text): use the two-frame crossfade there.
- **Text over bright sky** disappears: put number cards over the dark half of the frame or add the soft darkening; never a box.
- **A slow face is not a free subject.** Do not make an identifiable ordinary person the slowed or close-up hero. Slow a thing; show people as crowds, tracks or blurs.
- **Numbers drift.** Official pages update; quote the page and the year, keep the quote in `facts.md`, and recheck before the final render. Prefer one clean figure over a range.
- **Attribution credits are long.** Each one needs author, full title and licence on the end card. Treat them as fine print and keep the end card short (4–5 s); a long card of text on black loses the audience. The film's last moving shot runs on under the card, darkened.
- **Don't end on dead black.** After the hard stop, the coda is a page (about a second), then the closing line over one last moving shot (about 4 s), then the end card over the same shot.

## 10. Footage: where it comes from

1. **The user's own material comes first**: their time-lapses, a phone hyperlapse, photographs, documents, screen recordings of the product, open data about the place. Ask for it; it arrives through Materials or `npm run copy-asset`.
2. If there is none, these public sources work. Record title, author, licence and page for every file in the video's `facts.md`:
   - **Wikimedia Commons**: modern time-lapse, aerial and slow-motion footage under CC BY or CC0, photographs dedicated to the public domain, scans of old maps and books. Check the licence on each file page. Many videos are imports from video sites: use one only when its page carries a passed licence review with a reviewer and a date, or the author's own page still shows the licence.
   - **Library of Congress**: archive films published in the US before 1931 are public domain (as of 2026; the line moves a year forward every January); "no known restrictions" alone is **not** enough, you need the publication date or another public-domain basis.
   - **NASA**: public domain; night photographs of cities from orbit make a strong opening.
   - **Internet Archive**: scanned books and documents out of copyright with a pre-1931 publication date; take the line you quote from the scan, not from a modern edition.
   - **National archives**: check each country's terms.
   - Allowed: public domain, CC0, CC BY. Not allowed: share-alike licences, "no known restrictions" without a basis, re-uploaded mirrors, unknown sources, restored or colourised archive copies.
3. **Before downloading, tell the user** what you will download, from where, under which licence, and how it will be credited.

Recipe for a place: one single-camera time-lapse of 12 hours or more (the clock); 10–15 ground time-lapses and hyperlapses (streets, stations, bridges); 2–4 aerials, one of them top-down at night; one slow-motion shot of a fragile thing; one fixed-camera crowd shot for tracks; one archive film of the same kind of place; one night photograph from above; a same-position photo pair; three official numbers; one real sentence written about the place.

## 11. Building it here

- **Footage plays through Remotion's `<OffthreadVideo>`**, muted, from the video's `public/…/video/` folder. For a fixed speed set `playbackRate`; for a ramp, add up the speed curve to get the source frame for each output frame, and mount the video in a `<Sequence from={frame}>` with `trimBefore` set to that source frame, with `playbackRate` set to the speed at that moment. `trimBefore` counts in the film's 30 fps (source seconds × `fps`), whatever frame rate the clip was shot at.
- **Frame blending**: stack 4–8 copies of the same clip, each one source step later, with opacities 1, 1/2, 1/3, 1/4… so they average evenly. The two-frame crossfade for slow motion is the same trick with two copies. A render with the film look (`--blur`) adds more; don't rely on it.
- **The grade is CSS and SVG, with no shader**: one SVG filter (`feComponentTransfer` for the curve, `feColorMatrix` for warmth and saturation) on a wrapper around all footage; bloom is a second copy with `filter: brightness() contrast() blur()` and `mix-blend-mode: screen`; `Finish` gives grain and vignette; gate weave is a half-pixel `translate` from `noise(frame)`.
- **No automatic tracking.** Crowd tracks are SVG paths traced over a fixed shot and drawn on with `stroke-dashoffset`; they show the flow, they are not a measurement, so never put a count on them.
- **Grid and split** are CSS grids of clipped videos; cells light with `opacity` steps on whole frames. Keep at most 16 videos mounted at once and mount each only while it is on screen.
- **Numbers and ring**: digits with `fontVariantNumeric: "tabular-nums"`, rolled with `noise` and locked on quarter-pulses; the day ring is two SVG arcs. Type sizes come from `useLayout()` and `p(vertical, horizontal)`.
- **Overrides**: hard cuts only, so `<SceneTrack sweeps={false} />`, no `SceneFrame` push or drop, no springs on type (short fades and linear rules), no `Backdrop`. A hard cut needs the timeline built with `{ overlap: 0 }`: the engine's default leaves the old scene under the new one for 8 frames.
- **The pulse lives in `timeline.ts`**: export the unit in frames and one `at(phrase, pulse)` helper; every cut, ramp key and cue in `Soundtrack.tsx` comes from it.

## 12. In a product video

- **Fits a product that lives in a place or in time**: a transport app, a delivery service, a prayer-times app across a day, a market, a campus, a factory. The footage is of the real place it serves; the product itself appears once or twice, not all the way through.
- **This palette is the look; the brand colour takes the sodium role** (rules, the ring's hand, tracks) if it is warm and bright enough to read over dark footage. Otherwise keep sodium and put the brand colour only on the end card.
- **The real product** appears as a screen recording or screenshot inside a `Device`, graded with the rest, held during the brake (the one calm moment) or as a cell of the grid. A day of the app's own screens, alternating every few frames from one framing, is a "photo that moves".
- **Number cards carry only real figures**, each in `facts.md` with its page and year; the closing line is the product's own line or a real quoted sentence. Sample accounts or made-up content on the product's screens carry `<ExampleBadge />`. Tracks and any drawn flow are illustration, never a statistic.
- **End card**: the logo and one line on how to get it, aligned to the reading side over the last moving shot, darkened; footage credits as fine print below.
- **Arabic**: mirror the layout (text block and day ring swap sides); words appear whole, never letter by letter, with no letter-spacing; a wide, low-contrast geometric Arabic face stands in for the squared capitals. Digit locks run in reading order of the number, left to right.
- **9:16**: crop footage to its centre (top-down aerials and traffic crop best), stack the split, turn the grid to portrait cells, and keep number cards in the middle band, out of the top 12%, bottom 22% and right 12%.

## 13. Variation space

- Place: any city, port, airport, market, campus, factory, a river over a year (the clock becomes a season).
- Length: 30–90 s; one brake at least; one hard stop always.
- Sound character: rail and signal ticks are the default; a workshop (hammer, press, saw), a harbour (rope, hull, gull-less wind) or a kitchen (knife, lid, steam) work if the cues stay dry, short and on the pulse, and the brake stays a sudden silence.
- Pulse: 10–16 frames; prefer a unit whose half, third and quarter are whole frames (12 is the easiest).
- Grid sizes and the counterpoint pair change with the material (archive against today, day against night, two cities).
- Number cards: 2–4 at most; the day ring may be dropped for structures that are not a day.
- Fixed: footage only, the frame-blended light, cuts on the pulse, the brake, few words, one closing line, the hard stop, the release-print grade.
