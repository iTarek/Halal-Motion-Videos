# Data Physicalization

> Real statistics built as real objects: every unit is a countable piece of material (a plate, a block, a coin, a bar) stacked to exact scale on a crafted base, shot like a product in a studio: soft key, long shadows, shallow depth of field, believable materials. The camera reads a number at the edge of one plate, follows the data as it grows, and pulls back until the chart is an installation.
> References (grammar only): data-sculpture and physical-visualisation practice (one unit = one object, the object is the axis); product and still-life photography (a warm key at a low angle, a cool rim, a seamless sweep); annual-report editorial design (one reading per screen, a source line, a quiet grotesque). Copy none of them; never name them in the film.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

- **The unit is an object.** One plate, block or coin equals one stated quantity, and the rule is said on screen once ("1 plate = 10 GW"). A heavier material marks every tenth unit, so the stack is its own ruler.
- **Scale is exact.** Heights, counts and areas are computed from the data; rounding is one unit at most and written in the facts. Two series compared on the same base use the same unit.
- **Studio light, real matter.** Metal catches a soft reflection, wood shows grain, acrylic lets light through; nothing glows, nothing is outlined, no fake UI.
- **Growth is assembly.** Data increases by pieces being placed, one after another, with weight and sound; it never scales or morphs.
- **The pull-back.** At least once the chart is revealed as a made thing: a dial, a table piece, a landscape of stacks.
- **Every number has a source on screen**: real published statistics (or the user's own figures), credited in frame.

Not Isometric Infographic (no icons, no flat colour coding), not Glass Product Render (the object is data), not Data Storytelling (no paper chart or inked axes), not Dark Tech Keynote (no black stage or neon), not Brick Toy (no toy proportions, no characters).

## 2. Materials & rendering

- **Pieces**: thin boxes or cylinders in their hundreds, each with a tiny random offset and turn, a value change of about 7% and a hairline gap: that is what makes a stack read as stacked, not as a bar.
- **Material per series**, chosen so two series separate in one glance at thumbnail size: brushed metal against tinted acrylic, pale wood against dark stone, copper coins against paper. A third material (brass, say) only for the ruler pieces, shared by every series.
- **Base**: a slab with real texture (wood veneer, stone) and inlaid marks: a tick ring, the series' paths, the labels of categories or years, cut into the surface at the base's own scale and lit with the scene.
- **Light**: a warm key at 20–35° with long soft shadows, one or two cool rims so metal and acrylic separate from wood at distance, soft contact shadow under every piece, shallow depth of field, a light vignette.
- **Type lives on the screen, not in the set**: readings, verdicts, the source line, narrated lines, the title card. Every reading is pinned to its object and travels with the camera.
- **Finish**: light grain (the background is pale), no lens flares, no colour fringing.

## 3. Colour logic

- The set is warm and neutral: a seamless greige sweep, the base in a natural material, the series in material colours (silver, pale teal, brass, oak). Material, not paint, separates series.
- **One accent** (signal orange, say) for the reading or verdict that matters on this screen; nothing else is accent-coloured.
- Ink is near-black at two strengths (the value, then labels at about 60%).
- No rainbow series palettes, no colour ramps on the objects, no dark mode.

Example: sweep `#C4BEB4`, walnut base, aluminium `#C8CCD1`, acrylic `#9FD3D6` deepening to teal where it is thick, brass `#D2B07A`, ink `#1C1D20`, accent `#D4521C`.

## 4. Type & on-screen text

- **Roles**: a light grotesque for numbers (the hero reading at 12–16% of the frame's short side, a stack reading at about 5%); a sans for labels and verdicts; a monospace for units, years and source lines; a serif only for the title. A brand's own sans can take numbers and labels if it has a light weight.
- **A reading** = a dot on the object, a hairline leader, a small swatch of the series' material, the category line, then the value and unit, on a translucent paper plate (about 84%). Two readings of one pair stack in one column, larger value on top.
- **The source line** (dataset, publisher, year, licence: "Data: IRENA (2026), via Our World in Data · CC BY 4.0") is in frame with the data, small, ink at about 60%, in a corner away from the narrated lines, at least from the first reading to the summary, and on the title card.
- **A verdict** sits in the upper reading-side third on its own plate in the accent colour, underlined by a growing rule; it appears only after the readings it judges have been still for 2.5 s.
- **Narrated lines**: low on the reading side, a short ink rule and a paper plate fading away from it; one line only, each word arriving as it is spoken; a long sentence becomes two lines in turn.
- A counter always ends on its real landing value; nothing is smaller than about 2% of the frame's short side.

## 5. Motion quality

- Smooth motion on every frame; the camera never stops (a creep of 2–5% on holds).
- **Cascade**: a stack assembles bottom to top in 0.5–1 s, each piece dropping a short way and easing out.
- **Feed**: one period's increase arrives piece by piece from out of frame, with a long ease-out and a turn that unwinds ("placed", not "dropped"), one piece every 2–4 frames, the counter rolling with the pieces landed.
- **A single heavy object** falls with gravity and lands with one small damped bounce, its shadow tightening.
- Readings fade in with a small rise and draw their leader; they leave by fading, never by sliding.
- Never moves: the base, a piece once landed, the ruler pieces.

## 6. Camera grammar

| Move | What it expresses | Can serve |
|---|---|---|
| Macro dolly along a stack's edge, rack focus | the unit is real and countable | the rule "1 piece = X"; a single record |
| Low lateral track along the series | time passing; growth | a decade; a ranking; a sequence of sites |
| Locked three-quarter frame on two subjects | comparison | before/after; A against B; a crossover |
| Crane with the rising top | accumulation | one year's increase; a target being reached |
| Pull-back to near top view | the whole as an object | a summary; a ratio; an ending |
| Slow arc around the base | the installation as sculpture | a title; a breath |

The subject fills at least a third of the frame height at key moments; readings live in empty background, never over the narrated lines; comparisons keep both subjects completely in frame. Transitions are camera moves inside one set (one continuous take where possible); no cuts to a chart, no wipes, no page turns.

## 7. Sound palette

- **The material's own sound is the foley**: metal plates tick bright and short, acrylic toks duller, wood knocks, coins ring briefly, a heavy puck thuds. No reverb beyond a small, dry studio.
- **A cascade is a burst** of these ticks at the landing frames, each one quieter as they get denser; a feed is one tick per piece on the frame it lands.
- **The ruler rings**: every tenth piece gets one short, clear ring, one timbre per series, each a step higher than the last, so the count is heard rising. It is a counter, not a tune.
- **Three weights**: a light tick for a piece, a ring for a ruler piece, and a low thump with a bright top for the two or three designed landings (a turn, the last object). Those are the loudest moments.
- **A bed of studio air**: thin, quiet filtered noise, never tonal. The camera's travel stays silent.
- **Silence**: for 10–18 frames before each designed landing, everything but the air drops away.
- **Sound across a cut**: there are no cuts, so sound leads the camera instead: the next stack's ticking begins before the frame reaches it, and the last ring hangs over the pull-back.
- **Voice**: calm, precise, short sentences; ruler rings stay under it.

## 8. Native moves

- **The unit close-up.** Push into the side of a stack until one piece fills the frame; bracket one piece ("= 10 GW") and one ruler piece ("= 100 GW"). *Fits content like:* tonnes of CO₂ as steel plates; a budget as banknote bricks; a population as wooden discs.
- **The paired climb.** Two series on one base, one material each, built year by year as the camera tracks; the crossover is held still with both readings before the verdict. *Fits content like:* imports against exports; renewable against fossil capacity; two companies' revenue.
- **The feed.** One period's increase arrives piece by piece onto last period's level, the counter rolling with the landings. *Fits content like:* this year's new hires on last year's team; a quarter's sales; a record harvest.
- **Chart to installation.** Pull back until the bars are a dial, a skyline or a table piece; the base's inlaid marks become its typography. *Fits content like:* twelve months as a clock; regions as a map-shaped base; product lines as a city.
- **The last object.** One single heavier piece (a brass puck, a coin, a weight) lands on the base carrying the final number engraved. *Fits content like:* a total; a ratio; a target met.

## 9. Pitfalls of the medium

- Two materials too alike (bright aluminium and clear acrylic) read as one series: give one a tint or a finish change, and check at thumbnail size.
- Long shadows and warm light turn metal brown at distance: add a cool rim and keep the base darker than the pieces.
- Thin pieces shimmer into moiré when each layer is only 2–3 px tall: vary each piece's value and offset, or choose a coarser unit so layers stay thicker.
- A reading pinned to a moving object can leave the frame: clamp it to a safe box.
- Counters that roll by time instead of by landed pieces lie; tie them to the object count.
- Rounding to whole pieces can invert close values; when two values are within one unit, say so on screen or choose a finer unit.
- Flat faces with no grain read as a bar chart again: every face needs its material (brushing, grain, a darker edge) and a contact shadow.
- Thousands of separate pieces render slowly: only stacks near the camera are built piece by piece; far ones are one striped block.

## 10. Building it here

- **There is no 3D renderer here; the set is built in 2.5D.** One wrapper with `perspective` and a `preserve-3d` scene holds the base and the stacks; the camera is that wrapper's `rotateX` / `rotateY` / `translate3d` / scale, moved with `prog` and `ease.inOut`. Keep the arc within about 30° either side: only the faces that can be seen are built.
- **A piece is two or three pre-shaded faces** (top, front edge, side edge) as `div`s or SVG rects: brushed metal is a linear gradient plus `feTurbulence` grain (fixed `seed`) stretched along the brushing; wood is turbulence stretched into grain; acrylic is a translucent fill with a brighter edge line, tinted darker where stacks overlap (plain translucency: a blend mode between the planes of one 3D scene can't be relied on). Offset, turn and value per piece come from `noise(i, seed)`.
- **Light is painted, not computed**: a warm gradient on the sweep, a cool 1 px rim on the far edges, long shadows as skewed dark shapes with `filter: blur()`, a tight contact shadow under each stack. Leave out true reflections, refraction and ambient occlusion.
- **Depth of field is blur by layer**: split the set into near, subject and far groups and animate `filter: blur()` on each; a rack focus swaps which group is sharp. A `filter` flattens the 3D inside the element it sits on, so each group is its own `perspective` wrapper with the same camera numbers, blurred as a whole.
- **Assembly**: each piece has a landing frame in a schedule; before it the piece is unmounted, on it it drops in with `prog(…, ease.out)`. The counter reads the number of landed pieces. Build at most a few hundred real pieces; a far stack is one block with `repeating-linear-gradient` for the plate lines.
- **Readings**: project the object's point through the same camera numbers to place the dot and leader; `Reveal` or a short fade for the plate; digits with `fontVariantNumeric: "tabular-nums"`; `isolateNumbers` on ranges.
- **Overrides**: one continuous scene, so no `SceneFrame` entries and `<SceneTrack sweeps={false} />`; no `Backdrop`; no springs: the last object's fall is a parabola and its one small bounce a damped rebound in closed form (`Math.abs` of a decaying sine), since a spring's overshoot would sink it into the base. Leans on `Finish` at low grain.
- **The landing schedule lives in `timeline.ts`**; `Soundtrack.tsx` reads the same schedule for ticks, rings and thumps.

## 11. Sources, licences, facts

- **Statistics for a new topic**: Our World in Data (each chart names its original source; CC BY 4.0, credit both), World Bank Open Data (CC BY 4.0), Eurostat (CC BY 4.0), US Census / BLS / EIA (public domain); UN Data, IRENA, IEA free tables and national statistics offices: check each dataset's terms.
- **Licences**: CC0, public domain and CC BY are fine with a credit. Share-alike, database licences, "no known restrictions" or "free for non-commercial use" are not CC0: name the terms to the user and ask before using them.
- **The user's own numbers** (annual report, product analytics, sales): ask for them and for the document they come from; never invent or round them into a better story. Never let generated images stand in for real data or material.
- **Every on-screen number and spoken claim goes in the video's `facts.md`**: its source page, the quoted wording or row, and how any derived value (ratio, difference, units per piece) was computed. Made-up sample data carries `<ExampleBadge />`.

## 12. In a product video

- **The brand colour is the one accent** (the reading or verdict that matters), or the tint of "our" series' acrylic when two series are compared. The warm neutral set and the material colours stay: they are the look.
- **The product's own real numbers are the data**: users, orders, hours saved, places served, each from the product's site, store listing or the user's own figures, each in `facts.md`. Choose the unit so the tallest stack is 20–60 pieces.
- **The real product appears as an object on the base**: a `Device` phone lying or standing at the set's angle with a real screenshot, lit like the pieces, with a reading pinned to it; or the screenshot as a flat inlaid plate. No floating UI.
- **The last object carries the claim**: a puck engraved with the total, landing beside the logo.
- **End card**: pull back to the installation; the logo is inlaid in the base or set on the sweep, with one line on how to get it and the source line still in frame.
- **Arabic**: readings mirror (leader and plate to the other side), time runs right to left so the lateral track does too; words appear whole, never letter by letter, with no letter-spacing; values and units keep their own direction through `isolateNumbers`; a light, even-stroke Arabic sans, which also replaces the mono.
- **9:16**: stacks are tall, which suits the frame: turn the base so the series recede in depth instead of spreading wide, put readings above and below the subject, and keep them out of the top 12%, bottom 22% and right 12%.

## 13. Variation space

Decided per film: the unit and its materials, the base and its shape, which series share it, the camera route, pacing, the last object, the accent hue within the colour logic.

- Structures: a single series rising to a record; two series and their crossover; a ranking of regions laid out as a map-shaped base.
- Openings: the macro of one piece being placed; the finished installation seen from above, then the dive to the first unit; an empty base with its inlay waiting.
- Endings: the last object landing at the centre; a slow arc around the whole installation; a single piece lifted off to show what one unit is.
