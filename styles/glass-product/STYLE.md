# Glass Product Render

> Launch-film product rendering in transparent and frosted glass: a dark-field studio, strip-light sweeps, rainbow edges, caustics, light travelling inside the object, slow exploded views that snap shut on the pulse.
> References (grammar only): premium launch films (a sweep drawing a contour in the dark, slow orbits, macro rack focus, cuts on the pulse, ultra-thin wide type); transparent-hardware adverts (internals as ornament, exploded views, black and white plus one accent); dark-field glass photography. Copy none of their silhouettes, typefaces, sounds or colour templates, and never name them in the film.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

A single hero object of **glass, metal and light** floating in an infinite black studio. Long strips *behind and beside* it turn clear glass into a thin white outline with rainbow edges; the internals show through as in a display case. Everything moves slowly and precisely; every cut, sweep and light pulse lands on the pulse.

The object is **the user's own product, or a fictional object that stands for it** (a glass slab or capsule holding a software product's screen). Never reproduce another company's product silhouette, brand name, logo, typeface or UI.

Not a white-cyclorama catalogue shot, not a wireframe HUD, not a keynote slide: material and light are the subject.

## 2. Materials & rendering

- **Studio = strip lights, not a room.** A room's windows and clutter print as messy streaks on glass. The light kit is a few long, clean sources: tall side strips behind the object (the silhouette), a wide far strip (a horizon rim), a soft top box (volume for frosted glass and metal), a weak front card, an accent source driven by the light pulses, and **the sweep**: one tall strip travelling on an arc, whose highlight crosses every surface in order.
- **Clear glass**: almost invisible in the middle, a thin bright line at its edges, a second fainter line just inside (the back face), what is behind it bent and slightly enlarged where it is thick, and **a rainbow fringe at thick edges** only.
- **Frosted glass**: milky and soft. On black it reads as dark grey plastic unless **something bright is behind or inside it**: an opaque glowing shape inside (blurred into a soft internal glow) or a bright gradient behind.
- **Metals** make transparency worth looking at: satin bands, chrome kept slightly soft, brushed surfaces, boards with gold traces. **Everything inside the glass is opaque and sharp.**
- **Light guides**: dark polished channels carrying **comet pulses** (a bright head, a fading tail, a white-hot core, the hue drifting as it ages). Idle, they are dark.
- **Background**: black, with an optional dark-grey radial lift that separates glass edges; the floor fades into it, **no horizon line**. **Floor**: black gloss, a blurred mirror image, caustics (a lens ring with its red, green and blue slightly apart, warped light filaments, rings spreading from events).
- **Finish**: shallow focus in macro, deep focus in exploded views; glow only on the brightest emitters; no grain.

## 3. Colour logic

- The world is neutral: black, greys, the white of strip reflections, the metals' own tints.
- **One accent colour family, reserved for light** (emission, caustic rings, the thin line on the caption slab), optionally drifting between two neighbouring hues with age or energy. Examples: ice blue → soft violet; amber → rose; green → cyan. Pick it from what the product does.
- Emitters that only illuminate (inside frosted glass) stay cool-white and dim.
- Rainbow fringes are allowed only at thick glass edges and in the caustic, never as a gradient on surfaces or type.

## 4. Type & on-screen text

- **Narrated lines sit on a slab of frosted glass**: a centred rounded bar that blurs and slightly brightens the picture behind it, a little cool white, a thin highlight stroke and an accent line on top. A clean grotesque (the Inter Tight kind), light weight, near-white, about 3.5–4% of the frame height in 16:9 and 4.5% of the width in 9:16, each word arriving as it is spoken.
- **Titles**: the same grotesque at its thinnest weight, very large (9–13% of the frame's short side), in Latin script with very wide tracking, centred in the upper third; a diagonal glint sweeps across the letters in step with the sweep light on the object. Small tracked capitals for kickers and credits.
- The product name and slogan are **title cards**, not narrated lines.
- A brand's own sans takes these roles if it has a light or thin weight; otherwise it stays in the logo.

## 5. Motion quality

- **Smooth on every frame, always eased**: ease-in-out for camera orbits, ease-out for parts slowing into a float, ease-in for a snap back.
- **The camera always drifts**: slow orbits or push-ins; never a locked-off frame, never handheld, except a couple of frames of micro-shake on an impact.
- **Exploded view**: parts spread along one axis (to about twice the product's size), each with a small fixed tilt and a turn that grows with its distance; a long ease-out apart → a short drifting hover → a **very short ease-in snap** on a pulse.
- Objects lift out of cradles tilted toward the camera and settle upright; lids hinge at the back and stand behind the object as a backlit halo.
- Light pulses and sweeps are events on the pulse grid, not free animation.

## 6. Camera grammar

A vocabulary, not a route. Opening and ending come from the topic.

| Move | Expresses | Can serve |
|---|---|---|
| Sweep over a dark object, slow push | Contour before content | an unknown thing; a before-state |
| Slow high three-quarter orbit | The whole object in one move | a mechanism opening; variants |
| Macro, rack focus from surface to the part inside | Nothing to hide | an ingredient; a sensor |
| Side macro orbit | Material contrast | layers; a seam |
| Exploded view, its axis about 40° to the view, slow orbit | Technical beauty, suspension | architecture; a pause before a turn |
| Straight-on front, slight push | Declaration | a single number; a name |
| Top-down macro | A pattern at full size | channels; a circuit |
| Top-down on the floor | The effect leaving the object | range; spreading energy |
| Low view under the object | Monument, weight | durability; a flagship |
| Slow pull-back into negative space | Room for type | a claim; a question |

Framing: one hero, centred or on a strong diagonal, never cropped by the frame edge in a wide. Cut only on pulses (phrase lines where possible). Product films need **no spatial continuity** between shots: each shot is its own presentation stage, so hide or move anything that doesn't serve it. Transitions are cuts on the pulse or a sweep passing through black; never dissolves or wipes.

## 7. Sound palette

- **Foley follows the material**: a glass shimmer for every sweep (thin, bright, set left or right with the strip); glass-on-metal friction for moving covers; magnetic clicks (a tiny sharp transient, a metallic ring, a low thump); soft air puffs for parts moving; glass-on-glass ticks; a high glass ding.
- **Light has a sound**: a comet pulse is a short glassy glide that rises as it travels; a caustic ring is a soft low bloom on the floor. These are the only tonal sounds, single accents on one scale, never a tune.
- **The snap is the big hit**: a cluster of clicks a few milliseconds apart with one bright glass ring over a low thump. The film's one riser peaks on it.
- **Three weights**: ticks for small parts, clicks for parts seating, the snap. Slow orbits and drifting parts are silent.
- **A bed of dark air**: a very low, soft room, never tonal, barely there.
- **Silence as a tool**: a short stretch of total silence (tails cut too) before the snap or a release; at most a reversed whoosh inside it.
- **Sound across a cut**: a sweep's shimmer begins in the black before its shot; the snap's ring hangs over the cut to the title.
- **Voice**: restrained, low, launch-like, very few very short lines. Never a product name alone (listeners mishear it): give it a verb.

## 8. Native moves

A menu: use the ones your story needs. Adapting any topic: find **the invisible thing the product does** and make it visible as light inside glass.

- **Transparency.** The inside is the hero; rack focus from the shell to the part within. *Fits content like:* a juicer's blades; a watch movement; a supplement capsule's layers.
- **Lensing.** A thick glass dome magnifies and bends what is behind it; macro through it feels like looking into a jewel. *Fits content like:* a camera module; a magnifying tool; a water filter.
- **Rainbow edge.** A sweep crossing a thick edge becomes a moving spectrum. *Fits content like:* a prism lamp; a display's colour range; a skincare bottle.
- **Light that travels inside.** Energy (charge, data, heat) runs as light through channels. *Fits content like:* a battery filling; a router's data threads; a kettle heating.
- **Caustics.** An internal pulse becomes a ring spreading over the floor. *Fits content like:* a lamp's reach; a diffuser's scent; a wireless charger's field.
- **Exploded view and snap.** Parts float apart and snap back on a pulse. *Fits content like:* a modular backpack; a pen's refill system; a repairable phone.
- **Frost to clear.** A frosted shell clears and the inside appears. *Fits content like:* a privacy feature; a fridge's contents; an honest price breakdown.

## 9. Pitfalls of the medium

- A coloured emitter inside frosted glass tints the whole object → illuminating emitters stay cool-white; colour only on the pulses.
- Idle glow under a lens dome becomes a pale blob → dark when idle.
- Frosted glass on black is black → put brightness behind or inside it.
- Mirror chrome becomes a white disc → keep chrome a little soft, with a dark band through it.
- An exploded view seen square-on to its axis is a row of edge-on lines → turn the axis about 40° to the camera.
- Pure profiles read as simple shapes (a mushroom, a pot lid) → three-quarter views, lids upright behind.
- A flat grey highlight on a concave face reads as a sticker → leave that face dark.
- Painted highlights that never move read as printed on → every highlight is revealed by the sweep or slides a little with the camera.
- An orbit past the angle the object was drawn for breaks the illusion → keep each shot inside its drawn range and cut to a new view instead.

## 10. Building it here

- **There is no 3D renderer here; the object is drawn, layer by layer.** Each shot is its own stage: an SVG (or stacked `div`s) of the object from that shot's angle, with separate layers for shell, back-face line, internals, highlights and glow. A slow orbit is a few degrees of CSS `rotateY` on a `preserve-3d` group plus parallax between the layers; cut to a new drawn view for a new angle. A `filter`, a mask, a blend mode or a `backdrop-filter` flattens the 3D inside the element it sits on: keep them on single layers or on the whole stage, never on the `preserve-3d` group itself.
- **The sweep is a mask, not a light**: draw every highlight the strips could make (edge lines, long streaks on metal) as bright shapes, and reveal them through a `mask-image: linear-gradient(…)` band that travels across the object. The same band, with `mix-blend-mode: screen`, makes the title's glint.
- **Glass**: edges are thin white strokes; the rainbow fringe is three copies of an edge stroke in red, green and blue, a pixel or two apart, with `mix-blend-mode: screen`; lensing is a slightly enlarged, offset copy of the internals inside a `clip-path` of the dome. **Frosted** parts and the caption slab use `backdrop-filter: blur() brightness()`; frost to clear animates that blur to zero.
- **Metal** is gradients: a `conic-gradient` or banded `linear-gradient`, with `feTurbulence` (fixed `seed`) stretched along the brushing.
- **Comet pulses** are a short dash on an SVG `path` moved with `stroke-dashoffset`, plus a blurred copy for glow. **Caustics** are SVG rings in three offset colours and turbulence-warped filaments on a floor plane tilted with `rotateX`; the mirror is a flipped, blurred copy of the object under a gradient mask.
- **Exploded view**: parts as flat plates on one axis in CSS 3D (`translateZ`), tilts from `noise(i, seed)`, spread with `prog(…, ease.out)` and snapped with a 4–6 frame `ease.in`. Depth of field is `filter: blur()` by layer.
- **Overrides**: no springs (eased moves only), no `Finish` grain (`grain={0}`, vignette optional), no `Backdrop` (its glow is accent-coloured and its grid drifts; the studio is plain black with a grey radial lift), no sweep at cuts (`<SceneTrack sweeps={false} />`): the style's own sweep through black is the transition. Leave out true refraction and reflections of a moving room. A hard cut needs the timeline built with `{ overlap: 0 }`: the engine's default leaves the old scene under the new one for 8 frames.
- **The pulse lives in `timeline.ts`**: one hit list for sweeps, light pulses, the snap and the cuts; `Soundtrack.tsx` places the shimmer, glides and clicks from the same list.

## 11. In a product video

- **The brand colour is the accent light**: emission, comet pulses, caustic rings, the line on the caption slab, drifting to one neighbouring hue at most. The black studio, the white strip lines and the metals stay neutral: they are the look.
- **A physical product is drawn from the user's own photos and files**, its real silhouette and parts. **A software product lives inside the glass**: a clear slab or a dark-bodied `Device` holds a real screenshot as the lit, sharp internal; frost to clear is the reveal of the real screen, and the sweep crosses it once.
- **What the product does becomes the light**: data, charge or a result travels as a comet from the action on the screen to its effect. Simulated taps go through `Device`'s `taps`, each with a click on its frame.
- **A single real number** takes the straight-on declaration shot; it and every claim are in `facts.md`. Anything illustrative on a screen carries `<ExampleBadge />`.
- **End card**: a slow pull-back into black, the logo and the thin title in the upper third with one glint, one line on how to get it on the caption slab.
- **Arabic**: titles keep the size but drop the tracking and use a light (not hairline) Arabic sans with open counters; words appear whole, never letter by letter; the glint and the sweep travel right to left.
- **9:16**: stand the object upright and run the exploded axis up the frame; the title sits in the upper third below the top 12%, the caption slab above the bottom 22%, and nothing that matters in the right 12%.

## 12. Variation space

You decide the object, its internals, the accent, the structure, the opening, the ending, the camera path and the pacing.

- Structures: **a line-up** (three variants compared by what glows inside); **a day in light** (one object through morning, noon and night, the strip kit turning around it); **material stack** (one layer removed per pulse until the core stands alone).
- Openings: **inside first** (one internal part; the shell forms around it); **caustic first** (light on the floor, followed up to the object); **silhouettes** (several dark outlines; one begins to glow).
- Endings: **floor only** (the object leaves, its caustic keeps pulsing); **cool down** (emission fades to bare strip outlines); **held macro** (no pull-back; one detail, the name small).
