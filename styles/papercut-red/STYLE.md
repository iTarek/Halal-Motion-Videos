# Red Paper-cut

> Chinese window-flower paper-cuts brought to life: jointed red cut-outs on paper grounds, where the cut *is* the drawing and the holes are where the light gets in.
> References (grammar only): folk window flowers of Shaanxi and Yuxian (sawtooth, crescent, cloud and swirl cuts, fold-symmetric rosettes, positive and negative cutting); the 1958–59 Shanghai Animation Film Studio cut-paper shorts (jointed figures, profile acting, paper layers in parallax). Copy no studio character, no named artist's work and no real motif sheet: generate every pattern from code, and never name the references in the film.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

Every visible thing is **a piece of paper that was cut with scissors** and laid flat on another sheet. There is no painting, no gradient shading, no outline stroke: detail, volume and expression come only from **what was cut away**. Red paper is the hero material; grounds are paper too (a light paper by day, a deep dyed paper by night). Characters are **jointed cut-outs** (head, torso, upper and lower limbs pinned at rivets) animated in hard steps, about 12 poses a second.

Not shadow puppetry: no light behind a screen, no translucent dyed hide, no rods. Here the paper is **front-lit and opaque**; backlight is a special event you save for the moment that needs it. Not flat vector art (no strokes, no gradients), not a silhouette film (every shape's interior is cut).

## 2. Materials & rendering

- **Paper grain:** a neutral-grey texture (dye mottling, fibres, specks) blended softly inside each piece and clipped to its shape, so holes stay holes.
- **Cut edge:** every silhouette is a many-pointed outline with a slight slow wobble (hand-cut), a hairline light rim upper-left (about 0.1% of the frame's short side) and a dark rim lower-right (the cut face). Architecture and machines use straight segments plus wobble, never smoothing.
- **Paper thickness:** each piece casts a tight soft shadow on the layer below; stacked folded paper shows 2–6 offset darker edges.
- **Figures: red block + cut lines.** Every figure piece gets an **inset contour** (a thin slit just inside the outline): the classic double outline, which also separates red-on-red overlaps (an arm over a coat).
- **Folk cutting vocabulary:** sawtooth rows (fur, grass, tiles, fringe), serrated edges, crescents (scales, quilting), swirls (joints), clouds, rosettes, plum blossom, coin, tapered slits. Keep to plants, clouds, geometry and the story's own objects: the tradition's luck charms, guardian figures and zodiac omens stay out.
- **No floating islands.** A closed ring cut would drop its centre out of real paper. Eyes are two crescents (upper and lower lid) that leave bridges, plus a pupil *hole*.
- Faces never morph: expressions are **swappable face pieces** (smile, surprise, worry, determined, asleep).
- **The rosette (团花)** is designed as one 45° wedge and mirrored eight times; other folds give two, four or six. Alternate rings of positive cut (the band cut away, red lines left) and negative cut (lines cut out of a red field). Fold seams leave faint crease lines: keep them, they are true to the craft.
- **Light:** front-lit scenes use the paper as is. Night: the whole frame darkened except warm pools, then **backlit pieces** on top (light × the paper's translucency; holes at full light), a soft bloom and optional rays. Keep bloom low enough that the pattern stays legible at the centre.
- **Projected pattern:** a piece's holes, squashed flat onto the ground and added as warm light; on a character, limited to the character's shape over a solid warm spot, so cut features still read as holes.

## 3. Colour logic

- **China red** (around `#d2201f`, range `#c8102e`–`#d7261e`) is the hero material. Depth and role are shown by **paper tones one step apart**, not by shading: near pieces one step brighter, far pieces one step darker, a rival or a heavy object one step deeper.
- **Ground and hero must be different paper families.** If background and hero are both red, the silhouettes stop popping. At night, all background layers are one dark dyed family and only the pieces that matter to the story are red; by day, a light paper ground with red and pale-red layers.
- **Gold foil** is an accent kept for one late moment (a sun, a title, a coin); never a second main colour.
- Examples of ground families: indigo night papers (far layers lighter blue, near darker); a warm rice-paper day with pale-red far hills; a jade-green or black paper for a story that isn't festive. Choose from the topic.

## 4. Type & on-screen text

- **Narrated lines are a paper strip in the world**, e.g. a red banner (横批) darker than the picture's reds so cream text holds, serrated edges, swallow-tail ends; a sturdy serif (the kind of Fraunces SemiBold) at about 4% of the frame's short side, low, with a paper shadow. It drops in on two steps, like a banner being pasted, as its line is spoken; two banners never overlap.
- **Titles** are cut-out letters (red left standing, counters real holes) popping in one by one on the steps, with sawtooth border strips and a seal cut by code.
- **Faces must survive scissors**: sturdy strokes, open counters. A brand's own face can be cut if it has them; a hairline face cannot.
- Small credits may be printed ink on paper.

## 5. Motion quality

- **Characters step**: each pose holds 2–3 frames (about 12 poses a second). **Camera, light, weather, flames and flying pieces move on every frame.**
- Rigs pin pieces at rivets (neck, shoulders, elbows, hips, knees, jaws, tails). Hard poses, tiny eases, one-step overshoots (a "pop" is 1.15× for one step). Weight comes from **size and camera shake**.
- In profile, a raised near arm crosses the face: keep hands forward of the face or let a prop cover them.
- **Folding:** the flap swings up about its crease, getting darker as it stands, showing a lighter back colour past upright, with a growing shadow and a crease left behind. About half a second per fold, each on a hit.
- **Cutting:** the pattern is revealed along the scissor path; the offcut drops once its outer edge is passed; blades open and close on a division of the pulse; scraps flutter out and land.
- **Unfolding:** successive unfolds a fraction of a second apart, each flap swinging from folded to flat with an ease-out (the "pa!" snap), then a small scale bump and a warm flash on the accent.
- **One pulse** (say 15 frames) sets the folds, the snips and the unfolds; steps are its subdivisions.

## 6. Camera grammar

A vocabulary, not a route. Opening and ending come from the topic.

| Move | What it expresses | Can serve |
|---|---|---|
| Extreme close-up tracking the scissor tip | making; attention to craft | a beginning of work; a precise act; a signature |
| Flat front view, locked | the cut-out as an emblem | a title; a statement; a family portrait |
| Slow pan across paper layers (parallax) | a world laid out | a village, a route, a timeline |
| Low wide, a shape standing up from the ground layer | scale, a challenge | a storm; a rival; a deadline arriving |
| Top-down tabletop | the craft as plot | folding, cutting, assembling, sorting |
| Push toward a lit piece, pull back to many | one act spreading | influence; a network lighting up |
| Page turn / sheet peel | time or place changing | day to night; before and after; chapters |
| Pull back to an object in a room | the film was a thing someone made | memory; a gift; a tradition |
| Rotation around a rosette's centre | order, repetition | a cycle; a team |

Framing: layers stay parallel to the screen; depth comes from parallax and paper tones, never from perspective. Transitions are paper actions (fold, page turn, peel, a sheet flying off, a cut through the frame); no dissolves.

## 7. Sound palette

- **Foley follows the material:** scissors are a bright metallic shear, a short ring and a fibre crack; folds are a swish and a crease tap; an unfold is a sharp snap; heavy steps are a *cardboard* thud, not flesh; a paper peel; pasting is a palm pat; a match strike and a puff; firecrackers are dense clicks with low booms.
- **Every cut, fold, unfold and step has its sound on its own step.** Parallax pans, weather and light changes stay silent.
- **Three weights**: a snip, a crease tap, a cardboard thud. One big, deep hit is saved for one accent in the film.
- **Tonal accents, never a tune**: a run of small ticks rising in pitch, one per thing that appears; a low, soft thump repeating slowly for weight.
- **Bed**: paper hush only (a quiet room over the tabletop, a little wind outdoors), without pitch.
- **Silence**: everything stops for a held breath before the big unfold.
- **Sound across a cut**: the swish of a page turn or a peel starts a few frames before the sheet moves and its pat lands in the new scene.
- **Voice:** a warm storyteller, few lines; keep impacts out of the moments the voice is speaking.

## 8. Native moves

A menu: use the ones your story needs.

- **Fold – cut – unfold.** Fold a sheet two, four or eight times; one cut becomes many. Put the unfold on a strong pulse. *Fits content like:* one teacher, many students; a single design mass-produced; one seed becoming a field.
- **Holes are light.** A cut-out lit from behind glows deep red, every hole glows, and the pattern is projected onto the world. *Fits content like:* an idea shining through a person; a lighthouse; a window at night in a story about home.
- **Symmetry replicates.** A mirrored motif peels off and multiplies (rosette → window flowers → fireworks). *Fits content like:* a franchise; cell division; a habit spreading through a town.
- **Scraps have a future.** The bits that fall while cutting come back later (as snow, confetti, stars). *Fits content like:* recycling; leftover time; small failures that add up.
- **Everything is one flat sheet.** Things share the same cuts, so a ridge can stand up and be a creature, or a wave become a road. *Fits content like:* a landscape that becomes a map; a factory that turns out to be one machine; a zoom out to the made object.
- **Mirrored pair.** Two figures cut from one folded sheet. *Fits content like:* twins; a partnership; before/after of the same person.

## 9. Pitfalls of the medium

- **Red on red** reads as one blob → different ground family, near/far paper tones, inset contours for overlaps.
- **Darkening a layer with the night's light layer** also darkens what covers it → tint the layer's own paper.
- **A flap that only gets narrower reads as a sliding card**, not a fold → hinge it with perspective, darken it as it stands up, show the back colour.
- **Cut reveal shows the uncut stack through the holes** → stack thickness inside the "uncut" part only; the cut part gets its own offset copies.
- Scissors pointing backwards → the blade axis follows the direction of travel, the rivet a little behind the cutting point.
- Blank frames between shots → overlap outgoing and incoming paper actions.
- Grain or edge wobble that changes every frame turns paper into boiling ink → the texture and the wobble are fixed per piece; only poses step.

## 10. Building it here

- **Every piece is an SVG path** with its holes cut by `fill-rule: evenodd`; build the outline's wobble once with `noise(i, seed)`, never per frame. One SVG filter gives each piece its grain (`feTurbulence`, fixed `seed`, composited inside the shape), its light and dark rims (`feOffset` + `feComposite`) and its tight shadow (`feDropShadow`).
- **Rigs** are nested `<g>` elements rotating about rivet points. Step them with `const step = Math.floor(frame * 12 / fps)` and read poses from `step`; camera, light and flying scraps read the real frame.
- **The rosette** is one wedge defined once and placed eight times with `<use>` (rotated and mirrored).
- **Folds are the one place for CSS 3D**: the flap is a `div` with `transform-origin` on the crease and `rotateX`/`rotateY` under `perspective`, two faces with `backface-visibility: hidden`, a dark overlay whose opacity follows the angle.
- **Cutting** is an SVG `<mask>` holding a thick round stroke that grows along the scissor path (`stroke-dashoffset`); the offcut is its own piece that drops.
- **Night and backlight**: a dark layer with `radial-gradient` pools on `mix-blend-mode: multiply`; backlit pieces above it over a warm gradient, a blurred copy on `screen` for bloom; the projected pattern is the hole shapes with `scaleY` on the ground, on `screen`.
- **Overrides**: no springs (hard poses; `ease.out` for unfolds, a one-step scale for pops), no `Finish` grain (the fibres are the texture), no `MaskRise`, no `Backdrop`; `<SceneTrack sweeps={false} />` with paper actions as the transitions. Scenes take no `SceneFrame` entry or exit: its blurred push is the engine's default look, not this one. The timeline's `overlap` is how long the old scene stays mounted under the new one (8 frames by default): build it with `{ overlap: 0 }` for a hard cut, or give it the length of this style's own transition.
- **The pulse lives in `timeline.ts`**; `Soundtrack.tsx` puts each snip, crease and snap on the frame its step begins.

## 11. In a product video

- **Red is the look.** A brand that is red owns the hero paper; any other brand colour dyes the ground family, or takes the gold foil's place for the one late moment. Hero and ground still come from different paper families.
- **The real screen is a printed sheet pasted into the world**: flat, with a cut edge and a paper shadow, pressed down with a palm pat. Around it, the phone is a red cut-out whose screen is a hole; at night that hole is backlit and the real screen glows through it (holes are light).
- **A physical product is cut from red paper** in its true silhouette, its details as slits and holes.
- **Numbers are cut-out numerals** from a sturdy face, popping in on a step; each one is in `facts.md`. Sample data on a printed sheet carries `<ExampleBadge />`.
- **End card**: an unfolded rosette around the logo, the logo itself a printed or gold-foil piece in its exact shape (never recut by hand), and a banner with one line on where to get it.
- **Arabic**: banners unroll from the right and pans travel right to left. Cut each word as one whole piece that pops in at once, never letter by letter and never letter-spaced; dots are small pieces pasted in place. Choose a bold Kufi or a heavy Naskh display face whose joins are thick enough to cut.
- **9:16**: stack the paper layers (far hills high, near ground low) and keep the tabletop top-down, which already fits. The rosette sits in the middle square; the banner goes above the bottom 22%, and titles stay out of the top 12% and the right 12%.

## 12. Variation space

You decide the structure, the cast (or none), the sets, the paper families, the opening, the ending, the camera path, the pacing and which native moves carry the story.

- Structures: **a year of twelve cuts** (a calendar wheel, one wedge per month, rotating); **an assembly line** (a product built piece by piece from cut parts, each part a pattern); **a letter** (a folded message unfolds panel by panel, each panel a scene).
- Openings: **a finished pattern** (a full rosette, then one piece falls out and walks away); **an empty ground** (a blank sheet, one fold crease appears); **a crowd of cut figures** already busy at a market, parallax full.
- Endings: **refold** (the world folds back into a small square that is handed to someone); **scraps settle** (every offcut drifts down into a new pattern); **backlit only** (the lights go off except behind one cut piece, which projects the last image on the wall).
