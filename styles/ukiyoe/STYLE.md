# Ukiyo-e

> Edo-period woodblock landscape prints brought to life: flat colour blocks, carved ink lines, bokashi skies, paper you can feel, and a camera that moves over the print like an unrolling handscroll.
> References (grammar only): Hokusai's *Thirty-six Views* (one constant subject placed differently each time; bold geometric cropping); Hiroshige's *Fifty-three Stations* and *Hundred Famous Views* (small figures, rain lines, extreme foreground framing); animation that keeps print texture alive in motion (flat layers sliding, seals as screen language). Never reproduce a specific famous print (no copy of *The Great Wave*, *Red Fuji*, *Sudden Shower over Ōhashi*), and never copy their characters or compositions.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

Every shot is **a complete woodblock print**: a sheet of washi, a thin double ink border, flat areas of colour printed from separate blocks, a black key-block line on top, a title cartouche and a red seal. The world is flat on purpose. Depth comes from stacked horizontal layers, mist bands and bold cropping, never from perspective rendering, lighting or shading. The subjects are the landscape tradition: roads, bridges, harbours, fields, weather, work and travel.

What makes it read as *printed* rather than *vector*:
- **Paper** is always visible: warm washi, long fibres, slight edge ageing.
- **Each colour is its own block**, slightly **out of register** with the key line (about 0.15–0.25% of the frame's short side).
- Large colour areas show **woodgrain** and **baren rubbing** (uneven ink density, circular streaks) and fine **goma-zuri** specks.
- **Bokashi**, hand-wiped gradients, in skies and water.

Not a flat vector illustration with a paper overlay (no woodgrain, no misregistration), not Chinese ink wash (no bleeding washes, colour is printed, not painted), not anime cel shading (no light and shadow on forms).

## 2. Materials & rendering

- **The sheet is the frame**: the print fills the screen at zoom 1, inside a thick + thin ink border, with a paper margin beyond. In 16:9 the sheet is a landscape print; in 9:16 it is the tall vertical sheet of the classic view series. Sheets can sit on a long mounting scroll (darker tan paper, a roller at one end), so the camera can travel from print to print.
- **Blocks**: each colour group is its own layer (key lines; blues; greens / secondary; reds / figures), laid down with a small fixed registration offset, a different direction per block.
- **Texture every colour block**: woodgrain (strongest in sky and water) → baren streaks → goma-zuri specks, the last two knocked out of the ink so paper shows; the key block gets specks only; light paper fibres over the finished sheet. Big moving fills get the same textures inside their shape. Woodgrain is the contour lines of a warped noise field; baren is blurred circular arcs and zigzag streaks.
- **Bokashi** is one continuous gradient whose top edge wanders gently along its length (by 2–3% of the sheet's height). Never built from strips: strips leave seams.
- **Line**: key lines about 0.25% of the short side; large contours are variable-width filled strokes, tapered at both ends, their width wavering by 20–35%. Distant things get a grey key line or none.
- **Motifs of the medium**: mist as long rounded flat bands (suyari-gasumi) fading at both ends; pines with flat-topped **needle pads**; fields as high, flat, parallel layers whose cross lines are **parallel and slanted**, never converging; rain as two sets of fine straight lines at slightly different angles under a grey bokashi sky; water as banded blues with bokashi between bands, foam and spray left as paper.
- **Seal**: a mark cut out of red, with chipped edges. **Figures** are small in the landscape (roughly 7–12% of the sheet's height), flat, modestly dressed, with a clear silhouette; faces in a few strokes.

## 3. Colour logic

- **A pigment set, not a colour wheel**: 6–9 flat hues, each printed from its own block, over the paper ground. No gradients except bokashi, no shading on forms.
- **Sumi key line** over everything; **paper is the white** (no white pigment for foam, snow, mist, blank sky).
- **One dominant hue family** carries sky and water (in the 19th-century prints, Prussian blue); **red is rare**: seals, a sun, one garment or tab.
- Far layers pale and low-contrast, near layers saturated with dark key lines.
- Examples, one per film: an indigo-and-beni landscape (Prussian blue, pale blue, beni red, ochre, greens, sumi); a quiet interior (pale pink, mauve, grey-green, lacquer black, a mica-grey ground); an autumn greeting print (persimmon, ochre, grey-green, deep brown, one blue).

## 4. Type & on-screen text

- **A narrated line is a horizontal cartouche**: a cream slip with a thick + thin ink border and a small red seal at one end, set in a bookish serif with a brush-like stress, in sumi, near the bottom. It "prints" in with the reveal mask as the line is spoken and fades out. It covers about the bottom 14% of the sheet: keep ground lines and figures above it.
- **View cartouche**: a cream slip with the same border, a pale tab with the series title, the view title below in a brush-lettered face, a seal beside: the "full stop" of each view. The classic slip is vertical; set Latin words on it only as a short title turned a quarter turn, or use a horizontal slip.
- Titles live on the print as cartouches and seals, never as floating type.
- The brush face is part of the look and stays for view titles; a brand's own typeface can set the narrated slip if it is a calm serif or a plain sans.

## 5. Motion quality

- Prints are still. **Most of the frame does not move**; each view has one or two small actions (steam, rain, a lamp swaying, a falling leaf).
- **Figures and nature are stepped**: hold each drawing 4 frames (about 8 a second); rain and fast water 2–3 frames (about 12 a second). **The camera moves on every frame.** A stepped camera judders.
- **Printing reveal**: each block appears over about 0.3 s with a diagonal baren sweep with a noisy edge, sliding the last 0.5% of the short side into registration as it lands (pure noise looks like mould).
- Cartouches drop in (fade + a short slide); a seal *stamps* (scale about 1.35 → 1 in 0.2 s).
- A native move needs a **hold of about a second** to be read; a peak flashed in under a second reads as noise.

## 6. Camera grammar

The camera moves *over* prints and between them; it never rotates in 3D space inside one. Opening and ending come from the topic.

| Move | What it expresses | Can serve |
|---|---|---|
| Locked frame on one sheet | Reading a print on a wall | a place; a portrait of a trade; a still moment |
| Handscroll slide, right → left through the mounting gap (shorter slides = urgency) | Travel, the next station | a journey; a sequence of steps; a race |
| Very slow push toward a framing device (a window, a fan, a gate) | Attention narrowing | discovery; homesickness; a detail that matters |
| Tilt past the border into the mount | Something larger than the print | a flood; a storm; a runaway success |
| Pull-back from one sheet to many | The series seen whole | a summary; a timeline; a collection |
| Crash-zoom into the texture (grain, specks, fibres) | The medium itself becomes the image | a daydream; a breakdown; a memory fading |
| Pan across a triptych (three sheets side by side) | One scene too wide for one sheet | a procession; a harbour; a market day |
| Hard cut to a blank sheet | Ma, a held breath | aftermath; a pause before a decision |

Framing: bold cropping and extreme foreground elements (a branch, a post, a lamp crossing the frame) are native; depth by stacking, never by perspective. Allowed transitions: scroll slides, printing reveals, a wash back to paper, a cut to blank. No cross-dissolves, no 3D flips.

## 7. Sound palette

- **Paper**: the baren rub under every printing reveal (band-passed noise with a slow circular wobble), a sheet laid on the block, the scroll sliding on its roller, the seal as a low thump with a tacky peel.
- **Wood**: a block set down, hollow steps on a bridge, one dry clack of wood on wood for a hard cut.
- **Three weights**: a paper tick for a cartouche, a soft thud as each colour block lands in register, the seal as the heaviest sound and the full stop of each view. Most of the frame is still, so most of the film is quiet.
- **A season's ambience** under each view: rain as a fine hiss, wind in pines, water, the birds and insects of the season. Quiet, never tonal.
- **Silence as ma**: on a blank sheet, cut everything, tails too, for half a second or more. Use it once before the main view prints.
- **Sound across a slide**: the scroll starts to move in sound a few frames before the picture does, and the rain of one print runs on across the mounting gap into the next.
- **One tonal accent** at most: a glass wind chime, a single note left to ring. Never a tune.
- **Voice**: calm, few short lines, like a poem written beside the print.

## 8. Native moves

A menu: use the ones your story needs.

- **A series of views.** One constant subject in different compositions; the story is told by *where it sits in the frame* each time. *Fits content like:* one building through a century; a product in five homes; the same street in four seasons.
- **The handscroll.** Scenes don't cut; the camera slides right to left and the next print arrives. *Fits content like:* a supply chain; the stops along a coast road; the steps of a recipe.
- **Printing, block by block.** Key line first, then each colour lands slightly off and snaps into register. *Fits content like:* a building going up; an idea taking shape; a character being "made" for the story.
- **Paper is the white.** Foam, snow, mist and blank sky are unprinted paper; white can flood the frame and wash the print back to a blank sheet. *Fits content like:* a snowfall that covers a town; forgetting; a clean start.
- **The frame is an object.** Border, cartouche and seal are physical; something big enough breaks out of the border into the mount. *Fits content like:* a harvest too big for the sheet; a tide rising past the border; a runaway success.
- **Alternate impressions.** The same key block reprinted with different colour blocks (day → night, spring → winter). *Fits content like:* before and after; a city lit at night; one house in two seasons.
- **The triptych.** A scene split across three sheets that join. *Fits content like:* three generations; a panorama of a harbour; a debate between three voices.

## 9. Pitfalls of the medium

- Bokashi built from strips leaves seams → one gradient with a wandering edge.
- Too much speckle or fibre makes dark areas look like sandpaper or scratched film → specks at about 0.3 opacity, fibres at about 0.09.
- A perspective grid for fields looks digital → parallel, slanted, flat layering.
- Pine clusters drawn as half-discs look like parasols → flat pads with needle fans.
- Offset curves self-intersect at tight bends → build inner lines from the gentle side of the contour.
- Breaking out of the print must escape **both** the sheet's clip and the border's clip.
- A seal multiplied onto a dark ground vanishes → print it opaque there.
- Texture that crawls every frame reads as video noise → textures are fixed to the sheet and never animate.

## 10. Building it here

- **A print is a stack of SVG layers**, one per block, over a paper `div`; each layer carries its registration offset as a `translate` and prints with `mix-blend-mode: multiply`. Still layers are memoised, so only the one or two small actions redraw each frame.
- **Texture with SVG filters at a fixed `seed`**: woodgrain from `feTurbulence` stretched along one axis and thresholded with `feColorMatrix`, specks from a fine thresholded turbulence, both used as a `mask` that knocks ink out; fibres as a faint stretched turbulence on top.
- **Bokashi** is a `linear-gradient` inside the block's shape, its edge pushed around by `feDisplacementMap`. **Carved lines** are filled paths whose width comes from `noise(x, seed)`, tapered at both ends.
- **Printing reveal**: a diagonal `mask-image` gradient sweeps across the block, its edge roughened by a displacement filter, while the block slides into register with `prog` and `ease.out`. Text prints in with the same mask.
- **Stepping**: figures read `Math.floor(frame / 4) * 4`, rain `Math.floor(frame / 2) * 2`; the camera, one `transform` on the mount, reads `frame`.
- **Overrides**: no springs (short eases and steps only), no `Finish` (the paper is the grain), no `Backdrop`, no `MaskRise`, and `<SceneTrack sweeps={false} />`: the scroll slide is the transition. Nothing here is 3D. Scenes take no `SceneFrame` entry or exit: its blurred push is the engine's default look, not this one. The timeline's `overlap` is how long the old scene stays mounted under the new one (8 frames by default): build it with `{ overlap: 0 }` for a hard cut, or give it the length of this style's own transition.
- **Each view's reveal frames live in `timeline.ts`**; `Soundtrack.tsx` lands the rub, the thuds and the seal on them.

## 11. In a product video

- **The brand's colour joins the pigment set as one block**: as the dominant sky-and-water family if it is a calm hue, or as the rare red role (the seal, one tab, one garment) if it is a strong one. The pigment set and the paper are the look; print the brand colour flat, with grain, and add no second accent.
- **The product is the constant subject of a series of views**: redrawn as a flat key-line object, placed differently in each print. Its real screen appears as a slip on the sheet: a flat screenshot inside the thick + thin ink border (no gloss, no tilt, no texture over it), revealed with the printing sweep and read in a locked frame.
- **Real numbers and claims go on cartouche tabs**, each one listed in `facts.md`; a drawn scene that implies data carries `<ExampleBadge />`.
- **End card**: a blank sheet, the logo stamped as the seal, the product's name on a view cartouche and one horizontal slip saying where to get it.
- **Arabic**: the handscroll already runs right to left, so the journey keeps its direction. Use horizontal cartouches only; words print whole, never letter by letter or stacked, with no letter-spacing. A brush-written naskh for view titles and a clear naskh for narrated slips suit the print.
- **9:16**: the tall sheet; a triptych becomes three sheets panned one by one. Keep the narrated slip above the bottom 22%, the view cartouche below the top 12%, and seal and titles out of the right 12%.

## 12. Variation space

You decide the constant subject (or none), the number of views, the characters, the opening, the ending, the camera path, the pacing and the pigment set.

- Structures: **alternate impressions** (one key block of a single street, reprinted for dawn, rain, market night and snow); **a triptych unfolding** (three sheets revealed one at a time, the story only readable when all three join); **a print being made** (the carver's blocks, one per shot, each block's colour telling one chapter).
- Openings: **a close crop** (an extreme foreground detail, a lamp or a sleeve, fills the sheet; the pull-back finds the print); **the bare block** (carved wood inked, the paper laid on, the baren rubs; lifted to reveal the first print); **the mount first** (a closed scroll unrolls to its first sheet).
- Endings: **a seal alone** (the print fades back to paper, only the red seal remains); **the album closes** (sheets stack like pages, the cover lands); **the last print left unfinished** (key line only, colour blocks never land).
