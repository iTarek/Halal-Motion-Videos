# Broadcast News Package

> A television newsroom's on-air package, built entirely in code: a sting that becomes the channel bug, a rundown of today's stories that opens and closes the bulletin, a lower-third strap that never leaves the bottom of the frame, and full-frame graphics (split-flap price boards, a line breaking a threshold, explainer maps, documents flying out of a city, quote cards, spec cards with proportion bars, stamps) that carry the facts. The style is the **package system plus its cutting rules**, not a drawing style. A clean frame: no news crawl, no caption bar.
> References (grammar only): evening network news headline menus, business-channel data graphics, European news channels' explainer maps, tech-desk spec cards. Never copy a broadcaster's logo, set, fonts, colours, sounds or idents, and never name them in the film.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

1. **One package system, used consistently.** Every graphic is built from the same few parts: a brand plate (parallelogram), a tab in the accent colour, a paper strap, ink cards with an accent top edge, mono datelines, stamps. Viewers read the system before they read the words.
2. **The bulletin's order.** Sting → rundown ("today we have four stories") → item → item → … → back to the rundown, ticked off → sign-off. Each item opens with its category on the strap's tab and its date on the first graphic.
3. **One fact per screen.** A full-frame graphic states one thing: the price, the break, the two rivals, the place, the two letters, the quote, the verdict.
4. **The strap holds the headline.** One lower-third strap is on screen from the first story to the recap and changes its words with a vertical flip; the picture above it is free for the graphic.
5. **Clean panel.** No crawl and no caption bar: the strap's headline and the graphic's own words are the on-screen text. The frame is the graphic, the strap, the bug and a source line.

Not **Caption News** (that is captions-first social video: huge slammed captions are the lead, no rundown, no strap; here type is small and orderly and the graphics lead). Not **Data Storytelling** (one chart per moment, never a dashboard; the chart is an insert in a bulletin). Not a keynote: no centred title-and-bullets, no page turns. Not a studio show: no virtual set, no anchor on screen, no fly-throughs; the package is flat graphics over ink.

## 2. Frame and layout

| Zone | 16:9 | 9:16 | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| Content | 5–95% of the width, 5–78% of the height | 6–88% of the width, 14–64% of the height | the full-frame graphic; when a strap is up, nothing important below it |
| Lower-third strap | tab top at 79% of the height; strap from 84%, 65% of the width and 10% of the height, plus an ink end block | tab at 66%; strap from 70% to 78% of the height, the full safe width | category tab (accent, ink text), headline, one smaller second line |
| Source line | beside the strap, flush to the far margin | between the content and the tab, flush to the far margin | `SOURCE: …` in small mono at 75% opacity; short enough never to touch the strap |
| Channel bug | top corner on the far side, a third of the sting plate's size | the same, just under the top 12% and clear of the right 12% | the plate plus the channel's name |
| Corner tag | top corner on the reading side, about 17% of the width | the same, under the top 12% | `ILLUSTRATION` on a mood picture, `SCHEMATIC` on a schematic chart; flips in and out in 6 frames |

Content sits low, close to the strap, so the strap does not leave an empty band above it. When a zone is not used, re-lay the page; never leave the space it used.

## 3. Package parts (specs)

| Part | Spec | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| Sting | 45 frames | two accent slashes (frames 1 and 3) → the plate slams in with an overshoot, an impact ring and shake (frames 5–13) → letters drop in one by one → wordmark and dateline open from the centre → a glint → the plate flies to the top corner and **becomes the bug** while a vertical band wipes into the first shot. A hit within the first 12 frames |
| Brand plate | a parallelogram about three times as wide as it is high, accent gradient, ink and gold bars at the leading end, name in a heavy condensed face | the same object is the sting, the bug and the end card |
| Rundown | a date block (big numerals, month, week) and three or four rows, each with a number block in accent; side by side in 16:9, date block on top in 9:16 | rows flip in vertically on the pulse (back ease) with a glint; at the close the same object returns and each row is ticked |
| Lower-third strap | see section 2 | enters with the first story, flips (new words up from below, old up and out, 10 frames; the tab flashes white when the category changes), leaves with a slide when the recap starts. A **profile** version (tab `PROFILE`) names a speaker |
| Split-flap board | tiles a little wider than high, top and bottom halves clipped separately | old value and new value on the same board, so the change reads in one look; ×2 / +x% called out beside it |
| Threshold chart | line from the peak, the threshold as a dashed gold line | the line head advances one period per step; the last step crosses the threshold in accent with a shake and a ring; the readout panel slides in after; axes show only what the data supports |
| Bars | grow from a zero line (downward for losses), values roll | a reference line at the shared threshold plus a badge |
| Explainer map | the same projection, one long take | members light in order of distance from the city; the city label, the institution label with leader, a stamp; the camera pushes, then pans to make room |
| Documents | paper sheets, ink header | fly out of the city dot on an arc (15 frames, scale 0.08 → 1, rotation settles); check boxes tick on the voice's key words |
| Quote card | ink background with the package's light streaks, a large accent quotation mark lands first, lines push up one by one (5 frames apart), then a gold rule, name, occasion and date | the speaker's own wording, in the language it was said, with the film's language beneath; never paraphrase inside quotation marks; an underline sweeps the key word as the voice says it |
| Spec card | eyebrow (source · date), the product name pushed up from a mask, a category stamp, proportion bars on one scale (the whole vs the part, values rolling), a percentage badge, a context chip; a second group for the hardware or the place, filling cell by cell, joined by a bracket to its total | one scale for every bar, so the proportion is true; every number from the source page |
| Stamps | accent fill or ink with a double accent frame, tilted 6–10° | land from 1.9× in 5 frames with an expanding ellipse; one per story at most |
| Transitions | vertical band wipe (a wide accent band, a thin ink line and a gold edge, 9 frames) between stories and programme parts; hard cut with a low thud inside a story; slanted wipe into the end card | nothing else: no dissolves, no 3D flips, no page turns, no fly-throughs. **The package layer travels with its picture**: under a wipe each side draws its own strap words, tab, source line and corner tag (old state on the old side, new state on the new side), so the band brushes the headline over; on a hard cut they change on the same frame. The incoming picture's entry animation starts under the band, so its first revealed frame already has content |

## 4. Colour

| Role | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| ink | `#121417` | backgrounds, panels, strap end block |
| ink2 / ink3 | `#1B1E23` / `#22262C` | cards, land on maps |
| accent (lemon) | `#F2CF1D` (light `#F8DE4A`, dark `#D8B30C`) | plate, tabs, band wipe, bars, stamps, the line that breaks |
| gold | `#D4B064` | thresholds, datelines, source lines, light fins, badges |
| paper | `#F4F1E8` | strap, documents, the chart line |
| breaking | `#FE6B3D` | only for BREAKING and LIVE. A weekly round-up has neither, so it never appears |

Structure to keep when you change the channel: one accent + one gold-like secondary + paper + ink, and a reserved alarm colour. Ink text on the accent; on paper, secondary lines in a dark olive (`#6E5A1E`) because gold is too weak there.

## 5. Type & on-screen text

- **Headlines and strap**: a sturdy sans in its heaviest weights. A brand's own sans takes this role if it has a black weight.
- **Numbers, names, the plate**: a condensed sans in extra-bold (the kind of Barlow Condensed), with a semi-bold for secondary lines and quotes.
- **Datelines, sources, axis labels**: a monospace (the kind of IBM Plex Mono), all caps with a little tracking in Latin script. The condensed face and the mono are the package; keep them whatever the brand uses. No handwriting faces.
- **A second language**, if the film has one, gets its own smaller line under the first; a label never wraps.
- **Narrated lines are not repeated as captions.** The voice says one sentence per graphic; the strap flips to that story's headline and the graphic's key word lands (an underline, a tick, a number) on the frame the voice says it.

## 6. Picture layer and grade

- When there is no footage, the picture layer is data graphics drawn in code plus, at most, a few **mood pictures**, each tagged `ILLUSTRATION` for as long as it is on screen. They set a mood (a server room behind a model launch, a wafer behind a chip story); they never stand in for the event, the place or the people.
- One grade for every picture: greyscale → stretch the black and white points → a slight S-curve → duotone from `#0E1013` to `#ECE7D8`, plus a dark ink gradient on the reading side for type, a 50–55% vignette and light grain. Graphics and the package are never grained.
- Real footage and screen recordings take the same grade path when they are background; never crop out, blur or cover a station's logo or watermark (a clip that carries one is used as it is, with its credit, or not at all), and don't scale a clip past 1.5×.

## 7. Editing grammar

- **One pulse of 16 frames** (half 8, quarter 4), counted from the end of the sting. Every cut and wipe lands on a pulse or a half.
- **Shot lengths**: sting 1.5 s; rundown 4–5 s; story shots 3–7 s; a map long take (6–12 s) as the bulletin's breath; a comparison stays until every item has been fully on screen for 2.5 s or more. A 30-second bulletin is a sting, a short rundown, two or three items and the recap.
- **A package action every 0.5–1 s** inside a shot (flip, count, stamp, tick, strap change, map light, line step). No frame flat for more than a second; a strong action within half a second of every cut.
- **Date every story**: a weekly bulletin's items happen on different days; the date sits in the rundown row, the eyebrow line, the readout or the document.
- **The order inside an item**: what (the name or the number) → how big (the line, the bars, the price board) → who it affects or who said it (bars, documents, a quote) → the verdict stamp.

## 8. Sound palette

- **Foley of the package**: a whoosh on every wipe, flap clicks on split-flaps, a stamp thump and clack, teleprinter ticks under a chart, a click per lit country on a map, a sonar ping on a city, paper and typewriter ticks on documents, a pen tick on each check box.
- **Three weights of hit**: a light tick for a row or a tile, a low round thud on hard cuts and story changes, and one heavy impact kept for the sting and the biggest break.
- **Tonal accents only**: a bright metallic tick as each rundown row lands, a step higher per row, and the same ticks returning as the rows are ticked off. No tune.
- **Room**: server-room air under a tech picture, soft air under maps, very low; nothing under the rundown.
- **Silence**: one pulse of nothing before the line crosses its threshold, then the heaviest hit of the film; the room alone after the verdict stamp.
- **Sound across a cut**: the wipe's whoosh starts four frames before the band moves and peaks as it crosses the centre; the anchor's sentence may start under the band.
- **Voice**: one anchor, one sentence per graphic, starting about three frames after the cut; level, quick, unhurried on numbers.

## 9. Native moves

1. **Sting becomes the bug**: the brand plate slams in, then flies to the corner and stays there for the whole bulletin; opening and programme are one object.
2. **Strap tab = category**: the strap's tab changes category (LAUNCHES, PRICES, POLICY …) with a white flash when a new story starts; no separate item cards between stories.
3. **Rundown, then ticked off**: the same list opens and closes the bulletin; at the end each row gets its tick.
4. **Spec card**: a launch reduced to its proportions: the whole and the part on one scale, then what it runs on, joined by a bracket to the total.
5. **Strap that never leaves**: one strap group from the first story to the recap; words flip, the strap itself never exits mid-programme.
6. **Split-flap before/after**: old and new value on one board, each tile flipping from the old to the new.
7. **Breaking the threshold**: a line advances one period per step towards a dashed threshold; all sound stops for one pulse before the last step and the crossing gets the heaviest hit.
8. **Date in the eyebrow**: every graphic's eyebrow line carries source and date in mono caps, so a weekly round-up never blurs which day each item happened.
9. **City sends the letters**: on a map long take, documents fly out of the sending city to their addressees; one world → screen function keeps labels and documents attached through push and pan.
10. **Quote card + verdict stamp**: the speaker's own words in type, with name, occasion and date; an underline on the key word as the voice says it; one stamp.

## 10. Material

1. **Facts first.** Every on-screen number and every sentence of the voice comes from an official source (the company's own page, the institution's press release, the product's listing), recorded in the video's `facts.md` with the claim, the link, the quoted wording and any arithmetic. When a fact exists only in a news report, attribute it on screen and in the voice ("… reports") and keep to the plain claim; never put a single report's allegations about a real company or person on screen.
2. **Data.** Draw charts from real series only; when you have just a few reported numbers, draw a *schematic* line, tag it `SCHEMATIC`, and label only what you know. Invented sample values carry `<ExampleBadge />`.
3. **Pictures, in this order**: the user's own uploads (photos, logo, colours); screenshots and screen recordings of the product; public-domain, CC0 or CC BY photographs; abstract backgrounds drawn in code (gradients, grids, light fins: the package already carries the frame). Every word on screen is typeset in code, never baked into an image. No generated anchor, and no generated picture standing in for an event, a place or a person.
4. **Licences** (judge each file on its own page, not the search result): Wikimedia Commons CC0, public domain or CC BY, credited on the end card; U.S. federal agencies (NASA, NOAA, USGS…) when the page credits no third party; archives only where the rights statement is clear. Not used: CC BY-SA, "no known restrictions", unclear licences, re-uploads. Before downloading, tell the user what you will fetch, from where and under which licence.
5. **Maps**: Natural Earth (public domain). Borders are a claim too: ask the user before showing a disputed one.
6. **Logos**: never use another company's logo for a company in the news; set its name in type.
7. **People**: public figures in the news can be named on a profile strap; no private individuals, and no real personal data from a screenshot: fictional stand-ins with `<ExampleBadge />`.

## 11. Pitfalls

- **Package left behind.** If the strap, source line or tags are drawn once on top of everything, a wipe changes the picture under yesterday's headline and the strap catches up half a second later. Draw them per picture, inside the wipe's clip.
- **Leftover layout.** Removing a zone without moving the content down leaves a dead band above the strap.
- **Masked type peeking in.** A push-in title clipped by a mask shows its top edge before it moves if the travel distance doesn't clear the mask; start it fully below.
- **Words stronger than the source.** "Request for information" is not "investigation" or "penalty"; write "for the first time" only if a source says so, and check the institution's own pages, which can contradict a report.
- **Thresholds depend on the reference.** "Down 20%" from *which* high? Name the reference on the axis; from a different peak the claim can be false.
- **Attribute what only a report says.** When the official source does not name the companies, the graphic carries "reported by …" and so does the voice.
- **A schematic posing as data.** Rolling counters on a final value flash values that never existed; set the value with a mask push instead. Don't label axes the data doesn't support.
- **Source lines under the strap.** Keep them short, about 36 characters.
- **Stamps over key words.** A stamp hit can mask the voice; keep its clack short and dull, or move it a few frames past the word.
- **Gold on paper** has too little contrast: use ink or dark olive.
- **Names in synthetic speech.** Spell brand names, model numbers and abbreviations the way they should sound in the voice-over text, while the strap keeps the real spelling. When `npm run review` mishears an unusual name under "Heard:", check the line before rewriting it.

## 12. Building it here

- **Plain React, CSS and SVG.** Parts are absolutely positioned `div`s on zones computed from `useLayout()` and `p(vertical, horizontal)`; parallelograms and slanted wipes are `clip-path: polygon(…)`; charts, maps and leaders are SVG. No map library is installed: project the map data's coordinates into `<path>`s with a few lines of your own (one projection for the whole take).
- **One `Package` component per picture** draws that picture's strap words, tab, source line and corner tag. A wipe clips the old picture and the new one (each with its own package) with `clip-path: inset(…)` and draws the band between them.
- **Turn off the engine's own transition**: `<SceneTrack sweeps={false} />`; the band wipe replaces the light sweep. No `Finish` over the film: grain belongs to mood pictures only, as an overlay clipped to the picture. Scenes take no `SceneFrame` entry or exit: its blurred push is the engine's default look, not this one. The timeline's `overlap` is how long the old scene stays mounted under the new one (8 frames by default): build it with `{ overlap: 0 }` for a hard cut, or give it the length of this style's own transition.
- **The grade** is a CSS `filter: grayscale(1) contrast(…)` plus an SVG `feComponentTransfer` table for the duotone, then gradient layers for the ink side and the vignette.
- **Motion**: springs suit the package: `springAt(…, SPRING.snappy)` for the plate slam, stamps and flips, `prog(…, ease.back)` for rundown rows, `ease.inOut` for the map camera. Split-flap tiles are two halves with `overflow: hidden` and a `rotateX` on the falling half; rolling values use fixed-width digits.
- **Push-up words**: `MaskRise` for the product name on a spec card and the quote lines.
- **The pulse lives in `timeline.ts`**: export it and derive every cut, wipe and package action from it; `Soundtrack.tsx` lands whooshes, thuds and ticks on the same frames, and strap flips take their frame from the voice-over's word timings.

## 13. In a product video

- **The brand is the channel.** Its colour is the accent (plate, tabs, band wipe, the line that breaks), its logo sits on the plate, and the gold-like secondary, paper, ink and the reserved alarm colour stay.
- **The items are the product's own news**: a release, a new feature, a price, a milestone. Never dress an invented event as news, and never imitate a real broadcaster. BREAKING is used only for something that is actually new today.
- **The real product is a full-frame graphic**: a screenshot as a flat card with an accent top edge, or a screen recording in a `Device` held square to the camera (`float={0}`), with the strap naming what it shows. The spec card takes the product's real specifications.
- **Real numbers and quotes only**, each in `facts.md` with its source. A quote card needs a real, named speaker who agreed to be quoted; otherwise leave the move out. Sample data carries `<ExampleBadge />`.
- **End card**: the slanted wipe, the plate in the centre with the logo, one line on where to get it, the date.
- **Arabic**: mirror the package: the tab and the strap start from the right, the end block and the bug move to the left, the slant of the plate and the direction of the wipes flip. The channel name lands as one whole word instead of letter by letter, mono lines lose their tracking, and ranges and dates go through `isolateNumbers`. Use a sturdy Arabic news sans in a black weight (the kind of IBM Plex Sans Arabic).
- **9:16**: use the 9:16 column of section 2. Spec-card groups and the rundown stack, a board shows fewer tiles per row, the map pushes closer; the strap ends above the bottom 22% and nothing important enters the right 12%.

## 14. Variation space

Decide per film: the channel's name, plate and accent colour; how many items (one lead with a long package, three or four equal items, a five-item round-up); which full-frame graphics each item needs; where the long take goes; the anchor's voice.

- **Three structures:** rundown → items → rundown ticked (round-up); one lead package with a two-box and a short "also today" grid; a countdown of items (a results night, a top five).
- **Three openings:** sting → rundown; cold open on the biggest graphic, then the sting; sting straight into the first story with the rundown as a recap only.
- **Three endings:** rundown ticked → end card; a "coming up next week" teaser card; the last stamp freezing into the end card.
