# Crayon Picture Book

> A picture-book page drawn live in wax crayon on toothy paper, with a watercolour wash that reveals what the crayon was hiding.
> References (grammar only): hand-drawn picture-book films of the 1970s and 80s made in crayon and coloured pencil, for hand-textured frames that breathe, wordless tenderness and still backgrounds with living figures. Copy no character or image from any of them, and never name one in the film.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

A children's picture book page **being drawn while you watch**: wax crayon on warm off-white paper, a limited box of crayons, child-logic perspective, colour that spills past the outline, and every line gently **boiling** because each frame is a fresh drawing. The tone is picture-book storytelling: tender, unhurried, led by the sound of the crayon itself, with a few lines of narration that are literally the words printed on the page. Any topic becomes **one page of a picture book that gets finished**: drawing and colouring are the plot.

It is not "cute vector art with a crayon filter": the look comes from **material physics** (wax sticks only to the peaks of the paper tooth, pressure decides how much of the tooth it reaches, watercolour is repelled by wax). Not a whiteboard explainer (no marker, no hand), not a watercolour painting (the wash is a guest, the crayon the host), not a chalkboard.

## 2. Materials & rendering

- **Paper**: warm off-white (around `#f4efe3`). The tooth is **anchored to the page** (it pans and zooms with the camera), never fixed to the screen: fine grain plus a faint horizontal fibre. It reads as lightly embossed, lit from the top corner, less so where wax has filled it. Low mottling of about 2%, a warm vignette.
- **Wax adhesion (the core rule)**: every crayon mark has a **pressure**. Wax lands only where the paper's tooth is higher than the pressure allows: light pressure gives speckled peaks; heavy pressure is almost solid and slightly darker.
- **Strokes**: ribbons with a slow hand wobble, width varying about 15%, tapered ends, a denser core and one or two thin streaks. Characters and buildings get the heaviest outline; refined "adult" objects about half.
- **Colouring**: back-and-forth zig-zag hatching with a visible gap, each pass at a slightly different pressure; **overshoot the outline a little**; a lighter cross-hatch pass on big areas.
- **Watercolour wash**: a separate layer multiplied over the page. Pigment pools darker at its edges, gathers in the deepest pits of the paper, blooms unevenly, and is **resisted by wax** (crayon stays almost clean through it). Brush strokes are wide bands with bristle streaks and a darker wet leading edge while moving.
- **Occlusion**: a front part hides what is behind it inside its outline; characters "keep the paper white" behind them, the way a child draws the figure first and colours around it.
- **Characters**: a skilled adult imitating a child: round head (about 2.5 heads tall), dot eyes with a white-wax catchlight, pink cheeks, tube limbs, mitten hands, bold hair shapes; objects with faces get big eyes and eyelids. **Child perspective**: gable front plus slanted side wall; people bigger than doors.

## 3. Colour logic

- **A box of about a dozen crayons, never more.** Outlines in one dark colour (indigo, dark brown, plum), **never black**. One white crayon reserved for wax resist.
- **At most one watercolour** per film, chosen for what it reveals (ultramarine for night, rose for dawn, sap green for rain). It never paints the characters.
- Saturated crayon on lots of bare paper; value comes from pressure, not extra hues. One accent on a character may echo the story.
- Example boxes: *seaside*: navy line, coral, sand, teal, turquoise, lemon, white; *orchard*: brown line, russet, apple red, mustard, olive, plum, cream, white.

## 4. Type & on-screen text

- **Narrated lines are the book's printed words**: a handwriting face (the Patrick Hand kind), about 5% of the frame height in 16:9 and 7% of the width in 9:16, in the outline colour, centred low on the page, each word slightly tilted and offset, boiling with the drawing, written on in the reading direction as it is spoken, on a ragged patch of blank paper (never a dark box). None during a wordless passage.
- **Titles are hand-lettered on the page** (a bold, rounded hand, the Gaegu Bold kind), written stroke by stroke in Latin script. You can't fade crayon: a title leaves by the camera moving off it, a page turn or being coloured over.
- **The style needs its handwriting face** whatever the brand's font is; the brand's own face appears only in the logo.

## 5. Motion quality

- **Everything drawn is stepped**: hold each drawing 2–3 frames (about 12 a second) for lines, colouring, characters and the boil. The camera, the wash's leading edge and twinkles move on every frame.
- **Boil**: each stroke re-jitters its offset (under a pixel), wobble and hatching rows every drawing. Characters at full strength, background lines at about 0.6, background colouring still. Calm the boil to about half when the film goes quiet.
- **Drawing on**: lines reveal along their length in 0.4–0.9 s; colouring row by row; words in the reading direction.
- **Acting**: substitution, a new drawing per pose on the pulse; two key drawings alternate for a repeated action. Big simple poses; a child's drawing doesn't in-between.
- **Colouring is an action**: being coloured in *is* doing (a coat scribbled on = putting it on; pink scribble = feeling shy; a wash = night falling).

## 6. Camera grammar

The camera moves over a large page. A vocabulary, not a route; every move needs a reason. Opening and ending come from the topic.

| Move | What it expresses | Can serve |
|---|---|---|
| Locked full page | Paper before drawing; the whole picture | a beginning from nothing; a tableau; a reveal that needs the page |
| Push toward a character | "This one" | a title giving way; a feeling arriving |
| Locked medium close-up | Substitution poses act | comedy; a decision; a small failure |
| Tilt along a gaze | We see what they see | discovery; longing; something huge |
| Tight follow | The world draws itself ahead of the subject | a brave act; a path; building |
| Held wide while the brush moves | The material is the motion | a wash reveal; weather; day or night falling |
| Slow truck across the page | Reading in the page's direction | a list; a sequence; a family |
| Pull out past the page edge | The page is an object | a book on a table; a fridge drawing |
| Page turn | New chapter, time jump | before/after; the next day |

Key figures at least a quarter of the frame height in close shots; keep bare paper low on the page for the printed words. Transitions: drawing on, colouring over, the wash, a pan or a page turn; never a dissolve.

## 7. Sound palette

- **The materials are the soundtrack**: crayon is a stick-slip, band-passed rasp whose grain follows the stroke's speed (colouring is a row of even swishes); the wet brush is a soft, low hush with bristle grain, set left or right with the stroke; then felt puffs, fabric swishes, a sharpener, a crayon set down on wood, a page turn (lift, whoosh, flap).
- **Every stroke that draws on gets its rasp; held drawings and the boil stay silent.** Three weights: a light tick for a dot or an eye, a medium rasp for a line, a long swishing pass for colouring.
- **A quiet bed**: room tone and paper hush, a distant clock tick; never tonal.
- **Small accents, never a tune**: one tiny bright ting per uncovered sparkle, one woodblock tock per counted thing, each a single pitch.
- **Silence is a pause**: a full pulse of nothing after a failure; a short breath of silence before the wash begins.
- **Sound across a cut**: the page's lift starts a few frames before the turn; the brush's hush runs on over the pull-back.
- **Voice**: a soft, warm storyteller, short lines with breaths, close and unhurried. Avoid phrases from famous books.

## 8. Native moves

A menu: use the ones your story needs.

- **Wax resist.** White crayon is invisible until a wash passes; hide the payoff in plain sight from the first frame. *Fits content like:* a farewell message; a product's hidden feature; roots under a garden.
- **Drawn live.** The world draws itself just ahead of the hero. *Fits content like:* a bridge plank by plank; a journey map; a company timeline.
- **Colouring is an action.** *Fits content like:* a town lighting up window by window; a team putting on kit; fruit ripening.
- **Boil.** A held frame is alive; calming it quiets the film. *Fits content like:* a pause for thought; a goodbye; rest.
- **Child logic.** Wrong perspective, faces on objects, numbers in the sky. *Fits content like:* a launch countdown; a house that smiles once it is insulated; a sun that clocks out.
- **It is a book.** Pull back to the object; turn the page. *Fits content like:* a year in review; a "chapter two"; a thank-you card.

## 9. Pitfalls of the medium

- Gaps between wash bands leave white stripes → overlap the bands and cover all the intended paper.
- Wavy band edges and wide light streaks read as hills → small, fine waviness and thin, faint streaks.
- Light-pressure colouring goes muddy under a wash → press hard or cross-hatch wherever the wash crosses.
- Text where the wash passes survives it (wax resists) → place it elsewhere or accept it.
- Characters vanish under a wash → keep them on a layer above the wash.
- Outlines over hair read as a headband → outline hair masses on their outer edge only.
- A knock-out drawn as a pale crayon fill shows as grey crayon → make it clean paper: a paper-coloured shape with no wax texture.
- A page turn built from flat strips shows a staircase → turn one whole sheet in perspective and shade it.
- Double vignette at a page-to-object pull-out → the page carries none; ramp the outer scene's in.
- Tooth fixed to the screen looks like a dirty lens and crawls under the camera → anchor it to the page.
- Boiling the background fills makes the frame swim and renders slowly → boil lines and characters only.

## 10. Building it here

- **SVG for everything drawn; no shader.** Lines are `path`s with round caps, drawn on with `stroke-dashoffset` (`pathLength={1}`); colouring is one long zig-zag `path` that overshoots its outline, revealed along its length; the camera is one wrapper `transform` over a page larger than the frame.
- **Wax on tooth is an SVG filter**: `feTurbulence` with a **fixed** `seed` is the paper; add it to the stroke's alpha (`feComposite operator="arithmetic"`), then steepen with `feComponentTransfer`, so light strokes (low opacity) speckle and heavy ones fill. Apply it inside the camera wrapper so the tooth travels with the page.
- **Boil**: a second `feTurbulence` + `feDisplacementMap` on line and character layers, its `seed` set to `Math.floor(frame / 2.5)`; poses switch on the same step. Background colouring gets no changing seed. Use `noise(x, seed)` for any jitter, never `Math.random`.
- **Wash and resist**: the wash is a layer with `mix-blend-mode: multiply`, wiped on with a `mask-image` band whose edge is roughened by displacement, plus a darker blurred rim. White-wax drawing sits **above** the wash in the paper's own colour: invisible on bare paper, it appears as the wash passes beneath. Characters sit above the wash too.
- **Page turn and book**: one `div` with `perspective` and `rotateY`, a gradient for the shade; the desk scene is a plain layer behind it.
- **Text**: load the handwriting face in the brand's `fonts.ts`; reveal words with a `clip-path` wipe in the reading direction, timed to the voice-over's word timings.
- **Overrides**: no springs (stepped substitution instead), no `MaskRise`, no `Finish` grain (the paper is the texture), no `Backdrop`, and `<SceneTrack sweeps={false} />`. Camera moves use `prog` with `ease.inOut`.
- **Timing** lives in `timeline.ts`; each stroke's rasp in `Soundtrack.tsx` starts on the frame its line begins.

## 11. In a product video

- **The brand colour is one crayon in the box**, used as the accent on the hero or the product, or as the single watercolour if the reveal is the brand moment. The box stays a dozen crayons and the outline is never black.
- **The product is drawn first, shown real once.** Draw the phone and its key screen as a child would (big shapes, three or four elements); then show the real screenshot one time, flat, in a `Phone` lying beside the page in the pull-out, or taped onto the page like a photo with crayon drawn around it. Never tilt or glow it.
- **Wax resist is made for the hidden feature**: the product's best line, written in white wax from frame one, found by the wash.
- **Numbers in the sky are real numbers**, each in `facts.md`; a sample name or figure carries `<ExampleBadge />`.
- **End card**: the page as an object (on a desk, on a fridge), the real logo placed flat on bare paper like a sticker, one hand-lettered line on how to get it.
- **Arabic**: the page reads right to left, so trucks, drawing-on and the wash travel that way; words appear whole, one at a time, on the drawing step, never letter by letter or stroke by stroke, with no letter-spacing; tilt whole words, not glyphs; a friendly rounded Arabic handwriting face.
- **9:16**: the page is a tall sheet; stack what sat side by side, and keep the printed words on bare paper above the bottom 22%, clear of the top 12% and right 12%.

## 12. Variation space

You decide the structure, the characters (or none), the page, the opening, the ending, the camera path, the pacing, the crayon box and whether there is a wash.

- Structures: **a colouring-book page** (all outlines there from frame one; each idea colours one area); **a flip through several pages** (one stage per page, joined by turns); **two children, one page** (two crayon colours disagree, then combine).
- Openings: **a finished page rubbed out** to begin again; **a crayon tip in extreme close-up** mid-stroke, pulling back; **a crumpled page being flattened**.
- Endings: **the page taped to a fridge** among others; **a last scribble** filling the frame with one colour; **a stray splash of water** finding the white-wax message.
