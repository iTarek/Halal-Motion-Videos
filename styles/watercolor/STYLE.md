# Watercolor Brush

> A hand-painted journal page that paints itself: translucent brush strokes with dry-brush flying white on warm cotton paper, hand-written notes beside what is painted, nothing drawn with a vector line.
> References (grammar only): naturalist field-journal and botanical-plate watercolours (specimen + note); long horizontal handscroll panoramas (one continuous painted world); the textbook "transect" diagram (a gradient read across space). Borrow the grammar, never a specific artwork, character or layout.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

- **One sheet of warm, fibrous paper** under everything; it is also the eraser (paper-coloured mist washes things away).
- **Everything is individual brush strokes**: a variable-width ribbon at partial opacity plus a few thin **bristle tracks** that break into dashes (flying white). Strokes overlap and darken where they cross, like transparent pigment. No vector outlines; a darker edge stroke along a form is the only "line".
- **Things paint themselves in**, stroke by stroke, structure before detail (trunk → branches → leaves; outline wash → shadow → accent).
- **An annotation layer** in a hand the painter would write with: labels with pen leader lines, a small measured value, a quiet title.
- Tone: calm, curious, precise. Numbers are real; the palette can carry data.

Not ink wash (colour, not ink tones; no calligraphic brush-as-gesture), not impasto (no thick paint, no knife), not a children's picture book (no crayon, no cute outlines), not vector illustration with a watercolour texture laid on top.

## 2. Materials & rendering

- **Paper**: a warm base with slow, smooth mottling (about ±3.5%), a few thousand short curved fibres, fine grain and a light vignette. It sits under every frame and never moves.
- **Stroke**: a ribbon whose width follows a profile (brush, leaf, even, tip, trunk) with about ±22% roughness, filled as a thin wash (about 0.55–0.6 opacity), plus 3–9 bristle tracks at 30–85% opacity, each with its own dash pattern and its own early end: that is the flying white. A stroke can be painted up to any fraction of its length (a moving brush tip).
- **Wash**: the same shape painted in 3 passes with slightly shifted corners, each at a third of the opacity, so edges darken where the passes disagree (a pooled watercolour edge).
- **Depth by layers**: several parallax layers of washes and painted objects; farther = smaller, lighter, less saturated; paper-coloured mist bands between layers.
- **Finished objects are kept as still pictures**; only what is being painted or transformed is drawn stroke by stroke.
- **Everything is fixed by the frame number**: the same frame always gives the same strokes.

## 3. Colour logic

- **Paper is the white.** Never paint pure white; light is unpainted paper.
- **Transparent layering**: darker = more passes, not a darker pigment on top. Keep 3–5 hues per scene plus ink.
- **Ink** (a warm near-black and a lighter brown) for lines, text, edges and stems only.
- **Colour may carry the data or the mood**: blend sky, ground and background by position or time so the palette itself changes with the subject. Map ramps cut into 4–6 bands read as paint, continuous ramps read as software.
- **One accent** (a vermilion, a rain blue) reserved for one element that recurs: a stamp, a marker, a key object.
- Examples: arid-to-wet: red ochre → sage → straw → blue-grey → deep green; a harbour at dawn: `#f3e2c8` sky, `#9fb4c4` water, `#5f7486` hulls, accent `#c8442a` buoys; a vegetable garden: `#e8e0c0` soil light, `#8ea860` leaves, `#4e6e3c` shade, accent `#d9632e` pumpkins.

## 4. Type & on-screen text

- **Three voices**: an elegant serif (the Cormorant Garamond kind) for titles and narrated lines, a handwriting face (the Caveat kind) for notes, a small mono (the IBM Plex Mono kind, widely spaced in Latin script) for measured labels.
- **Hand notes** sit over a paper-coloured halo, are revealed in reading order like writing, and have a pen leader line that grows to its specimen.
- **Narrated lines sit on the paper, not in a box**: near the bottom, serif italic at about 3.3% of the frame's short side (two balanced lines at most), ink at about 78% over a paper-coloured halo, each word arriving as it is spoken.
- **Numbers** are written as digits on screen and spelled out in the voice-over text.
- A brand's own typeface can take the titles if it is a calm serif or a light sans; the handwriting for notes is part of the look and stays.

## 5. Motion quality

- **Painting-in is the main motion**: an object's progress follows where it sits on screen or a cue; the strokes in its list each take a slice of that progress, so structure appears first.
- **Sway** only after an object is finished: a small sideways lean made of two slow waves, about 0.006 for trees, 0.01–0.03 for grass.
- **Brush-edge wipes**: a wavy vertical edge reveals a whole scene; lines grow by stroke progress; labels open in reading order.
- **Mist** erases: paper-coloured layers with a wavy rising edge.
- Animated creatures and events may be phased to the film's pulse so landings fall on it.
- Smooth easing on every frame; the brush is slow and fluid, and nothing steps or jitters.

## 6. Camera grammar

A vocabulary, not a route. The picture is one painted sheet; cutting is rare. Opening and ending come from the topic.

| Move | What it expresses | Can serve |
|---|---|---|
| Long lateral dolly over a continuous world | a gradient; a journey; cause and consequence | a transect; a route; a timeline |
| Vertical tilt along one object | scale; measurement | a tall tree; a building; a depth |
| Locked sheet, things painted in | study; attention | a specimen plate; a recipe; a diagram |
| Slow push into a detail | the observer leaning in | a seed; an insect; a hand-written note |
| Pull-back to a map or diagram | the whole re-framed | where it all happened; a system |
| Mist wash to blank paper | chapter end; time passing | a season; forgetting; a new place |
| Page turn / new sheet slides over | a new entry in the journal | a new day; a new species; a new case |
| Change in time on a locked view | growth; decay; history | a season cycle; before/after; deep time |

Framing: generous paper margins; one hero subject per view with its note; labels never cover the subject. Transitions: brush-edge wipes, mist, a page turn, or a continuous camera move; no hard video cuts, no dissolves between unrelated pictures.

## 7. Sound palette

- **Sounds made from the scene's matter**: a brush rustle on paper for each painting-in (band-passed noise with a light tremolo, sweeping across with the brush); pencil scratch for notes; a soft thump for a stamp; a page flip; water in a jar.
- **Three weights**: a dab for a leaf, a longer sweep for a wash, many rustles at once over a soft low swell for a burst.
- **A nature bed from the subject** under each place: birds, wind, rain plinks, water, insects, a fire's crackle. Quiet, never tonal.
- **What stays silent**: sway, mist drifting, the camera.
- **Silence is the brush lifted**: everything out except paper and room, for half a second, to frame the one painted moment that matters.
- **Sound across a transition**: the next place's bed starts under the mist before the new scene shows, and the brush goes on rustling over a page turn.
- **Tonal accents** only as single drops: one soft plink for a raindrop or a landed stamp. Never a tune.
- **Voice**: a warm, unhurried documentary read, one line per idea.

## 8. Native moves

A menu: use the ones your story needs.

- **Painting-in as reveal.** A new thing appears stroke by stroke as it enters. *Fits content like:* the stages of a building; the ingredients of a dish; the organs of a body.
- **Burst.** A whole group paints in at once (a second at most) for a peak. *Fits content like:* a market crowd; a field in bloom; a city lighting up.
- **Palette as data.** Sky, ground and background blend by a measured value. *Fits content like:* pollution along a river; temperature by altitude; noise across a city.
- **Specimen + note.** One hero object with a hand-written label and leader line. *Fits content like:* a tool kit; an orchard's varieties of fruit; parts of a bicycle.
- **Mist erase.** The world washes back to paper. *Fits content like:* forgetting; a daydream ending; a site cleared.
- **Map in time.** A painted map whose fills change continuously through years. *Fits content like:* a language spreading; a coastline eroding; a railway network growing.
- **Painted transformation.** Objects cross-fade to a burnt, wet or aged version and regrow. *Fits content like:* a wildfire and recovery; a flood; a restoration.

## 9. Pitfalls of the medium

- Wash bands too short flatten tall shapes into flat tops and show seams on tilts → size them well beyond the frame.
- A translucent wash that ends at its own edge shows a hard seam under other layers → extend it below the frame and fade it to paper.
- Random marks that differ between renders of the same frame make strokes flicker → every stroke comes from a fixed seed, never from a live random number.
- Thousands of strokes per frame are too slow → keep finished objects as still layers.
- A vector outline, or a flat fill with a texture on top, reads as software → ribbons, bristles and three-pass washes only.
- Continuous colour ramps read as software → cut them into 4–6 bands.
- Motion phased to the pulse must be re-derived when the timeline changes.

## 10. Building it here

- **A stroke is SVG**: a ribbon `<path>` built from a centre line and a width profile, its roughness from `noise(x, seed)`, at partial `fill-opacity`; bristle tracks are thin `<path>`s with their own `stroke-dasharray`. Layers use `mix-blend-mode: multiply`, so crossings darken like pigment.
- **Painting-in**: reveal each stroke along its length with a `mask` or `clip-path` driven by `prog`; the strokes of an object share one progress value, sliced in list order. A wash is its shape drawn three times with corners shifted by `noise`.
- **Heavy scenes**: draw the strokes on a `<canvas>` from the frame number, and keep finished objects as memoised layers.
- **Paper**: a warm `div` with `feTurbulence` mottling and fibres at a fixed `seed`. **Mist** and **brush-edge wipes** are paper-coloured shapes or `mask-image`s with a wavy edge; sway is a small `skewX` from the frame number.
- **Camera and depth**: one `transform` on a wide world, with each parallax layer moving at its own rate (`prog` with `ease.inOut` or `ease.soft`). Nothing is 3D; a page turn is a new sheet sliding over.
- **Overrides**: no springs (nothing bounces), no `Finish` (the paper carries grain and vignette), no `Backdrop`, no `MaskRise`, and `<SceneTrack sweeps={false} />`. Notes open with a `clip-path` wipe; leader lines grow with `stroke-dashoffset`. Scenes take no `SceneFrame` entry or exit: its blurred push is the engine's default look, not this one. The timeline's `overlap` is how long the old scene stays mounted under the new one (8 frames by default): build it with `{ overlap: 0 }` for a hard cut, or give it the length of this style's own transition.
- **Cues live in `timeline.ts`** (when each object starts to paint, the pulse); `Soundtrack.tsx` lays a rustle under each painting-in on the same frames.

## 11. In a product video

- **The brand's colour is the one accent**: the stamp, the recurring marker, the key object. If it is soft enough to be a pigment it can be one of the scene's 3–5 hues instead. Paint it as transparent strokes, never as a flat fill.
- **The product is a specimen with notes.** Its real screen appears as a picture pasted into the journal: a flat screenshot with a thin ink edge and a soft paper shadow, hand-written labels and leader lines pointing at real parts of it. Paint the world around it; never paint over it.
- **Measured labels carry real values**, each listed in `facts.md`. A palette that claims to show data must come from real data, or carry `<ExampleBadge />`.
- **End card**: a locked sheet, the logo landing like a stamp, the name in the serif, and one hand-written line on how to get it.
- **Arabic**: the lateral dolly, the wipes and the writing-on all run right to left; notes appear a whole word at a time, never letter by letter; no letter-spacing, so measured labels drop the wide tracking. An elegant naskh for titles and an informal Arabic hand for notes suit the page.
- **9:16**: a tall sheet that the camera tilts down suits it best; on a lateral dolly the window is narrow, so keep each hero subject within about 80% of the width. Narrated lines stay above the bottom 22%, titles below the top 12%, notes out of the right 12%.

## 12. Variation space

You decide the subject, the structure, what gets painted, the palette logic, whether anything is measured, the opening, the ending and the camera.

- Structures: **a journal kept over one year** (locked page per month, each painted over the last); **anatomy of one object** (a single specimen dissected into labelled parts, each part a painted close-up); **a recipe or process** (steps painted top to bottom on one tall sheet, tilting down).
- Openings: **a finished painting** that we then watch being painted backwards to its first stroke, and forwards again; **a close-up of wet pigment** spreading, pulled back to show what it became; **a hand-written question** on blank paper, answered by painting.
- Endings: **the page left unfinished**, brush lifted mid-stroke; **a close-up of one detail** as the rest fades to paper; **the sheet dries and is filed** into a stack of other pages.
