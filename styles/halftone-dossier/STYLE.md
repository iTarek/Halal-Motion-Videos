# Halftone Dossier

> A retro print file: cream archive paper, overprinted halftone dots in a few flat inks, huge heavy headlines with misregistered shadows, rubber stamps that slam, and thick-outlined characters. The film is an inspector's file on one subject, read out one exhibit at a time.
> References (grammar only, never copy): Ben-Day-dot printing of mid-century comics and pop art for dot density as shading; riso and offset overprint zines for multiply layers and misregistration; office filing (ID-photo height charts, inspection forms, file folders) for the framing device; Japanese variety-show telop captions for the keyword-highlight caption bar.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

- **The whole film is paper.** A warm cream sheet with soft mottling, fine grain, a vignette and a fold; dark scenes are printed on it too, never glowing.
- **Shading is never a gradient**: it is halftone dots whose size follows a density field. Two or three dot layers at different screen angles overprint (multiply); that is the "printed" feel.
- **Office apparatus**: file numbers, file chips, numbered exhibits, height charts, name plates, stamps. Their seriousness is the frame; the subject can be weighty or trivial, and the humour is dry, never at anyone's expense.
- **Loud but readable**: one idea per card of 2–4 s, one big headline, one visual gag or exhibit, one caption line.

Not Risograph (no grainy two-ink texture as the whole look, no zine collage), not Swiss motion (no grid-driven type on white), not Game Show Flat (no candy sets, no rounds). Dots, paper and stamps must be visible in every frame.

## 2. Materials & rendering

- **Paper layer** (multiplied over everything, the same on every frame): broad mottling, fine noise with a slight warm bias, a radial vignette, a faint centre fold.
- **Dust layer**: a couple of hundred paper-coloured specks and fewer ink specks, changing every 2 frames: dust on the print, not film grain.
- **Halftone**: a rotated grid of circles; a step of about 2–2.4% of the frame's short side; a different screen angle per layer; each dot's radius follows the density at its place, and at about 0.7 of the step the dots merge into solid ink. Density fields: radial (halos, blooms, corner glows), edge (dots crowding the frame border), linear ramps ("lit from above"). The second layer is multiplied so inks overprint.
- **Halftone-filled numerals and shapes**: a flat fill overprinted with a darker halftone clipped to the glyph; giant numerals may bleed off the frame.
- **Line boil**: a small wobble of the outline that changes about 10 times a second (hold each state 3 frames), plus a fine ink-grain that eats a little of every fill. Characters and hand-drawn props only; **never** on text, halftone, file chips or captions.
- **Characters**: chunky shapes with a heavy ink outline (about 0.65% of the short side), flat fills, a lighter belly or face area, glossy eyes (an ink ellipse and two highlights) that can squint, blink or close into a happy arc. One head rig shared by all body poses.
- **Stamps**: a double rounded rectangle and bold text with rough edges and mottled ink; about 93% opacity, tilted up to 10° either way.
- **Props**: starbursts, speech bubbles, confetti, radial rays, sticky notes, folders, clocks: all flat fill and ink outline.

## 3. Colour logic

- **A paper ground, one dark ink, and four or five flat spot inks.** The dark ink (navy, black-brown or deep green) carries every outline, body text and the caption bar, and is the fade-out colour.
- One ink is the **overprint shadow** colour (misregistration offsets, caption shadows); one is the **highlight** for keywords and numerals; one warm red is reserved for stamps and alarms.
- Flat inks only. No gradients, no transparency ramps; tone comes from dot size and overprint.
- **Night or "confidential" scenes** switch to a dark ink ground with a lighter dot layer of the same family, still multiplied by the paper.
- Characters may own one or two local colours (fur, clothing) that appear nowhere else.
- Example palettes: cream / navy / cobalt / hot pink / yellow / red; manila / sepia-black / teal / mustard / vermilion; off-white / forest green / tomato / sky / ochre.

## 4. Type & on-screen text

- **Headlines**: a black-weight serif or slab, at 9–21% of the frame's short side, set near the reading-side margin, with a **misregistration shadow** (offset down and sideways by about 0.75% of the short side, in the overprint ink, multiplied).
- **Display and numerals**: a fat rounded display face (the Bagel Fat One kind) for names, big numbers, times. **Interjections**: a playful hand face, paper fill with a thick ink stroke and an offset shadow. **Data**: a heavy monospace for file numbers, dates, rulers, file names. **Captions and stamps**: a black-weight sans.
- **The style needs these heavy print faces.** A brand's own face takes the headline or caption role only if it has a black weight; otherwise it stays in the logo.
- **Cards are the text of the film.** A headline per card, plus a **caption bar**: a dark-ink rounded pill with an offset shadow in the overprint ink, paper-coloured text, the **keyword in the highlight ink**. It pops in after the headline. With narration, the caption bar carries the spoken line, each word popping as it is spoken.
- **File chips**: the file number and a chapter chip in the top reading-side corner, a status (a REC dot, a date) in the other; colours invert on dark scenes.
- Keep headlines to 2–4 words and stamps to 1–2 (PASSED, CLOSED).

## 5. Motion quality

- **Positions move smoothly on every frame; only the line boils**, about 10 times a second, so the drawing breathes while nothing jitters.
- **Everything enters with overshoot** (9–14 frames): rising from below the frame or growing from a little under full size. Nothing fades in.
- **Headlines pop piece by piece**: in Latin script each letter drops a short way and squashes in, 2 frames apart (1 frame for small text).
- **Stamp slam**: 3 frames from about 2.6× to 1×, easing in, then a small damped bounce; camera shake, flash and stamp sound on the same frame.
- **Squash landings** keep their volume (as wide as they are short), settling with a damped wobble.
- **Idle life**: breathing, tail or hair sway, blinks at scripted times, twinkles, a blinking REC dot or clock colon.
- **Dot wipe transitions**: a coarse grid of circles grows to full cover and shrinks back (about 13 frames) with a diagonal delay; the scene switches while covered. Each cut may take its own wipe colour. A hard cut is the contrast tool, on a big hit.

## 6. Camera grammar

A 2D camera over a flat, frontal page. A vocabulary, not a route. Opening and ending come from the topic.

| Move | What it expresses | Can serve |
|---|---|---|
| Locked frontal with a small zoom pulse (about 0.6%) | the page as a poster, alive | a title; a list; a finding |
| Slow push-in (1 → 1.05–1.08) | scrutiny, rising tension | a profile; a summary before a verdict |
| Shake that dies away fast | impact | a stamp; a crash; a landing |
| Flash frames (a white overlay, capped at half strength for big hits) | being photographed, a revelation | an ID photo; a verdict; a find |
| Pan across a pinned board | connection between exhibits | a timeline; candidates; a chain of events |
| Snap zoom into a halftone detail until dots are huge | "look closer" | a thumbprint; a signature; a number |
| Page turn or folder slide | next file, next chapter | a second file; a look back |
| Pull back to reveal the whole desk | the file is one among many | scale; "this happens every day" |

Compose like a printed page: headline at the top on the reading side, subject centre on the other side, caption bar low and centred, file chips in the corners. Transitions: dot wipe or hard cut; no dissolves.

## 7. Sound palette

- **Foley follows the print and the gag**: a camera shutter, paper slides and folder flaps, typewriter clicks, a pop for every piece of a headline, boings and plops for characters, glass or ceramic for breakables, small made-up animal voices.
- **The stamp is the big hit**: a boom that drops in pitch with a click on top, and a crackle for the biggest one. One stamp per film gets full weight; the others are lighter.
- **Three weights**: pops and clicks for text and chips, plops and slaps for characters and paper, the stamp.
- **A bed of a records room**: quiet air and paper hush, never tonal; dark scenes thin it to almost nothing.
- **Silence before the stamp**: empty everything for half a second so it lands alone. A rising sweep may lead into one reveal, once.
- **Small accents only**: a single bell as a tag on a happy finding; never a tune.
- **Sound across a cut**: the whoosh of a dot wipe starts before the cover and the typewriter of the next card is already clicking under it.
- **Voice** is optional; cards carry the film. If narrated: a dry, deadpan report voice, never breathless.

## 8. Native moves

A menu: use the ones your story needs.

- **The file frame.** A topic becomes "the file on X": list its findings. *Fits content like:* a cognitive bias under review; a city's traffic problem; a plant that won't stop growing.
- **Numbered exhibits.** Each card gets a giant halftone numeral behind it. *Fits content like:* five causes of a blackout; three clauses of a contract; the stages of a recall.
- **The stamp.** A verdict is a physical slam. *Fits content like:* APPROVED on a grant; EXPIRED on a policy; VERIFIED on a rumour check.
- **The ID photo.** Frontal subject, height chart, name plate, flash. *Fits content like:* a new species; a product teardown; the cause named in a bug report.
- **Pin board.** Exhibits pinned and linked with string, then shaken. *Fits content like:* a supply chain; a family tree; a parcel's journey.
- **Dot density = emotion.** Dots swell into a halo, crowd the edges for tension, bloom radially for joy. *Fits content like:* a record broken; a deadline closing in; a reunion.
- **Redaction.** Black bars print over words and lift one by one. *Fits content like:* a draft memo; a surprise party plan; spoilers.

## 9. Pitfalls of the medium

- **Letters placed by measurement overlap** when the font arrives late → let the browser lay the letters out in a row and animate each in place.
- **Boil on text or dots** turns them to mush → filter only characters and props.
- **Clip and filter ids are shared by the whole page** → every clipped numeral and every filter needs its own id.
- **An element shows at its resting place before its cue** → hide it explicitly until its start frame.
- **A dot wipe short of full coverage** at the cut shows the hard switch → the largest dot must cover its grid cell corner to corner.
- **A full white flash** reads as a dropped frame → cap it at about half.
- **Dots too small** (a step under about 1.3% of the short side) turn to moiré after encoding, and to grey on a phone.
- **Too many inks** lose the print feel → at most five plus paper.

## 10. Building it here

- **Plain SVG and CSS on a flat page**; the camera is one wrapper `transform`. Layout comes from `useLayout()` and `p(vertical, horizontal)`.
- **Halftone**: compute the circles from a density function and draw each layer as one SVG `path`, rotated to its screen angle, with `mix-blend-mode: multiply`; memoise static fields and recompute only the ones that animate. Clip to a numeral with a `clipPath` (ids from React's `useId()`). A `<canvas>` drawn from the frame number is fine for very dense fields.
- **Paper and dust**: one `feTurbulence` layer with a fixed `seed` plus a `radial-gradient` vignette and a fold line, multiplied over everything; dust is a few hundred tiny shapes placed with `noise(i, Math.floor(frame / 2))`.
- **Boil and rough ink**: `feTurbulence` + `feDisplacementMap` on characters and props with `seed = Math.floor(frame / 3)`; the stamp gets a coarser displacement and a grain mask with a fixed seed.
- **Pops and slams**: `springAt(…, SPRING.bouncy)` or `prog(…, ease.back)` for entrances (the engine's springs suit them); the stamp is `prog(frame, at - 3, 3, ease.in)` on scale, then a damped wobble, with the shake on the wrapper and a white overlay at half opacity on the same frame. Misregistration is a second copy of the text, offset, in the overprint ink, multiplied.
- **Dot wipe**: an overlay grid of circles whose radius runs up and back with a diagonal delay, placed over each cut; list the cuts in `index.ts` so the review strips check full coverage.
- **Overrides**: `<SceneTrack sweeps={false} />` and no `SceneFrame` entries (the wipe is the transition); no `Finish` (the paper has its own grain and vignette), no `Backdrop`, no `MaskRise` (words pop, they don't rise from a mask). A hard cut needs the timeline built with `{ overlap: 0 }`: the engine's default leaves the old scene under the new one for 8 frames.
- **Timing lives in `timeline.ts`**; `Soundtrack.tsx` puts the stamp's boom, each pop and each wipe's whoosh on the same frames.

## 11. In a product video

- **Build the ink set around the brand**: the brand colour is the highlight ink (keywords, numerals) or the stamp's ink when the stamp is the brand moment. Paper, one dark ink and at most five spot inks stay: that is the look.
- **The product is Exhibit A**: a real screenshot pinned in the file like a photo print, with a paper border and a paperclip, flat or slightly tilted, multiplied by the paper so it sits in the print; a snap zoom reads one detail. Or its ID photo: a flat `Phone` with an ink outline in front of the height chart, with a name plate.
- **Stamps tell the truth**: VERIFIED, APPROVED or a number only when the product's own site or listing says so, each in `facts.md`. Giant numerals are real figures or exhibit numbers.
- **Names and file data are fictional** unless the user supplies them; sample data carries `<ExampleBadge />`, styled as one more file chip. The "problem" on file is a situation, never a named competitor or person.
- **End card**: the folder closes, the logo sits on its tab or label, one line on how to get it in the caption bar, and the last stamp lands.
- **Arabic**: mirror the page (headline top right, subject left of centre, file chips swapped); words pop whole, one at a time, never letter by letter, with no letter-spacing; a black-weight Arabic display face with square, sturdy shapes for headlines and stamps, and a heavy sans in place of the mono.
- **9:16**: stack the page: file chips and headline below the top 12%, subject in the middle, caption bar above the bottom 22%, nothing that matters in the right 12%; giant numerals bleed off the reading side.

## 12. Variation space

You decide what the file is about, its findings or exhibits, the characters (or none), the inks within the colour logic, the tone (playful or grave), the opening, the ending and the length (it grows with the number of exhibits; each needs time to be read).

- Structures: **a released report** whose pages are unredacted one by one until the real story shows; **a lost-parcel file** that works backwards from the last scan; **a quality audit** where each exhibit tells a different story from the brochure.
- Openings: **the specimen bag** (one object on a dark ink ground, the file assembles around it); **a thumbprint** in huge dots that resolves into a picture as we pull back; **the phone call** (a caption bar alone on paper, the file drops in after).
- Endings: **the file goes into a drawer** full of identical files; **a redacted final page** (the answer stays blacked out); **the stamp misses** (it lands on the table, and the subject shuffles out of frame unstamped).
