# Paper Pop-up Book

> A pop-up book on a real desk becomes a stage: flat cut-paper actors with white borders perform in front of paper sets that spring up from the page, under real lamp light and a real macro lens.
> References (grammar only): the *Paper Mario* series (flat cut-paper actors with a white border in a diorama, bubbles that type on with blips, action ratings); Robert Sabuda / Matthew Reinhart pop-up books (layered V-folds, pieces rising back to front, pull-tabs); macro "tiny world on a real desk" photography. Never copy their characters, logos, UI or sounds, and never name them in the film.

## 1. Essence, and what it is not

Two worlds in one frame. **The real world** at human scale: a believable desk surface in a softly lit room, a few real objects, a macro lens (shallow focus, warm lamp light, soft contact shadows). **The paper world**: a hardcover book in that room; when it opens, one board stands up as the **backdrop** and the lower page is the ground. Every set piece is a **flat cut-paper card** that lies on the page and **pops up** around its bottom edge. Characters are cut paper too: a few centimetres tall, flat, with a thick off-white border, a grey cardboard edge and grain.

What sells it is the **mismatch of scale and material**: flat, saturated, outlined paper cartoons under real light, casting cut-out-shaped shadows and going soft in real lens blur. Keep the paper graphic; let light and lens make it real.

Not a papercut silhouette film (no one-colour lace, no backlight), not a 3D cartoon (nothing modelled round), not claymation (paper hinges, folds, curls and slides; it never squishes).

## 2. Materials & rendering

- **Real proportions**: a page 30–50 cm wide, actors 3–5 cm tall, so an actor is about a tenth of the page. At eye level an actor fills a third of the frame's height and the room behind is a blur.
- **Cut-paper finish**: art with a dark ink outline; its silhouette grown outward into a wide off-white border, and grown again, shifted slightly down, into a grey cardboard edge (the thickness); a shared paper grain on top.
- **Drawing**: one dark-brown ink (never black), a thick outline plus a thinner detail line, round joins; shading = one hard **crescent** (fill with the shade, refill shifted with the base colour); wobbly shapes, never perfect geometry; simple faces.
- **Pop-up pieces**: flat cards with art on the front and plain paper on the back. **The shadow is the cut-out silhouette**, never a rectangle. Anything "3D" is several flat cards (front and back layers, triangles hinged on a keel).
- **The book**: a cloth cover with a foil title that catches a glint, a page block with edge lines, a leaf that curls as it turns.
- **Real world**: a softly blurred room, real wood or fabric under the book, a few real props. Colours stay neutral: no grade that shifts the paper's hues.
- **Depth of field**: blur grows with distance from the focus plane, in front of it and behind it; a sharp foreground card must never pick up blur from what is behind it. Then a light bloom, a vignette, a little warmth.

## 3. Colour logic

- Saturated children's-book colour, **never neon**; the real world stays natural and slightly warm so paper pops against it.
- **One dominant hue family per spread**, so a page turn is also a colour change; a few accents (a red, a yellow, a gold) recur across spreads to stitch the book together.
- Ink dark brown, border off-white, card backs plain paper.
- Unfinished or "outside the story" pages lose colour: graphite on cream. Night is a change of the paper art; the real lamp keeps its warmth.
- Examples of spread families: green meadow under sky blue; pink-and-lilac market with mint accents; ochre desert with a turquoise oasis.

## 4. Type & on-screen text

- **Rounded faces**: a rounded sans for narrated lines, bubbles and signs (the kind of Fredoka); a chunky display face for the cover, banners and action words (the kind of Lilita One); a storybook serif for taglines (the kind of IM Fell English). A brand's own face can take the cover title and the signs; bubbles and action words keep the rounded faces.
- **Narrated lines**: low and centred, about 3.7% of the frame's short side, cream with a thick dark-brown outline and a soft shadow; each word appears as it is spoken.
- **Speech bubbles** live in the paper world: white card, ink outline, a tail that follows the speaker on every frame; they pop in with overshoot and type on with blips.
- **Titles are paper objects**: a cardboard sign dropping on strings and swinging; the cover itself is the film title. Action words (italic display, gradient, double stroke, rays) sparingly, like a game rating.

## 5. Motion quality

- Every frame is new, and everything is computed from the frame number. Paper moves stiffly; life comes from easing: a back-ease overshoot for pop-ups, springs for things on strings, squash and stretch on characters only.
- **Pop-ups rise back to front** (the delay grows with distance from the reader), far pieces lying back, near ones folding forward face-down; they fold down in the same order.
- Plants and light props sway a degree or two around their base.
- **Characters are flat cards redrawn per pose** (walk phase, arms, eyes, mouth, look, blink); the walk phase follows the distance walked, so feet don't slide.
- **Turning around = a mirror flip** between a front drawing and a back drawing; never a turn through the edge. Jumps: parabolic arcs, squash at both ends, a paper puff on landing.
- **Page turn**: the leaf is a strip of many narrow segments, each bending a little more than the last, so it curls like paper.
- Never moves: the room.

## 6. Camera grammar

One virtual macro camera keyed as position, tilt, zoom and focus depth, eased between keys; a new list of keys is a hard cut. A vocabulary, not a route. Opening and ending come from the topic.

| Move | What it expresses | Can serve |
|---|---|---|
| Eye-level macro track, shallow focus | being inside the story at the actors' size | a walk; a conversation; a race |
| Pull up to a 3/4 view of the spread | the mechanism, the map | a page turn; a new setting; a plan |
| Descending dolly from human height | entering the book's scale | an arrival; zooming into one part of a system |
| Push-in on a face | feeling | a wish; a worry; a decision |
| Reverse shot from inside the book to the blurred room | the world beyond the page is huge | curiosity; a reader watching |
| Rack focus paper ↔ real object | paper and reality touch | cause and effect; a real product in a paper story |
| Top-down over the open spread | a board game, a diagram | a route; two sides of the gutter compared |
| Locked frame on a pull-tab | the trick is the event | before/after; a count |
| Rising crane leaving the book small | scale, distance | aftermath; a summary; time passing |

Actors in focus, room objects as soft foreground or background; keep near pop-ups out of the lens path. Transitions: page turns, a book closing and reopening, a hard cut on a paper sound; never a dissolve inside the book.

## 7. Sound palette

- **Paper foley is the signature**: page swish, cardboard thump, pop-up snap (a short rising sweep plus a crinkle), fold and crease, pull-tab slide, footsteps as tiny taps with crinkle, slat flaps, string-drop tinks; the book's spine creak and closing thump.
- **Every pop-up gets its snap on the frame it stands up**, lighter for small far pieces and heavier for the big near ones; swaying plants and the camera stay silent.
- **Character voices are typing blips**: one waveform and pitch range per character, a blip every other letter. They are chatter, never a tune.
- **Ambience**: room tone and a clock for the room; one quiet bed per spread for the paper world (meadow air, a market's murmur, desert wind).
- **Silence** is the book held still with only room tone: before a turn that matters, or a hesitation.
- **Sound across a cut**: the page swish starts before the leaf lifts and its thump lands on the new spread; a pull-tab's clicks can set the pulse that the next pop-ups rise on.
- **Voice**: one warm storyteller, picture-book sentences.

## 8. Native moves

A menu: use the ones your story needs.

- **The book opens**: creak, the board swings up, the world pops up back to front. *Fits content like:* a project launch; a first school day; a recipe book.
- **Page turn = scene change**: old pieces fold down as new ones spring up. *Fits content like:* stages of a process; a farm's seasons; chapters of a biography.
- **Pull-tab mechanisms**: flipping slats, a sun on a stick, sliding strips, a hinged door, pieces on strings. *Fits content like:* a forecast; old way/new way; a lock opening.
- **Paper as character**: crumpled, smoothed, refolded, torn, taped. *Fits content like:* a mistake becoming a lesson; recycling; a rejected draft becoming the design.
- **The edge of the page**: crossing between paper and the room, either way. *Fits content like:* a character stepping into a phone on the desk; a real object entering the story; a drawing coming true.
- **The real object plays a part**: a lamp as a sun, a mug as a tower. *Fits content like:* a product at home; the reader's presence; real clock time.
- **Typed bubbles and ratings**. *Fits content like:* a tutorial; a quiz; a small victory.

## 9. Pitfalls of the medium

- Turning a card about its upright axis shows it edge-on, then its blank back → mirror-flip between two drawings. A flip also mirrors any lettering on a prop → draw a mirrored version instead.
- A container made of one card hides its contents → a back card and a front card, contents between.
- Near-lens foreground pop-ups fill the frame with blur → move them, or blur them less.
- Rectangle shadows kill the illusion → every card's shadow follows its cut-out shape (`box-shadow` follows the box; it is the wrong tool).
- A turning leaf shows the art mirrored on its back → give it two faces, art in front and plain paper behind.
- A grade that shifts hues turns paper colours muddy → keep the paper's colours as designed and warm only the room.
- A `filter` or `overflow: hidden` on a 3D container flattens everything inside it → blur and clip the individual cards.
- Rebuilding every character drawing on every frame is slow → keep one drawing per pose and switch between them.

## 10. Building it here

- **No 3D renderer: the book is CSS 3D.** A stage `div` with `perspective` and `transform-style: preserve-3d` holds the ground page (laid flat with `rotateX`), the backdrop board (hinged on the spine) and one `div` per card. A card pops up by rotating about its bottom edge (`transform-origin: bottom`) from flat to upright with `prog(frame, start, dur, ease.back)`; things on strings use `springAt(…, SPRING.bouncy)`.
- **The camera** is a `transform` on the stage (`translate3d`, a few degrees of `rotateX`/`rotateY`, `scale`), eased between keys with `ease.inOut`. Keep it within about 25° of the designed view; a top-down shot or a reverse shot is its own flat layout, not the same stage seen from far round.
- **Cut-paper finish** is one SVG filter on each card: `feMorphology` (dilate) on the alpha for the off-white border, a second dilate with `feOffset` for the grey edge, `feTurbulence` (fixed `seed`) for the grain.
- **Shadows**: a darkened, blurred copy of the card laid on the ground plane from the card's base, so it shortens as the card stands up; `filter: drop-shadow(…)` for contact shadows.
- **Lens**: `filter: blur(…)` on each card by its distance from the focus depth, a blurred photo or painted plate for the room, a warm `radial-gradient` pool of lamp light, then `Finish` for a light vignette and grain.
- **Page turn**: nested segments, each with `transform-origin` on its inner edge and a small `rotateY`, two faces with `backface-visibility: hidden`.
- **Left out**: real reflections, light that changes with the camera, a room you can turn around in. The desk and room are a plate: a photo the user supplies, or a painted surface.
- **Pop-up order and page turns live in `timeline.ts`** (each card's start is its spread's start plus a delay by depth); `Soundtrack.tsx` lands every snap, swish and blip on the same frames. Use `<SceneTrack sweeps={false} />`: the page turn is the transition. Scenes take no `SceneFrame` entry or exit: its blurred push is the engine's default look, not this one. The timeline's `overlap` is how long the old scene stays mounted under the new one (8 frames by default): build it with `{ overlap: 0 }` for a hard cut, or give it the length of this style's own transition.

## 11. In a product video

- **The brand's colour is the cover cloth and the hero spread's hue family**, and the accent that recurs across spreads. A neon brand colour is toned to children's-book strength on paper and kept pure in the logo.
- **The real product is the real object on the desk**: a product photo, or a `Device` lying beside the book with a screen recording playing. Rack focus from paper to it; a paper character can step off the page and the real screen answers.
- **Screenshots become pop-up cards**: the real screen printed as a card with the off-white border and cardboard edge, rising with the rest of the spread. Don't redraw the interface as a cartoon.
- **Numbers live on signs and pull-tabs**: a counter on sliding slats, a price on a hanging sign. Each is in `facts.md`; sample data on a card carries `<ExampleBadge />`.
- **End card**: the book closes on its cover with the product's name in foil, or everything folds but one card: the logo, and one line on a hanging sign saying where to get it.
- **Arabic**: the book opens from the other side: spine on the right, pages turning the other way, pieces still rising back to front. Bubbles fill word by word with one blip per word, never letter by letter, and no letter-spacing. Use a rounded Arabic face for bubbles and a chunky display one for signs (the kind of Baloo Bhaijaan and Lalezar).
- **9:16**: the backdrop board fills the upper half and the ground page the lower; three or four cards per spread, actors in the middle band. Narrated lines sit above the bottom 22%, bubbles keep out of the right 12%, signs hang below the top 12%.

## 12. Variation space

You decide the book, the room, the characters (or none), the spreads and mechanisms, the opening, the ending, the camera path and each spread's palette.

- Structures: **the almanac** (one spread per month or stage, each pull-tab left running so the book fills with motion); **two books facing each other**, characters trading objects across the gap; **one spread pulled again and again**, each pull a step of how something is made.
- Openings: **mid-turn** (a page already curling, we land in chapter three); **inside the gutter** (low along the fold as the first piece rises over the lens); **a mechanism alone** (a tab slides by itself before any character appears).
- Endings: **the book closes** (pieces fold in reverse, a puff of air, stillness); **one piece stays up** (everything folds but the card that is the point); **the shelf** (the camera finds this book among many).
